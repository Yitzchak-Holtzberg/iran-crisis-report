Apr 5 — U.S. Rescue Mission Inside Iran; Major CSAR Aircraft Losses

A U.S. combat search-and-rescue (CSAR) operation deep inside Iran successfully recovered both crew members of the downed F-15E Strike Eagle. The mission, described as one of the most costly in special operations history, involved dozens of aircraft including Navy SEAL Team 6 and CIA assets, with at least two HC-130J transport aircraft deliberately destroyed and multiple MH-6 helicopters and MQ-9 drones lost to prevent capture; estimated mission cost exceeds $2 billion. Iran's military claimed several "enemy aircraft" were destroyed in Isfahan province during the operation, demonstrating the effectiveness of surviving Iranian air defenses in contested airspace. The incident raises significant questions about U.S. air superiority assumptions and operational risk in the remaining campaign phase. — Sources: NYT, Apr 5; BBC, Apr 5; Al Jazeera, Apr 5; Defence Security Asia, Apr 5

Mar 22 — Iran missile strikes on southern Israel (Dimona, Arad); Natanz reports under investigation

Iran launched missile strikes that struck towns near Israel's Dimona nuclear research center and Arad, injuring over 100 people according to emergency services. Israeli strikes over Iran and reports of an incident at the Natanz enrichment site have prompted IAEA inquiries. These developments raise risks to Iran's strategic infrastructure and complicate assumptions about command-and-control and escalation management. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 22; BBC, Mar 22

Mar 21 — Israeli strikes reported on Tehran; Natanz reportedly hit; regional diplomatic fallout

These developments heighten the risk to Iran's remaining strategic infrastructure and complicate command-and-control assumptions. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 21; NBC News, Mar 21 (Natanz report)

From Vacuum to Transition: Mojtaba Under Fire

Iran’s naval forces are effectively neutralized, but missile, drone, cyber, and proxy threats persist. The key uncertainty: can Mojtaba consolidate centralized release authority over surviving arsenals — or will pre-delegated IRGC launch protocols and proxy autonomy drive uncoordinated escalation?

Military Risks

  • 1,500–2,500 BMs/CMs remain; ~100–200 TELs; pre-delegated IRGC launch authority may persist
  • Proxy autonomy: Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi PMF can escalate without centralized direction
  • Cyber fully intact: APT33/APT34 kinetic-proof; pre-positioned in Gulf energy/finance networks

Succession: Control of the Arsenal

Iran's Retaliation — Who Was Attacked & How (Feb 28–Mar 1)

Within hours of the Operation Epic Fury strikes, Iran activated its pre-planned asymmetric retaliation doctrine — designating its response "True Promise 4." The response was simultaneous and multi-front — triggering the largest simultaneous air defense activation in regional history across five theaters. Per Critical Threats/ISW, the initial barrages targeting Israel involved fewer than 100 ballistic missiles; broader combined attacks (missiles and drones) across the region totaled "hundreds" of projectiles. — Critical Threats/ISW, Feb 28, 2026; Stars & Stripes, Mar 1, 2026

Summary — Iran's First Retaliatory Salvo (Feb 28–Mar 1)

Iran launched its retaliatory first wave — IRGC-designated "True Promise 4" — on Feb 28. Per Critical Threats/ISW analysis, the initial barrages targeting Israel involved fewer than 100 ballistic missiles; broader combined regional attacks (ballistic missiles and drones across Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, and Jordan) totaled "hundreds" of projectiles — the largest combined Iranian missile-and-drone barrage ever recorded. Explosions were reported in Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. Air defense systems — Patriot PAC-3, THAAD, Arrow 3, Iron Dome, David's Sling, and Aegis BMD — were activated simultaneously across all theaters. No US casualties or significant damage to major US installations were confirmed from the initial waves. As of July 17, 2026, the US-Israeli strikes continue while Iran has not launched a confirmed second ballistic-missile wave — though IRGC doctrine from the 2024 Israel barrages called for a follow-on salvo 24–48 hours after an initial barrage to target repair crews and overwhelm reconstituted air defenses. — Critical Threats/ISW Evening Special Report, Feb 28, 2026; ISW Understanding War; Stars & Stripes, Mar 1, 2026; Army Recognition; Special Eurasia; Wikipedia 2026 Iran conflict

What Remains Unknown — & The Second-Wave Risk

Precise interception rates, confirmed ground damage at targeted bases, US/Israeli/Gulf casualty figures, and whether Houthi and Iraqi PMF attacks were launched simultaneously are all not yet confirmed as of July 17, 2026. The critical unknown is whether Iran has already initiated — or is planning — a second ballistic-missile wave. IRGC doctrine from the 2024 Israel barrages called for a follow-on salvo 24–48 hours after the first, specifically targeting repair crews and taking advantage of partially-depleted interceptor magazines. With Khamenei now dead and the IRGC command structure fragmented, authorization for a second wave may rest with corps-level commanders operating under pre-delegated authority — making it simultaneously harder to predict and harder to deter through conventional diplomatic signaling. The additional strategic wildcard: Iran has been withholding its Fattah-1 hypersonic-maneuvering missiles in reserve — if a successor authority deploys them in a second wave, current air defense systems face their most difficult intercept challenge of the conflict. — Chosun, Mar 5, 2026; ISW; Critical Threats
