Iran: Strike Damage Assessment

Operation Epic Fury — confirmed and assessed damage to Iranian targets (Feb 28–Mar 29, 2026)
Critical
Mar 29 — IAEA team arrives in Tehran; Natanz cascade halls confirmed severely damaged

An IAEA inspection team arrived in Tehran on Mar 29 for the first on-site visits since Operation Epic Fury began. The IAEA’s Mar 25 technical assessment confirmed that Natanz centrifuge cascade halls A and B are “severely damaged and unlikely to be operational for 6–18 months.” No radiation release has been detected at any monitoring station. The team is expected to visit Isfahan’s uranium conversion complex and the Natanz surface facilities during the visit. — Source: IAEA; Reuters; Mar 25–29

Mar 28 — Pause extended; IRGC drone kills 4 US troops at Al-Udeid perimeter

Trump extended the five-day energy-infrastructure pause by 72 hours, citing “extraordinary progress” in Oman talks. However, an IRGC-linked militia drone struck the perimeter of Al-Udeid Air Base, killing four US service members — the deadliest single attack since mid-March. CENTCOM struck the responsible militia cell in eastern Syria in retaliation. — Source: AP; Pentagon; Axios, Mar 28

Mar 25 — Iran executes four protest-movement members including two women; UN condemns killings

Iran’s judiciary executed four individuals convicted of “collaboration with foreign intelligence” in connection with protest activities, including two women. The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights issued an emergency statement condemning a “systematic wave of judicial killings designed to terrorize the civilian population.” Human rights groups say more than 40 people have been executed since Feb 28 on protest-related charges. — Source: UN OHCHR; BBC, Mar 25

Mar 24 — IDF strikes IRGC logistics depots in western Iran near Kermanshah; 15+ killed

The IDF conducted precision strikes on IRGC logistics depots and fuel storage near Kermanshah, reporting “15+ IRGC operatives killed.” Iranian state media acknowledged strikes but disputed casualty numbers. The strikes targeted supply routes supporting remaining IRGC ballistic missile units. — Source: Jerusalem Post; Ynet, Mar 24

Mar 22 — Wide-scale strikes across Iran; Natanz hit; IAEA investigating

The Mar 22 wave of strikes targeted missile production and defence-industrial sites across Iran. Regional reporting described damage at the Natanz enrichment complex; the IAEA said it detected no rise in radiation levels. Iranian ballistic missiles struck towns near Dimona and Arad, causing 160+ injuries in southern Israel. — Source: IAEA; Reuters; Al Jazeera; NYT, Mar 22

Mar 15 — UN refugee agency: 3.8 million displaced inside Iran; humanitarian needs surge

UN humanitarian agencies report the internally displaced population inside Iran has grown to approximately 3.8 million, with the sharpest increases in Khuzestan, Tehran, and Isfahan provinces. Food security is “increasingly critical” due to logistics disruption and fuel shortages. — Source: UN OCHA (citing UNHCR), Mar 15–29

Gulf States: Iranian Retaliation Damage

Iran's ballistic missile and drone attacks on US-hosting Gulf nations — Feb 28–Mar 12, 2026
Active
Mar 28 — IRGC-linked drone kills four US troops at Al-Udeid perimeter; Qatar condemns attack

An IRGC-affiliated militia drone struck the outer perimeter of Al-Udeid Air Base in Qatar, killing four US service members and wounding seven others. Qatar formally condemned the attack and cooperated with a US CENTCOM retaliatory strike on the responsible cell in eastern Syria. — AP; Pentagon, Mar 28

Mar 27 — First US Navy-escorted convoy of 12 tankers transits Hormuz without incident

The first convoy of 12 commercial tankers, escorted by US Navy vessels, successfully transited the Strait of Hormuz on Mar 27. Iran’s Foreign Ministry stated Iran would “not fire on neutral-flagged commercial vessels operating under international law.” Ship-tracking data showed 40+ vessels queuing for subsequent convoys. — Bloomberg; Reuters, Mar 27

Iran's Retaliation — 500+ BMs, ~2,000 Drones Since Feb 28; Now Sharply Reduced

Iran has fired over 500 ballistic/naval missiles and ~2,000 drones since Feb 28, with ~40% aimed at Israel and ~60% at US regional targets. CENTCOM says Iran’s missile launches are down 90% and drones down 83% from initial rates as IRGC launch capacity is degraded. New attacks continue but at much reduced tempo. — Sources: Compiled reporting (Tier‑2/3/4 mix), Mar 10–29

Mar 21 — Saudi Arabia expels Iranian military attaché and staff

Saudi Arabia declared Iran’s military attaché, his assistant, and three embassy staff persona non grata and ordered them to leave within 24 hours, citing recent Iranian attacks on Saudi territory. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 21

Shipping & Insurance Disruption (Mar 15–29)

The first US Navy-escorted convoy of 12 tankers successfully transited the Strait of Hormuz on Mar 27, signaling the beginning of a cautious reopening. US Navy mine-countermeasures vessels detonated two influence mines on Mar 25–26. War-risk insurance premiums remain at record highs but have eased ~15% since the convoy announcement. Shipping operators are cautiously preparing vessel queues for additional convoys; analysts estimate full commercial traffic could resume within 3–6 weeks if talks progress. — Sources: Bloomberg, Mar 27; USNI News, Mar 26; Reuters, Mar 28

Update (Mar 27)

Brent crude fell ~12% from its post-war peak as the convoy transit and extended energy-strike pause raised hopes for a partial Hormuz reopening. Ship-tracking data shows 40+ tankers queuing to join subsequent convoys; ADNOC Gas said it is “evaluating resumption of normal LNG export schedules.” — Bloomberg; Reuters; ADNOC, Mar 27–28

Ongoing: Transit paralysis and stranded tankers slowly easing

At peak (Mar 21), over 480 tankers were stranded in the Persian Gulf and more than 300 idling in the Gulf of Oman. Following the first convoy and mine-clearing operations, the number of actively stranded vessels has declined to approximately 320. Insurers are cautiously revising Gulf war-risk terms but have not yet fully restored cover. — NYT; Reuters; S&P Global, Mar 21–29

Global Diplomatic Response

International reactions to Operation Epic Fury — Mar 1–29, 2026
Elevated
Mar 29 — IAEA team in Tehran; HEU framework under review; talks at critical phase

An IAEA inspection team arrived in Tehran for the first on-site nuclear-site visits since Feb 28. US and Iranian negotiators in Oman are reviewing a preliminary HEU disposal framework covering a 440 kg 60% HEU transfer to a third country and a six-month enrichment freeze. Iranian FM Araghchi and the White House describe talks as “in their most productive phase.” — IAEA; Reuters; WSJ, Mar 29

Mar 28 — Trump extends energy-strike pause 72 hours; Al-Udeid drone attack kills 4 US troops

President Trump extended the five-day pause on energy-infrastructure strikes for 72 additional hours citing “extraordinary progress” in Oman talks. The same day, an IRGC-linked drone killed four US troops at the Al-Udeid perimeter — the deadliest single incident since mid-March. CENTCOM struck the responsible cell in retaliation. — Axios; AP, Mar 28

Mar 28 — Russia and China veto ceasefire resolution at UNSC; US and UK block text

Russia and China introduced a UNSC draft resolution calling for an immediate and permanent ceasefire and withdrawal of all foreign forces. The US and UK vetoed the resolution. Russia and China issued a joint statement condemning “the illegal aggression against a sovereign state.” — UN; Reuters, Mar 28

Mar 27 — First Hormuz convoy transits; oil falls 12%; Turkey proposes co-mediation

The first US Navy-escorted convoy of 12 tankers successfully transited Hormuz. Brent crude fell ~12% from its post-war peak. Turkish President Erdogan proposed co-mediating alongside Oman, speaking with both Trump and Araghchi. EU foreign policy chief visited Oman and proposed a €20 billion post-deal reconstruction fund. — Bloomberg; Reuters; Politico EU, Mar 27

Mar 23 — Iran strikes injure 160+ in southern Israel; Trump announces five-day energy pause

Iranian missiles struck near Dimona and Arad, injuring 160+. Trump announced a five-day pause on strikes targeting Iranian energy infrastructure while US-Iran talks via Oman proceeded. — BBC; Politico, Mar 23

Mar 10 — US Senate Democrats demand public hearings on Iran war

Democratic senators called for immediate public hearings, citing concerns over strategy, civilian harm, and troop protection. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9–10

Energy & Shipping Markets — Updated Mar 20

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely disrupted into week three. Kharg Island strikes and ongoing maritime attacks have kept Brent crude above $100/barrel at times, though prices swung after reports the U.S. may release sanctioned Iranian crude. Governments are discussing naval escorts and insurance backstops; the IEA is reportedly considering further emergency reserve releases as markets remain volatile. On Mar 20, the U.S. Treasury announced a temporary 30‑day waiver (Mar 20–Apr 19) allowing the sale of roughly ~140 million barrels of Iranian oil already at sea to relieve immediate market pressure; refiners in Asia are examining purchases. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2); BBC (Tier‑2); CNBC (Tier‑2); Bloomberg (Tier‑2); CNN, Mar 19–21

Regional Mediation Efforts — Turkey, Oman, Egypt

Turkey, Oman, and Egypt are pressing for a diplomatic end to the fighting via quiet back-channel talks with Tehran and regional capitals. Mediation is active but constrained by preconditions on both sides; no public ceasefire agreement has been reached. Update (Mar 23): President Trump announced a five‑day pause on strikes against Iranian energy infrastructure while diplomatic conversations continue and characterized talks as "very good and productive." — Sources: Haaretz (Tier‑4), Mar 15; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14; Politico, Mar 23
