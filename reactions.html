Iran: Strike Damage Assessment

Operation Epic Fury — confirmed and assessed damage to Iranian targets (Feb 28–Mar 22, 2026)
Critical
Mar 22 — Wide‑scale strikes reported across Iran; Natanz hit; IAEA investigating

The latest wave of strikes on Mar 22 targeted missile production and defence‑industrial sites across Iran. Regional reporting and Iranian state outlets described damage at the Natanz enrichment complex; the IAEA said it is investigating and reported no detected rise in radiation levels outside the facility. Iranian ballistic missiles struck towns near Dimona and Arad, causing dozens of injuries in southern Israel. CENTCOM and allied statements describe renewed, intense targeting of regime military infrastructure. — Source: IAEA; Reuters; Al Jazeera; New York Times, Mar 22

Mar 15 — UN refugee agency: Millions displaced inside Iran; humanitarian needs surge

The UN refugee agency and humanitarian observers report a major internal displacement crisis in Iran, with estimates of roughly 3.2 million people displaced inside the country since the start of large-scale strikes. Aid organisations warn of urgent needs for shelter, medical care, and safe corridors. — Source: Los Angeles Times (Tier‑4, citing UNHCR), Mar 15

Mar 15 — Diplomatic push to halt fighting stalled; U.S. rebuffs allied ceasefire initiatives

Multiple reports indicate allied efforts to open immediate ceasefire talks have been rebuffed by the U.S. administration, while Iran insists all strikes must end before negotiations. Regional mediators (Turkey, Oman, Egypt) continue quiet diplomacy but say a breakthrough is not yet in sight. — Sources: Haaretz (Tier‑4), Mar 15; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14

Mar 14 — US Strikes Military Sites on Kharg Island; Oil Infrastructure Spared for Now

The U.S. military struck military targets on Kharg Island, a strategic node for Iran's oil export network; officials said oil export terminals were deliberately avoided. Iranian outlets disputed the extent of damage. Global supply-risk concerns intensified. — Sources: AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 14

Mar 12 — Fresh Iranian Attacks on Shipping; Oil Above $100 / School Strike Under Investigation

Iran launched new missile and drone attacks on Gulf shipping and terminals (Mar 11–12), with tankers struck in Iraqi waters and impacts near Dubai and Bahrain. Oil climbed above $100/barrel. Separately, the U.S. military opened an investigation into a strike that hit an elementary school in Iran, reportedly killing dozens of children; Congress is pressing for details. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 12; Bloomberg (Tier‑2), Mar 12; NBC (Tier‑4), Mar 12; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 12

Mar 10 — Israel Announces New Wide-Scale Waves Across Iran

Israel said it carried out several wide-scale waves of strikes across Iran overnight, focusing on IRGC long-range missile launch sites and regime infrastructure. Crowds rallied in Tehran in support of the new Supreme Leader as strikes continued. — Sources: BBC (Tier‑4), Mar 10; CNN live (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Gulf States: Iranian Retaliation Damage

Iran's ballistic missile and drone attacks on US-hosting Gulf nations — Feb 28–Mar 12, 2026
Active
Iran's Retaliation — 500+ BMs, ~2,000 Drones Since Feb 28

Iran has fired over 500 ballistic/naval missiles and ~2,000 drones since Feb 28, with ~40% aimed at Israel and ~60% at US regional targets. Half of Iran's missiles at Israel carried cluster bomb warheads (IDF assessment). Gen. Caine says Iran's missile launches are down 90% and drones down 83% from initial rates. — Sources: Compiled reporting (Tier‑2/3/4 mix), Mar 10–12

Mar 21 — Saudi Arabia expels Iranian military attache and staff

Saudi Arabia declared Iran's military attache, his assistant, and three embassy staff persona non grata and ordered them to leave within 24 hours, citing recent Iranian attacks on Saudi territory. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 21

Military Targets Hit

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — US 5th Fleet: Naval Support Activity Bahrain struck by Iranian missiles (Feb 28). Bahrain condemned the strikes as "a flagrant attack on sovereignty." — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base: Largest US air base in the Middle East and CENTCOM Forward HQ targeted. Patriot systems intercepted. US has not confirmed base damage. — Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 1
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Abu Dhabi: Missile struck near a US-linked site; 1 killed, others injured. Patriot and THAAD batteries activated; multiple intercepts confirmed. UAE intercepted 9 BMs and 35 drones on Mar 10. — US News (Tier‑4), Feb 28
  • 🇰🇼 Kuwait — 12+ Killed: Drone attack on Port Shuaiba killed 6 US soldiers (Mar 5). On Mar 10, fresh barrages killed 12 more including 6 US service members and 2 Kuwaiti soldiers. Ali Al Salem Air Base on FPCON Delta. — NBC/Euronews, Mar 10; Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 5–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia: Impacts on Saudi soil including a major refinery (Mar 2) and drone hits on the US Embassy compound in Riyadh (Mar 3). Ras Tanura refinery/export terminal closed. US ordered non-emergency staff to depart. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 3 & 7; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Civilian & Economic Impacts

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — Iranian drone struck a water desalination plant (Mar 8), targeting civilian infrastructure. Bapco Energies declared force majeure on some products. — AP (Tier‑2), Mar 8; Axios (Tier‑4), Mar 9
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — declared force majeure on LNG exports after drone attacks disrupted output and logistics. Doha offered mediation; Iranian ambassador summoned. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 7–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia — GCC emergency summit convened in Riyadh; humanitarian and air-defense cooperation expanded with CENTCOM. Airport closures reported across the region.
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Emergency National Security Council session; temporary airport closures. Formal protest to Iran filed.

Shipping & Insurance Disruption (Mar 11–15)

Repeated attacks on tankers and Gulf terminals have forced operators to suspend or reroute voyages and prompted a surge in war-risk premiums. The Mar 14 Kharg Island strikes and continuing Iranian maritime activity have intensified calls for naval escorts and insurance backstops. Shipping and LNG flows remain highly constrained. — Sources: Bloomberg (Tier‑2), Mar 12; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 12; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14

Update (Mar 20)

Iraq declared force majeure on foreign‑operated oilfields after exports near‑halt linked to Strait of Hormuz disruptions; shipping data showed no crude tankers transited in the prior 24 hours, though two Indian‑flagged LPG tankers prepared to sail amid a narrow window of resumed transits. These developments sharpened calls for naval escorts and insurance interventions. — Reuters; MarineLink; Insurance Journal, Mar 20

Mar 21 — Transit paralysis and stranded tankers

Reporting indicates severe shipping paralysis: analyses cite over 480 tankers stranded in the Persian Gulf and more than 300 idling in the Gulf of Oman, with daily transits falling from roughly 138 ships to a handful. Insurers pulled Gulf war‑risk cover and surcharges spiked, exacerbating supply and cost pressures. — The New York Times; Reuters; CNN, Mar 20–21

Energy & Shipping Markets — Updated Mar 20

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely disrupted into week three. Kharg Island strikes and ongoing maritime attacks have kept Brent crude above $100/barrel at times, though prices swung after reports the U.S. may release sanctioned Iranian crude. Governments are discussing naval escorts and insurance backstops; the IEA is reportedly considering further emergency reserve releases as markets remain volatile. On Mar 20, the U.S. Treasury announced a temporary 30‑day waiver (Mar 20–Apr 19) allowing the sale of roughly ~140 million barrels of Iranian oil already at sea to relieve immediate market pressure; refiners in Asia are examining purchases. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2); BBC (Tier‑2); CNBC (Tier‑2); Bloomberg (Tier‑2); CNN, Mar 19–21

Regional Mediation Efforts — Turkey, Oman, Egypt

Turkey, Oman, and Egypt are pressing for a diplomatic end to the fighting via quiet back-channel talks with Tehran and regional capitals. Mediation is active but constrained by preconditions on both sides; no public ceasefire agreement has been reached. Update (Mar 23): President Trump announced a five‑day pause on strikes against Iranian energy infrastructure while diplomatic conversations continue and characterized talks as "very good and productive." — Sources: Haaretz (Tier‑4), Mar 15; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14; Politico, Mar 23
