Background & History

How seven decades of confrontation led to Operation Epic Fury
Why This Page Exists

The Iran crisis didn't start on February 28, 2026. It is the product of seven decades of coups, revolutions, hostage crises, proxy wars, nuclear brinkmanship, and broken promises — on both sides. This page provides the context needed to understand how we got here.

🇺🇸 US-Iran Relations: 1953–2025

The US-Iran relationship is defined by a cycle of intervention, revolution, and mutual distrust stretching back to the Cold War.

1953 — Operation Ajax
The CIA and MI6 orchestrate a coup overthrowing democratically elected Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh, who had nationalized Iran's oil. The Shah is restored to absolute power. For Iranians, this remains the original sin of US interference.
1957–1979 — The Shah's Iran
The US backs the Shah's modernization program (the "White Revolution") while his secret police, SAVAK, crush dissent. Iran becomes the US's chief Middle East ally and a major arms buyer. Growing inequality and repression fuel opposition from Islamists, leftists, and nationalists.
FEB 1979 — Islamic Revolution
Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini returns from exile as the Shah flees. The Islamic Republic is declared. Iran's foreign policy pivots to "Neither East nor West" — and the US becomes the "Great Satan."
NOV 1979 — Hostage Crisis
Iranian students seize the US Embassy in Tehran, holding 52 Americans hostage for 444 days. The crisis destroys Jimmy Carter's presidency and poisons US-Iran relations for a generation. Diplomatic ties are severed and have never been restored.
1980–1988 — Iran-Iraq War
Saddam Hussein invades Iran. The US backs Iraq with intelligence and weapons while Iran's forces fight with human-wave tactics. The war kills an estimated 500,000–1 million people. In 1988, the USS Vincennes shoots down Iran Air Flight 655, killing all 290 aboard — the US calls it an accident; Iran calls it murder.
1990s–2000s — Sanctions and Proxy Wars
The US imposes escalating sanctions. Iran builds a network of proxy forces — Hezbollah, Hamas, Iraqi militias, the Houthis — creating an "Axis of Resistance" to project power across the region without direct confrontation. The 1996 Khobar Towers bombing kills 19 US airmen; Iran is later implicated.
2002 — "Axis of Evil"
President George W. Bush names Iran alongside Iraq and North Korea in his State of the Union address. The label hardens positions in Tehran and empowers hardliners who argue the US will never accept the Islamic Republic.
2009 — The Green Movement
Millions take to the streets after the disputed re-election of Mahmoud Ahmadinejad. Protesters chant "Where is my vote?" The regime crushes the movement. The Obama administration's muted response is later criticized from both sides — too little support for protesters, or inappropriate interference, depending on whom you ask.
2020 — Soleimani Assassination
A US drone strike kills IRGC Quds Force commander Qasem Soleimani at Baghdad airport. Iran retaliates with ballistic missiles at a US base in Iraq, injuring over 100 troops. The two countries come closer to open war than at any point since 1988.

The Trust Deficit

Neither side has a partner it can trust. Iranians remember the 1953 coup and Flight 655. Americans remember the hostage crisis and decades of proxy attacks. Every negotiation starts from a baseline of mutual suspicion — which is why diplomacy keeps failing.

Iran's Nuclear Program

Iran's nuclear ambitions have been the central driver of international confrontation for over two decades.

2002 — The Reveal
The MEK (an Iranian dissident group) reveals the existence of secret enrichment facilities at Natanz and a heavy-water reactor at Arak. The IAEA launches inspections and finds Iran has been concealing nuclear work for 18 years.
2006–2012 — Sanctions and Sabotage
The UN Security Council passes six rounds of sanctions. Israel and the US launch covert operations: the Stuxnet computer worm destroys roughly 1,000 centrifuges at Natanz (2010). Several Iranian nuclear scientists are assassinated in Tehran street attacks.
2015 — The JCPOA
After years of negotiations, Iran and the P5+1 (US, UK, France, Germany, Russia, China) sign the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action. Iran limits enrichment to 3.67%, removes two-thirds of its centrifuges, and ships out 98% of its enriched uranium. In return, sanctions are lifted. Breakout time is extended to approximately one year.
MAY 2018 — Trump Withdraws
President Trump pulls the US out of the JCPOA, calling it "the worst deal ever negotiated." "Maximum pressure" sanctions are reimposed, devastating Iran's economy. Iran's moderates — who had staked their credibility on the deal — are humiliated. Hardliners say they were right all along.
2019–2024 — Creeping Toward the Threshold
Iran methodically breaches JCPOA limits. Enrichment rises from 3.67% to 60% (weapons-grade is ~90%). The uranium stockpile balloons. IAEA inspectors are progressively blocked. By 2024, Iran is a "threshold state" — capable of producing a bomb's worth of material in weeks.
2025–2026 — The Breaking Point
Iran amasses roughly 440 kg of near-weapons-grade uranium. US and Israeli intelligence conclude breakout time has collapsed to under two weeks. With diplomacy stalled and enrichment accelerating, the military option moves from "last resort" to "active planning."

The January Massacre & Protest Movement

Iran has a long history of mass protests — each crushed more brutally than the last. The January 2026 massacre was the bloodiest yet, and it changed the calculus for military intervention.

Waves of Uprising

Previous Uprisings

  • 2017–2018: Economic protests spread to 80+ cities. Dozens killed, thousands arrested.
  • 2019 "Bloody November": Fuel-price protests met with a nationwide internet blackout and mass shootings. Reuters reports 1,500+ killed in days.
  • 2022 Mahsa Amini: The "Woman, Life, Freedom" movement erupts after morality police kill a young Kurdish woman. Months of protests; 500+ killed, 22,000+ arrested.

January 2026

  • Jan 6: Reza Pahlavi publishes Washington Post op-ed calling the regime "finished."
  • Jan 8–9: Pahlavi-called protests draw millions. Regime responds with live ammunition on crowds.
  • Estimated toll: 7,000–36,500+ killed in what becomes known as the "January Massacre."
  • Complete internet blackout imposed. IRGC deployed in all major cities.
  • Mar 22: Multiple outlets reported that Iran hanged three men accused of killing security forces during the January protests; rights groups warn of a possible wave of executions aimed at deterring dissent. — Haaretz; Los Angeles Times; Mar 22
  • Jun 16: Reuters reported that Iran executed two individuals described as armed leaders in early‑2026 unrest, citing the judiciary's Mizan outlet; rights groups expressed alarm as Tehran continues to use capital punishment tied to the January protests. — Reuters; Jun 16

The Student Uprising (Feb 21 onward)

University students launch a coordinated protest wave starting February 21. Unlike previous movements, this one has explicit backing from exiled opposition figures and tacit support from elements within the regime itself. By late February, the protests have merged with the broader crisis — fuel shortages, hyperinflation, and the looming military confrontation create conditions the regime cannot control.

Why the Massacre Mattered

The scale of the January killings shocked the international community and gave interventionists — including voices in the Trump administration — a humanitarian justification for military action. Senator Lindsey Graham explicitly cited the massacres when calling for strikes. The regime's brutality inadvertently accelerated the very outcome it was trying to prevent.

The Path to Operation Epic Fury

By February 2026, three crises converged: Iran's nuclear breakout was imminent, the January Massacre had created a humanitarian rationale, and the regime was at its weakest in decades. Here is how the final countdown unfolded.

US/Coalition Buildup

  • Jan–Feb: USS Abraham Lincoln and USS Carl Vinson carrier strike groups surge to the Persian Gulf
  • Feb 5: Kushner convenes Iranian-American business leaders in Palm Beach — transition planning begins
  • Feb 13: Pahlavi offers "wide tent" amnesty to regime insiders willing to defect
  • Feb 14: Munich Security Conference — Pahlavi presents transition roadmap with Graham and EU support
  • Feb 20: Trump issues 10-day ultimatum demanding Iran halt enrichment and release political prisoners
  • Feb 25: State of the Union address — Trump signals readiness for "decisive action"

Iranian Provocations

  • Jan–Feb: IRGC accelerates enrichment and conducts Hormuz naval exercises
  • Feb 16: Pahlavi calls for "humanitarian intervention" — citing massacre scale
  • Feb 22–25: Student protesters chant Pahlavi's name — leaderless movement gains a figurehead
  • Feb 26: Geneva Round 3 opens; Pahlavi publishes open letter to IRGC generals
  • Feb 27: Geneva talks collapse; Iran rejects key terms
  • Feb 27: Iran threatens to close the Strait of Hormuz and strike US bases if attacked
FEB 28, 2026 — OPERATION EPIC FURY BEGINS
US and Israeli forces launch coordinated strikes across Iran — hitting nuclear facilities, missile sites, air defenses, IRGC command nodes, and naval assets. The largest US military operation since the 2003 invasion of Iraq has begun. Within hours, Khamenei's compound in Tehran is hit.
The War Was 73 Years in the Making

From the 1953 coup that created the conditions for the Islamic Revolution, to the hostage crisis that severed diplomatic ties, to the JCPOA collapse that restarted the nuclear clock — every failed attempt at resolution brought the two countries closer to this moment. Operation Epic Fury is not a bolt from the blue. It is the culmination of seven decades of mutual hostility, broken agreements, and missed opportunities for peace.
