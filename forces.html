Iran's Remaining Military Capability

Post-Operation Epic Fury: degraded, decapitated — but not disarmed
🔴 Operation "Epic Fury" — Day 2 (March 1, 2026)

U.S.-Israeli strikes entered a second consecutive day on March 1, targeting nuclear, ballistic-missile, and IRGC command-and-control infrastructure. Iran fired approximately ~170 ballistic missiles in its retaliatory first salvo on Feb 28 — toward Israel and US bases across Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. Iranian state media confirmed the death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei on March 1. Regional air defenses — Arrow 3, Iron Dome, Patriot, THAAD, and Aegis BMD — were activated simultaneously across all five theaters. Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz remain disrupted as oil trading houses suspended crude, fuel, and LNG transit. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera, Critical Threats, Mar 1

🆕 Admiral Cooper CENTCOM Video Briefing — Day 5 Operational Update (Mar 4, 2026)

CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper released a video operational assessment on Mar 4 — the most comprehensive public accounting of the operation to date. Key figures: nearly 2,000 Iranian targets struck since operations began; 17 Iranian naval vessels destroyed, including an Iranian submarine; 50,000+ U.S. troops participating in the operation; Iran's air defenses "severely degraded" with hundreds of ballistic missiles, launchers, and drones eliminated. Cooper stated: "Many of you may remember the Shock and Awe strikes of 2003. The first 24 hours of this operation were nearly double the scale." On one-way drones: "These drones were originally an Iranian design. We took them back to America, made them better and fired them right back at Iran." Cooper's overall assessment: "Our combat power is building. Iran's is declining. My overall operational assessment is that we are ahead of our game plan." — Source: Admiral Brad Cooper, CENTCOM video statement / ABC News, Mar 4, 2026

🔎 Post-Strike Battle Damage Assessment (BDA) — Updated Mar 4 (per Cooper briefing)

The following assessment reflects confirmed and reported strike effects through Day 5 (Mar 4). Figures updated per Admiral Cooper's CENTCOM video statement. BDA in active strike environments is inherently uncertain; the figures below represent the commander's public unclassified assessment.

Confirmed / Reported Struck

  • IRGC Command-and-Control Nodes: Multiple C2 facilities deliberately targeted in the first wave to degrade Iran's ability to coordinate reprisal strikes. Several senior IRGC generals reported killed. — PBS NewsHour, Feb 28
  • Nuclear Sites — Natanz & Isfahan: Strikes extended and deepened damage from June 2025; centrifuge infrastructure further degraded. Fordow underground bunker — effectiveness of penetration contested due to UK base-access denial.
  • Parchin Military Complex (Day 2): Targeted on March 1. A soil-covered concrete hardening structure visible in Feb 20 satellite imagery was specifically designed to blunt bunker-busting munitions. — The War Zone, Feb 20; Reuters, Mar 1
  • Ballistic Missile Infrastructure: Mobile TEL (transporter-erector-launcher) convoys and mountain tunnel complex access points targeted. A fraction of ~200 pre-positioned launchers are estimated destroyed; exact count unknown.
  • Air Defense Networks: S-300 radar nodes and Bavar-373 battery sites struck to suppress coverage for follow-on strike waves. Central Iranian air defense coverage assessed as severely degraded.
  • Tehran — IRGC HQ Complex: Multiple explosions reported across Tehran districts. Significant structural damage to IRGC administrative, intelligence, and command facilities. Civilian casualties reported in surrounding areas. — Al Jazeera, Reuters, Feb 28
  • Airfields: IRIAF (Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force) airfields reportedly struck; runways and hardened aircraft shelters targeted to ground Iran's limited air response capability.

Leadership Casualties — Confirmed / Reported

  • Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei — DEAD: Iranian state media confirmed his death March 1. His daughter, son-in-law, and a grandchild were also killed. No succession has been announced. — AP, WSJ, NBC citing Fars, Mar 1
  • IRGC Senior Commanders: Multiple generals and deputy commanders across corps branches reported killed. Total IRGC personnel KIA: 435+ as of March 4, 2026.
  • IRGC Quds Force Leadership: Key figures in Iran's proxy coordination network targeted. Individual commanders' status not fully confirmed; Quds Force operational capacity to direct Hezbollah, Houthis, and Iraqi PMF is degraded but not eliminated.
  • Air Force Command: IRIAF command structure disrupted — leadership casualty reports unconfirmed independently; operational sorties not observed.

Not Yet Confirmed Destroyed

  • Fordow Deep Bunker: Built under 80+ meters of granite mountain. US B-2 Spirits carrying GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrators were denied Diego Garcia basing by UK PM Starmer; their penetration effectiveness against Fordow is unconfirmed.
  • IRGC Navy / Hormuz Force — DESTROYED (Mar 3): Pentagon and CENTCOM announced on March 3 that Iran's naval forces have been effectively destroyed following sustained U.S. strikes on Iranian naval bases, port facilities, and vessels. Most surface combatants are sunk or disabled; the IRIN and IRGC Naval Forces no longer pose a coherent maritime threat. The formal Hormuz closure threat has been significantly degraded. — Pentagon / CENTCOM, Mar 3
  • Dispersed Mobile Missile Launchers: Estimated 150–180 TEL launchers remain after first-wave targeting; mountain tunnel dispersion makes comprehensive destruction extremely difficult.
  • Shahed Drone Stockpiles: Production facilities not confirmed destroyed; remaining Shahed-136/238 arsenal size is unknown.
  • "Pickaxe Mountain" Facility: Deep centrifuge facility near Isfahan built with Chinese tunneling expertise — hardened depth may have limited strike effects.

⚠️ The Command Vacuum: Iran Without Khamenei

Khamenei's death removes the singular authority that has controlled Iran's nuclear trigger, proxy activation orders, and all strategic military decisions for 35 years. This is not merely a symbolic loss — it creates a genuine and dangerous command vacuum over a still-armed state with ballistic missiles and drones. Iran's naval forces have been declared destroyed by CENTCOM (Mar 3), but the remaining missile and drone arsenal — and the proxy network — still represent serious threats.

Military Risks: Decapitation Without Disarmament

  • Iran retains an estimated 1,000–1,300 ballistic missiles after the ~170-missile first salvo — with no single confirmed authority now controlling their release
  • IRGC corps commanders may hold pre-delegated launch authority for second-wave ballistic salvos — a "dead hand" scenario where tactical commanders fire without political oversight
  • The Quds Force, absent central command, may activate terrorist sleeper cells globally under pre-standing contingency orders — no authorization needed if Khamenei is confirmed dead
  • Hezbollah, Houthis, and Iraqi PMF forces may escalate independently — the decapitation of the Quds Force removes the coordinating authority but not the proxy arsenals
  • Risk of competing IRGC factions forming separate power centers — creating unpredictable, uncoordinated decision-making loops with no diplomatic interlocutor
  • Iran's cyber units (APT33/APT34) operate autonomously and may launch pre-planned destructive attacks against Gulf financial systems and energy infrastructure without central authorization

Succession Under Fire: Who Controls Iran Now?

  • Assembly of Experts: Constitutionally empowered to elect a new Supreme Leader — but the body is fragmented, some members may be dead or unreachable, and it has never convened under active air strikes
  • Mojtaba Khamenei (son): Had been discreetly groomed as a potential successor but holds no formal military, clerical, or governmental authority — IRGC generals are unlikely to defer to him
  • IRGC General Command Council: The most powerful surviving military body — may assert de facto control over ballistic missiles and strategic assets regardless of formal succession processes
  • President Masoud Pezeshkian: Reformist president constitutionally subordinate to the Supreme Leader — elevated in interregnum, but commands no IRGC loyalty and has no control over the missile force
  • Expediency Council / Judiciary: Could attempt to convene an interim governance structure — but both lack the IRGC's weapons and the IRGC's willingness to follow civilian orders
  • The Hardliner-Reformist Fault Line: The succession fight between IRGC hardliners and regime reformists could fracture what remains of Iranian state cohesion at the worst possible moment
The Most Dangerous 72 Hours

The window between Khamenei's death and the emergence of any successor authority is assessed by defense analysts as the most dangerous phase of the conflict. Pre-delegated launch protocols may allow IRGC corps commanders to fire second-wave ballistic salvos without political authorization — potentially larger than the 170-missile first wave. US and Israeli military planners are almost certainly accelerating strikes against remaining launch infrastructure before any successor can consolidate control and either escalate further or negotiate a ceasefire. The absence of a recognized decision-maker on the Iranian side also complicates any US back-channel ceasefire outreach.

The cumulative effect of the June 2025 Israeli-US strikes and the Feb 28–Mar 1 Operation Epic Fury strikes has fundamentally altered Iran's military posture. Iran is no longer a pre-strike power deterring an attack — it is a post-strike state that has fired its first salvo and is now assessing what remains. The central military question is no longer whether Iran will retaliate, but what authority, and with what remaining arsenal, Iran can mount a second wave.

The China Factor

China has emerged as Iran's primary military supply chain. Despite sanctions, Chinese companies provided dual-use components for missile guidance, drone manufacturing, and radar systems throughout 2025–26. The "Pickaxe Mountain" deep centrifuge facility near Isfahan was reportedly built with Chinese tunneling expertise. Beijing's calculus: a weakened-but-surviving Iran keeps US military resources tied down in the Middle East — away from the Taiwan Strait. China's Feb 24 agreement to supply additional anti-ship missiles was confirmed days before the Feb 28 strikes. Whether China continues supply into an active wartime Iran remains a critical open question — and a major source of US-China tension.

💥 Iran's Retaliation — Who Was Attacked & How (Feb 28–Mar 1)

Within hours of the Operation Epic Fury strikes, Iran activated its pre-planned asymmetric retaliation doctrine. The response was simultaneous and multi-front — approximately 170 ballistic missiles in coordinated salvos across five theaters, triggering the largest simultaneous air defense activation in regional history.

Summary — Iran's First Retaliatory Salvo (Feb 28–Mar 1)

Iran fired approximately ~170 ballistic missiles — the largest single Iranian missile barrage ever recorded — toward Israel and toward US military installations across four Gulf nations. Explosions were reported in Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. Air defense systems — Patriot PAC-3, THAAD, Arrow 3, Iron Dome, David's Sling, and Aegis BMD — were activated simultaneously across all theaters. As of March 4, 2026, the US-Israeli strikes have entered a second day while Iran has not launched a confirmed second ballistic-missile wave — though IRGC doctrine calls for a follow-on salvo 24–48 hours after an initial barrage to target repair crews and overwhelm reconstituted air defenses.

  • Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base

What Remains Unknown — & The Second-Wave Risk

Precise interception rates, confirmed ground damage at targeted bases, US/Israeli/Gulf casualty figures, and whether Houthi and Iraqi PMF attacks were launched simultaneously are all not yet confirmed as of March 4, 2026. The critical unknown is whether Iran has already initiated — or is planning — a second ballistic-missile wave. IRGC doctrine from the 2024 Israel barrages called for a follow-on salvo 24–48 hours after the first, specifically targeting repair crews and taking advantage of partially-depleted interceptor magazines. With Khamenei now dead and the IRGC command structure fragmented, authorization for a second wave may rest with corps-level commanders operating under pre-delegated authority — making it simultaneously harder to predict and harder to deter through conventional diplomatic signaling.
