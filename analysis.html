What the War Has Established—and What It Has Not

The best institutional work converges on the operational picture and diverges over leverage, success, and the requirements of a durable settlement
Bottom line

The United States and Israel have inflicted severe conventional damage and retain the operational initiative. Iran has nevertheless preserved enough distributed capability and geographic leverage to prevent military superiority from becoming a clean political outcome. The conflict has moved from a campaign expected to compel rapid change into a contest over endurance, Hormuz, reconstitution, and the terms of an incomplete peace.

01

Proposition: Iran Is Militarily Defeated

True in one sense, misleading in another

Evidence for the proposition

U.S. and Israeli forces have sustained access across Iran, damaged command and air-defense networks, struck missile and drone infrastructure, and imposed major naval and logistics losses. RAND describes substantial operational gains; CSIS assesses serious damage that Iran will struggle to reverse quickly.

Why it is incomplete

Military defeat is not disarmament. Mobile missiles, drones, coastal systems, mines, small craft, cyber tools, partner relationships, and replaceable production inputs can remain effective without a functioning conventional fleet or air-defense umbrella. CSIS's reconstitution work treats damage as the start of a continuing contest, not a final state.

02

Proposition: Leadership Decapitation Weakened the State

It altered the ruling coalition; it did not automatically dissolve it

Ali Khamenei's death removed the system's ultimate arbiter and created a real succession shock. Mojtaba Khamenei's selection restored a formal center, while wartime command increased the influence of the IRGC and other security institutions. CTP–ISW emphasizes this militarization; IISS argues that war interrupted protest momentum and strengthened the Guards' practical role. The counterpoint is that greater coercive concentration may store up future instability by narrowing accountability, worsening civilian burdens, and tying regime survival more tightly to military performance.

Assessment

The state is more militarized and more brittle, not simply stronger or weaker. The indicators that would resolve the question are observable elite splits, repeated command failures, large verified defections, or a durable civilian-security bargain—not rumors about personalities alone.

RAND's early judgment that escalation would not work was correct if “work” meant defeating U.S. conventional power or forcing immediate withdrawal. Later evidence partly falsified a broader reading: Iran converted maritime disruption and infrastructure vulnerability into real bargaining leverage. RUSI and IISS stress that this leverage is costly to Iran itself and cannot by itself deliver a stable order.

04

Proposition: Military Pressure Will Produce Political Change

The causal mechanism remains unproven

Why pressure could weaken the state

  • Leadership loss and command stress can widen elite disagreement.
  • Infrastructure damage and economic failure can erode legitimacy.
  • A visibly ineffective security promise can change public and elite risk calculations.

Why pressure can strengthen coercive rule

  • External attack can produce nationalism and discredit foreign-aligned opposition.
  • Blackouts, displacement, and fear make organizing harder.
  • Wartime institutions gain authority while civilian politics contract.

The historical record and current observation do not support a simple bombing-to-democratization pathway. Political change depends on organization, elite behavior, security institutions, territorial bargains, and an administratively credible alternative. Civilian suffering is therefore not evidence that a desired transition is approaching.

The analytical danger now

Every side can point to evidence of success: destroyed assets, surviving salvos, partial shipping recovery, continued state control, or renewed negotiations. None is a sufficient measure of the war's outcome. The right test is whether a future arrangement reduces attacks, restores normal commerce, reconstructs nuclear verification, constrains reconstitution, protects civilians, and survives a serious violation.
