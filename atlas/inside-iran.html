Inside Iran: A Compound National Crisis

War damage, coercive rule, interrupted livelihoods, degraded public systems, and political uncertainty are acting on the same society at once
The most important caution

Iran is unusually hard to observe. An 88-day nationwide internet shutdown ended only partially, access remains restricted, foreign reporting is constrained, and both state and opposition channels have strong incentives to shape the picture. Silence is not proof of consent. A viral video is not proof of a nationwide uprising. Every judgment about public mood or political control should remain proportional to the evidence.

War and Civilian Harm

The damage extends beyond target lists into housing, health, water, electricity, movement, and family life

The humanitarian crisis is geographically uneven. Some communities experienced direct strikes and displacement; others face degraded services, inflation, shortages, school interruption, health-system pressure, and fear of renewed attack. Renewed July strikes mean the May baseline is best understood as a floor and a point of comparison, not a live counter.

02

Who Governs

Succession is constitutionally clearer than the practical distribution of authority

Formal structure

Mojtaba Khamenei was selected supreme leader on 8 March after Ali Khamenei was killed in the opening strikes. President Masoud Pezeshkian, Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, senior clerics, the Supreme National Security Council, and the armed institutions remain visible parts of the state.

Practical authority

The exact balance is opaque. CTP–ISW assesses that the IRGC has gained influence in wartime decision-making, and commander Ahmad Vahidi is central to the security picture. That is a defensible assessment, not direct proof that constitutional government has been replaced by a military junta.

Resilient does not mean stable

The state still commands coercive institutions, administers territory, speaks through recognized offices, and can organize military and diplomatic action. It also faces leadership losses, damaged infrastructure, economic strain, public anger, and uncertainty over elite cohesion. Both facts can be true at the same time.

03

Economy and Daily Life

The war reaches households through prices, income, connectivity, mobility, and public services

Income

Sanctions, disrupted exports, damaged facilities, and interrupted commerce reduce state revenue and private employment even when some trade continues.

Prices

Currency weakness, scarcity, transport disruption, and uncertainty amplify the prewar inflation burden.

Services

Health, water, electricity, communications, and municipal systems face physical damage, shortages, and overloaded staff.

National oil-price headlines do not describe household welfare. The key internal questions are whether salaries arrive, medicines and food can move, banks and communications function, and provincial services can absorb both war damage and displacement.

04

Protest, Repression, and Political Mood

Wartime mobilization can suppress visible dissent without resolving the grievances that produced it

Nationwide protest before the war drew on economic grievance, demands for political freedom, anger over gender repression, ethnic and regional inequality, and the legacy of Woman, Life, Freedom. The state responded with lethal force, arrests, surveillance, and communications controls. War then changed the environment: fear, displacement, nationalism, and the immediate need to survive can reduce public action even as anger deepens.

05

Water, Health, and Infrastructure

Prewar scarcity and governance failure magnify every wartime disruption

Water stress

Long-running drought, groundwater depletion, inefficient agriculture, and contested allocation already made water a political issue. Damage to pumping, power, treatment, and transport systems can turn structural scarcity into local emergency.

Health-system stress

Mass casualties arrive alongside shortages, damaged facilities, displaced patients, interrupted supply chains, and the ordinary burden of chronic disease. WHO's emergency support is evidence of system strain, not a complete national assessment.

06

The Regional War at Home

Iran's external strategy and its domestic crisis can no longer be separated

Missile and drone operations, partner-force relationships, Hormuz, and the nuclear program are often described as foreign-policy files. Inside Iran they are also arguments about national security, revolutionary identity, scarce public money, and the price citizens are being asked to pay for deterrence. Successful attacks can produce pride among some Iranians; retaliation, isolation, and infrastructure loss can deepen opposition among others. There is no reliable basis for reducing that range of responses to a single national mood.

Best present judgment

The Islamic Republic remains a functioning coercive state under extraordinary strain. Iranian society remains politically alive under conditions that make collective action difficult to see and dangerous to attempt. Claims of imminent collapse and claims of durable wartime unity are both ahead of the evidence.
