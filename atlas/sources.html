How the Report Knows What It Knows

Different sources answer different questions; none is treated as a universal authority
Source typeBest usePrimary limitation
Primary international bodiesSafeguards, humanitarian compilations, energy balances, maritime advisoriesReporting lags, access limits, and dependence on member-state data
Official belligerent releasesWhat a government says it did, targeted, intercepted, or intendsNot independent proof of effect, loss, or legal characterization
Independent news organizationsCurrent events, multi-source confirmation, named-official reportingEarly reports change; anonymous sourcing and access vary
Specialist institutionsOperational, strategic, economic, legal, and regional interpretationMethods and policy preferences differ; many figures originate with belligerents
Commercial and technical dataShipping, pricing, connectivity, imagery, and market behaviorCoverage gaps, proprietary methods, and false precision
Advocacy and aligned outletsClaims, narratives, local leads, and underreported constituenciesSelection bias and weak independence; claims require corroboration

Publication rules

  • Separate struck, damaged, and destroyed.
  • Date inventories, casualty figures, prices, traffic measures, and official assessments.
  • Label belligerent claims and institutional forecasts.
  • Do not convert silence, blackout, or absence of evidence into a positive conclusion.
  • Replace old synthesis with a fresh cross-source judgment; retain only explicitly time-bounded historical analysis.

What the report will not publish as settled fact

  • Exact remaining Iranian missile, drone, launcher, mine, or vessel inventories.
  • A single undated percentage for Hormuz traffic.
  • That Iran is denuclearized, has resumed enrichment, or has decided to build a weapon without direct evidence.
  • Representative support for any opposition figure without representative data.
  • Precise scenario probabilities unsupported by a defensible model.

Core Source Library

The standing sources used to rebuild the report, organized by the question they can answer

Primary and intergovernmental

  • International Atomic Energy Agency — safeguards, inspection access, and nuclear verification.
  • UN/OCHA Humanitarian Update No. 4 — time-bounded civilian and infrastructure impact with stated methodological limits.
  • International Energy Agency — oil production, exports, supply, inventories, and prices.
  • U.S. Energy Information Administration — market response and U.S. energy context.
  • UKMTO / JMIC — maritime advisories and risk characterization.
  • U.S. Central Command — attributed U.S. operational claims and announcements.

Current reporting anchors

Associated Press and Reuters are the primary cross-domain news anchors. BBC, AFP, the New York Times, Washington Post, Financial Times, Guardian, CNN, NPR, Al Jazeera, regional outlets, and specialist trade publications are used when they add direct reporting, named sourcing, local access, or a distinct data set. A homepage link is never substituted for a specific article in a factual citation.

  • AP: IAEA safeguards reporting, 4 June 2026
  • AP: Pickaxe Mountain, 22 July 2026
  • AP: Gulf allies' exposure, 6 March 2026
  • AP: Pahlavi and Kurdish opposition tensions, 27 February 2026

Strategy, operations, and war termination

  • CSIS Iran war analysis — current phase, reconstitution, costs, energy, and diplomacy.
  • RAND Iran research — operational effect, escalation, and war-termination trade-offs.
  • IISS Online Analysis — campaign assessment, Gulf strikes, maritime security, and counter-proliferation.
  • RUSI Iran research — maritime limits, loitering munitions, blockade leverage, and state resilience.
  • CTP–ISW Iran Updates — high-frequency chronology and explicitly attributed operational assessment.

Regional, political, economic, and dissenting analysis

  • Carnegie Endowment — Gulf politics, Iranian adaptation, and wider regional spillover.
  • Brookings — energy, trade, maritime normalization, legal questions, and diplomacy.
  • Council on Foreign Relations — U.S. policy, deal breakdown, sanctions, and Hormuz.
  • Atlantic Council — U.S.–Israeli divergence, energy, maritime policy, and competing prescriptions.
  • Chatham House — critical analysis of war aims, Iranian adaptation, and Gulf equilibrium.
  • Middle East Institute — Iranian political economy and spillovers into trade, food security, and Asia.
A source list is not a confidence score

Confidence comes from fit between claim and evidence, independence, date, scope, corroboration, and honest treatment of conflict—not from the number of links attached to a paragraph. The report cites specific articles near consequential claims and uses this library to make the research surface auditable.

Four rules for reading the report

The source page keeps methodology visible without turning the reader experience into an update dashboard.

Editorial standard

The reader should be able to tell what is known, inferred, and still contested.

Primary records establish baselines; independent reporting verifies events; specialist institutions supply analysis. None of those roles is interchangeable.

Required disclosure

Serious sources often disagree because they answer different questions.

The report maps those disagreements instead of averaging them away. Belligerent claims, imagery estimates, humanitarian compilations, and policy arguments keep their provenance and limits.
