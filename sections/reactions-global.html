International reactions to Operation Epic Fury — Mar 1–15, 2026
Elevated
Apr 19 — U.S. fires on and seizes an Iran‑flagged cargo ship attempting to breach the blockade

U.S. forces fired on and disabled an Iran‑flagged cargo vessel on Apr 19 and then boarded and seized it after it failed to comply with repeated warnings; Tehran warned of retaliation and U.S. negotiators continued planning talks in Pakistan, raising doubts about the ceasefire's durability. — Reuters; CNN; Apr 19

Apr 18 — Iran reimposes strict control of the Strait; gunboats fire on tankers

Reuters and NBC reported Apr 18 that Iran reverted to strict control of the Strait of Hormuz and that IRGC gunboats fired on at least two vessels attempting transit; the U.S. said its blockade remained in effect and that transits were contested. — Reuters; NBC; Apr 18

Apr 4–5 — Two U.S. warplanes downed; previously missing crew recovered in high‑risk CSAR operation

Initial reporting said Iranian air defenses shot down a U.S. F-15E over Iran and a separate U.S. A-10 crashed near the Strait of Hormuz on Apr 3; U.S. forces conducted a risky search‑and‑rescue operation on Apr 4–5 that recovered the previously missing airman. Iranian officials said several "enemy" aircraft were destroyed during the mission. — The New York Times; BBC; Reuters; Apr 3–5

Apr 12 — U.S. announces blockade of the Strait of Hormuz

President Trump announced on Apr 12 that the U.S. Navy would begin blocking and interdicting vessels entering or leaving the Strait of Hormuz; officials said the action is intended to prevent Iranian tolling/payment schemes and to interdict ships deemed to be transiting under Iranian control. — Reuters; AP; PBS; Apr 12

Apr 16 — Israel–Lebanon 10‑day truce begins; diplomacy advances

A 10‑day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon went into effect on Apr 16; President Trump said diplomacy with Iran showed signs of progress and Reuters reported negotiators had narrowed differences and were discussing a limited interim memorandum. — AP; Reuters; Apr 16

Mar 31 — Tanker struck off Dubai; US says war at a decisive moment

Reuters reported a giant oil tanker off Dubai was hit in an Iranian strike on Mar 31; U.S. officials described the campaign as entering a "decisive" phase while Tehran threatened further actions against US businesses in the region. The incident added fresh pressure on markets and regional shipping security. — Reuters, Mar 31

Mar 28–29 — Houthis join the conflict with missile and drone strikes on Israel; strikes continue

Reports say Yemen‑based Houthi forces launched missile, drone, and cruise missile attacks targeting Israeli military sites on Mar 27–28, widening the war beyond Iran and Israel. Multiple outlets reported continued strike waves across Tehran and Iranian ports on Mar 29. — The Guardian; ISW; Bloomberg; Mar 27–29

Mar 23 — Iran strikes injure 160+ in southern Israel; strikes continue

BBC reported more than 160 people were injured in Iranian strikes on southern Israel; NBC and Jerusalem Post reported additional strike waves and targeted killings of regime-linked commanders in recent days. CENTCOM and allied statements continue to describe intense strike operations across the theater. — BBC; NBC News; Jerusalem Post; Mar 22–23

Mar 23 — U.S. announces pause on strikes against Iranian energy infrastructure

President Trump announced a five‑day pause on strikes targeting Iranian energy infrastructure while US‑Iran diplomatic talks proceed; the administration described conversations as "very good and productive." — Politico, Mar 23

Apr 8 — Israel strikes in Lebanon kill 250 in deadliest day of the war

Reuters reported that Israeli strikes pummelled Lebanon on Apr 8, killing more than 250 people in the deadliest day since the conflict expanded to Lebanon; the heavy strikes occurred even as a two‑week ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran was announced, raising questions about the ceasefire's geographic scope and durability. — Reuters, Apr 8

Apr 11 — Islamabad talks end without agreement

Reuters and NPR report the U.S.–Iran talks in Islamabad concluded on Apr 11 after roughly 21 hours without reaching an accord; negotiators left key issues unresolved, leaving the ceasefire fragile. — Reuters; NPR; Apr 11

Mar 10 — US Senate Democrats Demand Public Hearings on Iran War

A group of Democratic senators called for immediate public hearings, citing concerns over strategy, civilian harm, and troop protection. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9–10

Mar 9 — Trump: 'Nowhere Near' Deciding to Send Troops Into Iran

President Trump said he is "nowhere near" deciding whether to deploy US troops into Iran to secure enriched uranium stockpiles, downplaying speculation of imminent ground operations. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9

Mar 20 — UN Security Council Holds Urgent Consultations on Gulf Energy Attacks

The UN Security Council convened urgent closed consultations to discuss recent attacks on Gulf energy facilities and the wider regional implications; European leaders publicly urged a moratorium on strikes targeting energy and water infrastructure. — Source: Times of Israel (Tier‑4), Mar 20

Mar 21 — Natanz enrichment site reportedly struck

Reports say an airstrike hit the Natanz enrichment facility using bunker‑buster munitions; Iranian media reported damage with no reported radioactive leak and the IAEA has said it detected no rise in radiation levels outside the facility. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2); IAEA; Washington Post (Tier‑2/1), Mar 20–22

Apr 25 — US cancels planned envoy trip to Pakistan

BBC and other outlets reported that President Trump cancelled plans to send envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to Pakistan on Apr 25, calling off the scheduled delegation and pausing the planned indirect talks with Iran. — BBC; CNN; Apr 25

Energy & Shipping Markets — Updated Mar 20

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely disrupted into week three. Kharg Island strikes and ongoing maritime attacks have kept Brent crude above $100/barrel at times, though prices swung after reports the U.S. may release sanctioned Iranian crude. Governments are discussing naval escorts and insurance backstops; the IEA is reportedly considering further emergency reserve releases as markets remain volatile. On Mar 20, the U.S. Treasury announced a temporary 30‑day waiver (Mar 20–Apr 19) allowing the sale of roughly ~140 million barrels of Iranian oil already at sea to relieve immediate market pressure; refiners in Asia are examining purchases. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2); BBC (Tier‑2); CNBC (Tier‑2); Bloomberg (Tier‑2); CNN, Mar 19–21
