International reactions to Operation Epic Fury — Mar 1–15, 2026
Elevated
Apr 19 — U.S. fires on and seizes an Iran‑flagged cargo ship attempting to breach the blockade

U.S. forces fired on and disabled an Iran‑flagged cargo vessel on Apr 19 and then boarded and seized it after it failed to comply with repeated warnings; Tehran warned of retaliation and U.S. negotiators continued planning talks in Pakistan, raising doubts about the ceasefire's durability. — Reuters; CNN; Apr 19

May 4 — U.S. announces escort plan; Iran reports turning back a U.S. warship

President Trump announced on May 3 a program to help stranded commercial vessels exit the Strait of Hormuz ("Project Freedom"); Reuters reported on May 4 that Iran said it turned back a U.S. warship near the strait and U.S. officials denied a strike, underscoring the operational risks that continue to complicate diplomacy. — NYT; CNN; Reuters; May 3–4

May 5 — U.S. pauses Project Freedom amid reported diplomatic progress

NBC and The Guardian report that President Trump paused the Project Freedom escort mission on May 5, citing progress in negotiations; earlier in the operation two American‑flagged merchant ships successfully transited the strait under U.S. protection. — NBC; The Guardian; May 5

May 6 — U.S. fires on Iranian oil tanker as talks advance

CENTCOM reported U.S. forces fired on and disabled an Iranian oil tanker on May 6 after it attempted to breach the blockade; U.S. officials framed the action as enforcement while diplomacy on a potential deal continued. — AP; Reuters; May 6

May 6–8 — U.S. fires on two Iran‑flagged tankers; exchanges test ceasefire

CENTCOM and multiple outlets reported that U.S. forces fired on and disabled two Iran‑flagged oil tankers on May 6–7 after attempts to breach the blockade; exchanges of fire around the Strait of Hormuz continued on May 8 while President Trump said the ceasefire remained in effect. — Reuters; AP; CNBC; BBC; May 6–8

May 27 — U.S. conducts strikes in southern Iran; Iran retaliates

Reuters and CNN reported that U.S. forces conducted limited strikes on May 27 against drone launch and control facilities near Bandar Abbas after intercepting multiple Iranian attack drones; Iran said it struck an American base in retaliation, raising tensions and threatening the fragile ceasefire. — Reuters; CNN; May 27–28

May 28–29 — Negotiators report tentative 60‑day MoU to extend ceasefire

U.S. and Iranian negotiators reported reaching a tentative memorandum of understanding to extend the ceasefire for roughly 60 days and to open a new round of nuclear talks; U.S. officials said the framework required final approval from the president and Iran had not publicly confirmed all elements. — Reuters; AP; May 28–29

May 30 — U.S. disables merchant vessel attempting to breach blockade

Reuters reported on May 30 that U.S. forces struck and disabled a merchant ship attempting to reach an Iranian port, saying the action prevented a breach of the blockade and highlighting continued enforcement measures amid talks. — Reuters; May 30

Jun 1 — U.S. conducts self‑defense strikes on Iranian radar and drone command sites; Iran reports retaliatory action

Reuters reported Jun 1 that U.S. Central Command conducted self‑defense strikes on Iranian radar and drone command and control sites over the weekend (Goruk, Qeshm); Iranian statements said Tehran struck back at U.S. positions and reporting indicated missiles were intercepted overnight with no personnel harmed. — Reuters; New York Times; Jun 1

Jun 18 — Islamabad Memorandum signed; ceasefire implementation begins

On Jun 18 U.S. and Iranian representatives signed a 14‑point Islamabad Memorandum committing both sides to end hostilities, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and enter a 60‑day negotiation window; mediators and Pakistan's prime minister said implementation steps began immediately and Iran agreed to invite IAEA inspections. — Reuters; AP; Jun 18–19

Jun 19 — Israel & Hezbollah agree ceasefire; U.S.–Iran talks postponed

Reuters and the New York Times report that Israel and Hezbollah agreed to a ceasefire on Jun 19, a development that briefly stalled the planned next round of U.S.–Iran talks in Switzerland as negotiators reassessed timing and sequencing. — Reuters; NYT; Jun 19

Jun 26 — U.S. strikes Iranian missile, drone and radar sites after vessel attack

U.S. Central Command said on Jun 26 that U.S. forces carried out strikes on Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in retaliation for the drone attack on a commercial vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz; officials described the strikes as self‑defense and released footage from the operation, a development that tested the durability of the Islamabad Memorandum and prompted renewed diplomatic activity. — AP; Reuters; NYT; Jun 26

Jun 27 — U.S. conducts additional strikes after vessel attack; Iran reports strikes on U.S. sites

U.S. Central Command said U.S. forces conducted additional strikes on Iranian military targets on Jun 27 in response to attacks on commercial shipping near the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials said they struck U.S. facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait; those claims were reported by international outlets but have not been independently verified. — Reuters; AP; NYT; Jun 27

Jul 1–2 — Indirect talks in Doha: positive progress; negotiations to continue

U.S. and Iranian negotiators met separately in Doha on Jul 1–2 with Qatari and Pakistani mediators; hosts reported "positive progress" and said discussions would continue. — AP; NPR; CNN; Jul 1–2

Jul 3 — Talks paused for Khamenei's funeral

Update (Jul 3): U.S. officials agreed to pause direct talks with Iranian negotiators for roughly one week while Iran conducts multi‑day funeral ceremonies for the late Supreme Leader beginning Jul 4; mediators said technical discussions would be suspended during the ceremonies and resume afterward. — Fox News; NPR; Washington Times; Jul 3–4
