Global Diplomatic Response

International reactions to Operation Epic Fury — Mar 1–15, 2026
Elevated
Apr 19 — U.S. fires on and seizes an Iran‑flagged cargo ship attempting to breach the blockade

U.S. forces fired on and disabled an Iran‑flagged cargo vessel on Apr 19 and then boarded and seized it after it failed to comply with repeated warnings; Tehran warned of retaliation and U.S. negotiators continued planning talks in Pakistan, raising doubts about the ceasefire's durability. — Reuters; CNN; Apr 19

May 4 — U.S. announces escort plan; Iran reports turning back a U.S. warship

President Trump announced on May 3 a program to help stranded commercial vessels exit the Strait of Hormuz ("Project Freedom"); Reuters reported on May 4 that Iran said it turned back a U.S. warship near the strait and U.S. officials denied a strike, underscoring the operational risks that continue to complicate diplomacy. — NYT; CNN; Reuters; May 3–4

May 5 — U.S. pauses Project Freedom amid reported diplomatic progress

NBC and The Guardian report that President Trump paused the Project Freedom escort mission on May 5, citing progress in negotiations; earlier in the operation two American‑flagged merchant ships successfully transited the strait under U.S. protection. — NBC; The Guardian; May 5

May 6 — U.S. fires on Iranian oil tanker as talks advance

CENTCOM reported U.S. forces fired on and disabled an Iranian oil tanker on May 6 after it attempted to breach the blockade; U.S. officials framed the action as enforcement while diplomacy on a potential deal continued. — AP; Reuters; May 6

May 6–8 — U.S. fires on two Iran‑flagged tankers; exchanges test ceasefire

CENTCOM and multiple outlets reported that U.S. forces fired on and disabled two Iran‑flagged oil tankers on May 6–7 after attempts to breach the blockade; exchanges of fire around the Strait of Hormuz continued on May 8 while President Trump said the ceasefire remained in effect. — Reuters; AP; CNBC; BBC; May 6–8

May 27 — U.S. conducts strikes in southern Iran; Iran retaliates

Reuters and CNN reported that U.S. forces conducted limited strikes on May 27 against drone launch and control facilities near Bandar Abbas after intercepting multiple Iranian attack drones; Iran said it struck an American base in retaliation, raising tensions and threatening the fragile ceasefire. — Reuters; CNN; May 27–28

May 28–29 — Negotiators report tentative 60‑day MoU to extend ceasefire

U.S. and Iranian negotiators reported reaching a tentative memorandum of understanding to extend the ceasefire for roughly 60 days and to open a new round of nuclear talks; U.S. officials said the framework still required the president's approval and Iran had not publicly confirmed all terms. — Reuters; AP; May 28–29

May 30 — U.S. disables merchant vessel attempting to breach blockade

Reuters reported on May 30 that U.S. forces struck and disabled a merchant ship attempting to reach an Iranian port, saying the action prevented a breach of the blockade and highlighting continued enforcement measures amid talks. — Reuters; May 30

🇺🇸 United States

President Trump confirmed "major combat operations" against Iran, framing it as a last resort after talks failed. CENTCOM named the campaign Operation Epic Fury. The US ordered non-emergency staff to depart Saudi Arabia and deployed additional Marine/naval forces (USS Tripoli + MEU, B-2 rotations). — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9; Axios (Tier‑4), Mar 13

US Domestic — Texas Shooting (Iran-Linked Probe)

A shooting near Joint Base San Antonio left multiple casualties (Mar 1). FBI leads the investigation; suspected Iran-linked connections consistent with pre-existing threat warnings. Force protection raised nationwide. — Sources: FBI (Tier‑1), DHS (Tier‑1), Mar 1

Erdoğan condemned the attack; NATO invoked Article 4 consultations. Turkey recalled its ambassador to Tehran, closed airspace to mission-related overflights, and reserved its right of self-defense. — Reuters, Mar 4
  • 🇦🇿 Azerbaijan — Nakhchivan Struck (Mar 5): Iranian drones hit an airport and landed near a school in Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan exclave. Azerbaijan withdrew diplomats and promised a military response. Iran denied responsibility (claimed Israeli false flag). — Euronews (Tier‑4), Mar 5

Apr 19 — Oil prices jump after ship seizure and renewed Hormuz closure

Markets reacted to reports that the U.S. disabled and seized an Iran‑flagged cargo ship and that the Strait of Hormuz remained largely closed; Reuters and CNBC reported oil prices surged on Apr 19 amid fears the ceasefire could collapse and shipping disruptions would persist. — Reuters; CNBC; Apr 19

May 6–7 — Oil prices fall as reports suggest a deal may be near

CNBC and the BBC reported that oil prices fell sharply on May 6–7 after reports that U.S. and Iranian negotiators were close to a deal to end hostilities and amid a pause in the escort mission; markets reacted to reduced war-risk premia and hopes the Strait of Hormuz could reopen. — CNBC; BBC; May 6–7

Apr 5 — Ships begin limited transits through the Strait of Hormuz

Shipping data on Apr 5 showed an Iraq/Iran-linked Suezmax and other vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz, reflecting Iran allowing passage for vessels it deems friendly amid an effective blockade for others and offering limited relief to shipping flows. — Bloomberg; Reuters; Apr 5

Apr 8 — Oil prices plunge after ceasefire agreement

Reuters reported European energy shares fell and oil prices dropped on Apr 8 after news that the U.S. and Iran had agreed to a two‑week ceasefire, trimming the war risk premium that had supported a multi‑week rally. — Reuters; Apr 8

May 27 — Oil falls as investors weigh ceasefire hopes and renewed strikes

Reuters reported on May 27 that oil prices settled about 5% lower as markets balanced reports of diplomatic progress with new U.S.–Iran exchanges of fire; traders remained cautious about lasting reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. — Reuters; May 27

May 11 — Aramco warns market normalisation could be delayed until 2027

Reuters and CNBC reported on May 11 that Saudi Aramco's CEO said the oil market likely will not normalize until 2027 if disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz persist, signalling long‑range downside risk to supply and sustained energy price volatility. — Reuters; CNBC; May 11

Mar 29 — Markets react as Houthis expand conflict; oil surges

Bloomberg and the New York Times reported renewed oil and market volatility as the conflict widened with Houthi attacks and additional strikes on Iranian ports, intensifying concerns over supply disruptions and insurance costs. — Bloomberg; New York Times; Mar 29

May 20 — Limited tanker exits from Hormuz noted

Reuters reported on May 20 that two supertankers exited the Strait of Hormuz carrying roughly 6 million barrels, easing some immediate supply pressure though not restoring normal transit patterns; markets remained jittery. — Reuters; May 20

Regional Mediation Efforts — Turkey, Oman, Egypt

Turkey, Oman, and Egypt pressed for a diplomatic end to the fighting via quiet back‑channel talks with Tehran and regional capitals. Mediation remained constrained by preconditions on both sides; no public ceasefire agreement had been reached in late March. Update (Mar 23): President Trump announced a five‑day pause on strikes against Iranian energy infrastructure while diplomatic conversations continued and characterized talks as "very good and productive." — Sources: Haaretz (Tier‑4), Mar 15; AP (Tier‑2), Mar 14; Politico, Mar 23

Update (Mar 29): Pakistan hosted talks that brought together foreign ministers from Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Egypt and others to discuss diplomatic ways to halt the fighting; discussions continued without a public ceasefire agreement. — The Hill; NPR, Mar 29

Update (Apr 7–8): Reuters, AP, and NPR report that mediators' efforts culminated in a two‑week ceasefire agreement announced on Apr 7 between the U.S. and Iran, contingent on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, contingent on implementation steps that remained under negotiation. — Reuters; AP; NPR; Apr 7–8

Update (Apr 11): Reuters and NPR report that face‑to‑face talks in Islamabad concluded on Apr 11 after roughly 21 hours without an agreement, leaving mediators to continue shuttle diplomacy to resolve verification and scope disputes. — Reuters; NPR; Apr 11

Update (Apr 24): President Trump dispatched envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to Pakistan to meet Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi as mediators sought to revive ceasefire talks. — AP; Politico; Apr 24

Update (Apr 25): BBC and other outlets reported that President Trump cancelled the planned Witkoff/Kushner trip to Pakistan on Apr 25, pausing the scheduled indirect talks with Iran and signaling renewed diplomatic friction. — BBC; CNN; Apr 25

Update (Apr 27): The New York Times reported that on Apr 27 Iran proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping and lifting the U.S. blockade in exchange for postponing detailed nuclear negotiations to a later phase, a sequencing offer that mediators were said to be assessing. — New York Times; Apr 27

Update (Apr 27): BBC reported Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi travelled to Russia on Apr 27 to meet senior officials as Tehran sought political leverage while talks with the U.S. remained on hold. — BBC; Apr 27

Update (May 28–29): AP and Reuters report negotiators reached a tentative memorandum of understanding to extend the ceasefire by roughly 60 days and to open new nuclear talks; U.S. officials said the agreement required final approval from the president and Iran had not publicly confirmed all elements. — Reuters; AP; May 28–29
