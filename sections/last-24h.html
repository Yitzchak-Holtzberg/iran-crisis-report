Sep 13 An Iranian cargo vessel was struck off Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state media reported one person killed and four wounded; UKMTO separately reported a vessel hit by a projectile while transiting the strait, without confirming whether it was the same incident. Attribution remains unresolved. This is a renewed attack on commercial shipping after the September 9–10 escalation and occurred inside the Strait of Hormuz. It shows the maritime lull has not held, although the attacker, damage assessment and link to the UKMTO report remain unconfirmed. Associated Press
Sep 12 Iraq said an attack launched from its territory shut a key Saudi pipeline and accused Iran-backed militias; the militia umbrella group denied involvement. Baghdad dismissed two senior officials and ordered nonstate groups to disarm by September 30. The reported closure adds a new infrastructure front against Saudi energy exports already exposed by Houthi attacks and disruption around Hormuz. Iraq’s confirmation of the launch location is stronger than its contested attribution of responsibility. Associated Press
Sep 12 BRICS leaders meeting in New Delhi condemned attacks on civilian infrastructure and safeguarded peaceful nuclear facilities, while criticizing unilateral sanctions. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian attended. The statement gives Tehran political backing from major non-Western powers, but it does not create a ceasefire, restore nuclear inspections or establish a new negotiating mechanism. Associated Press
Sep 10 Houthi forces reportedly entered or seized Mokha, Yemen, according to residents and Yemeni government sources cited by the Associated Press. The port is about 80 kilometers from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, but the territorial claim has not been independently verified. A Houthi foothold at Mokha could increase pressure on the Red Sea approach to Bab el-Mandeb, creating a potential second maritime chokepoint at risk alongside the Strait of Hormuz. Associated Press
Sep 9 The IAEA Board of Governors voted 23–member states in favor of referring Iran’s nuclear noncompliance to the UN Security Council, citing Tehran’s failure to cooperate with investigations into uranium traces at undeclared sites. Iran denies pursuing nuclear weapons. The referral moves the dispute beyond the IAEA process and could reopen the path to UN sanctions or other Security Council action, narrowing diplomatic space while military tensions remain elevated. Associated Press
