Sep 30 Iranian officials said Tehran received a formal U.S. response to its latest proposal to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The contents were not disclosed, and there was no confirmation that Washington accepted Iran’s conditions. The exchange marks movement beyond the expected mediator meetings, but it does not establish an agreement, ceasefire, sanctions relief or verified reopening of the strait. AP
Sep 29 The United States sanctioned 10 individuals and entities in Iran, Hong Kong and Pakistan that it accused of helping procure weapons and components for Iran’s defense ministry. AP also reported that Iran’s currency fell beyond 2.5 million rials per U.S. dollar. The measures add economic pressure and target Iran’s defense supply chain while diplomacy remains unsettled, potentially increasing the difficulty of reaching a broader compromise. AP
Sep 29 UN briefers said IOM estimated approximately 144,000 people displaced inside Yemen by September 28. WHO reported 4,481 cumulative casualties since August 6, including more than 830 deaths, and said nine health facilities or health assets had been damaged or taken out of service. The updated figures show continued humanitarian deterioration beyond the previous feed, with mounting displacement and wider damage to Yemen’s already strained health system. WHO’s casualty data exclude areas outside Aden-based reporting. UN Geneva briefing
Sep 28 Qatari mediators were expected to hold separate discussions with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and U.S. officials on September 28–29 over an amended version of Iran’s proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The mediation follows Washington’s rejection of Iran’s original proposal and shows that negotiations remain active, but there is still no agreement, ceasefire or verified reopening of the strait. Reuters
Sep 25 A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran would not offer concessions on its nuclear program even if Washington accepted the proposed Hormuz arrangement. Iran’s reported conditions include lifting the port blockade, releasing frozen funds, waiving oil sanctions and ending regional hostilities. The statement separates the nuclear dispute from the proposed maritime and ceasefire deal, narrowing prospects for a comprehensive settlement. It does not provide new IAEA verification or evidence of changed nuclear activity. Reuters via MarketScreener
