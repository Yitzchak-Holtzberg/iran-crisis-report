Oct 24 Iran’s ambassador to Pakistan said Tehran remained open to U.S. talks under the Pakistan-brokered interim framework reached in June, while accusing Washington of undermining diplomacy and prolonging the Strait of Hormuz standoff. The statement is a more conciliatory signal than Iran’s October 20 position that talks would not resume until its demands were met. It indicates that mediation channels remain open, but does not show that a meeting, agreement or de-escalation has occurred. Associated Press
Oct 24 The United States is reducing staffing at four Middle Eastern embassies after intelligence indicated that Iran had increased efforts to target American personnel in the region and elsewhere, according to three U.S. officials. The locations were not identified, and no new embassy attack was reported. This broadens U.S. diplomatic force protection beyond the Saudi-specific drawdown already reported, indicating that Washington assesses the threat from Iran and affiliated groups as regional rather than confined to Saudi Arabia. Associated Press
Oct 24 Drinking-water supplies in Taiz, Yemen, have reached crisis levels as Houthi forces continue besieging the city during escalating fighting with government forces. Residents reportedly face severe difficulty obtaining water. The deterioration adds a significant humanitarian consequence to the expanding Houthi front, showing that the conflict is worsening inside Yemen as well as threatening Saudi infrastructure and Gulf shipping. Associated Press
Oct 22 Saudi Aramco ordered employees to leave its Dhahran headquarters and work remotely, according to people familiar with the matter cited in reporting. The reason was not immediately clear, and the move does not establish that a new strike hit the building. The measure is a new business-continuity and force-protection step at the headquarters of the world’s largest crude exporter, showing that perceived threats to eastern Saudi Arabia’s energy sector are affecting core corporate operations. The Economic Times
Oct 22 Iranian state television and the Revolutionary Guard said seven oil tankers were damaged or caught fire in or near the Strait of Hormuz overnight. Tehran attributed the incidents to attacks or naval mines, but the number, causes, vessel identities and Iranian responsibility were not independently verified. If confirmed, this would be the largest reported single-day disruption to commercial shipping since the war began and a significant escalation beyond the previously reported October 17 supertanker incident. Associated Press
