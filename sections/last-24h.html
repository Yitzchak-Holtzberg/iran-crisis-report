Nov 13 The U.S. military said it intercepted two Iranian attack drones headed toward Bahrain. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claimed it struck a U.S. early-warning radar system in Bahrain after a U.S. strike in southern Iran; Washington reported no injuries or damage, and the alleged radar strike was not independently verified. This marks renewed kinetic exchange after several quieter days and the first reported Iranian attempt to target a facility in Bahrain since September. The verified effect is the interception of two drones, while the claimed Iranian retaliation remains unconfirmed. Associated Press
Nov 13 Iran told the IAEA that talks on restoring nuclear monitoring cannot proceed while it faces continuing U.S. threats, especially against nuclear facilities. Iran reaffirmed that safeguards implementation could resume when conditions permit, but the IAEA said it still lacks access to several sites and cannot verify the status of Iran’s nuclear material. Nuclear transparency remains materially degraded with no operational pathway yet for restoring comprehensive inspections. The IAEA described the continuing information gap as a proliferation concern and called for gradual technical steps toward renewed access. Associated Press
Nov 11 UKMTO reported suspicious activity involving a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. Crew status, vessel damage and environmental effects were unknown when the warning was issued; authorities were investigating and vessels were advised to transit cautiously. UKMTO did not classify it as an attack or identify a responsible party. This is a new security incident at the waterway’s core, following several recent attacks and dangerous encounters around the Strait. It indicates that operational uncertainty remains high for commercial shipping, although the report does not establish that an attack occurred or who was responsible. UKMTO
Nov 10 JMIC identified the tanker struck in the Arabian Gulf on Nov. 10 as Fujairah Energy and reported that multiple crew members sustained casualties and were taken ashore for medical treatment. The remaining crew were safe; vessel damage and environmental effects remained undetermined. The incident now has confirmed reported casualties, materially worsening the maritime picture beyond the previously known projectile strike and incomplete damage assessment. It also places a consequential attack in the Arabian Gulf, beyond the immediate Strait transit lane. JMIC/UKMTO
Nov 10 UKMTO reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile 19 nautical miles northeast of Lima, Oman. The crew were safe and no environmental impact was reported; the master was expected to assess damage at first light. Attribution was not established. The incident shows that maritime attacks or dangerous encounters remain active near the eastern approaches to the Strait of Hormuz despite ongoing diplomatic signaling. It broadens the recent pattern beyond the Strait itself while leaving responsibility unresolved. UKMTO
