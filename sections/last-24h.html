Sep 25 Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran presented Washington with a concrete seven-day plan to restore normal passage through the Strait, conditional on lifting the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports, easing oil sanctions and observing a ceasefire including Lebanon. The proposal adds a public timetable and clearer conditions to the exploratory Hormuz talks, but there is no reported U.S. acceptance, ceasefire or verified reopening. AP via Local 10
Sep 24 U.S. and Iranian negotiators reportedly explored a phased formula in which Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz while the United States lifted its blockade of Iranian ports and vessels, potentially with access to frozen Iranian assets. The framework remained preliminary and the sides were still far apart. The talks have advanced from initial indirect contacts to discussion of a concrete sequencing formula linking Hormuz access to blockade relief, though there is no agreement, ceasefire or confirmed concession. Sep 24 U.S. sanctions targeting Iran’s aviation sector took effect after a deadline barred foreign companies from continuing work with Iranian airlines. Iranian flights to Gulf destinations, including Dubai, appeared to be canceled, although the scale and precise causes were not independently established. The measure adds a new economic and civilian-connectivity pressure point to the crisis, potentially restricting Iran’s access to regional air links beyond the existing maritime and financial pressures. Reuters via AOL
Sep 23 U.S. Central Command said 115 commercial vessels had been redirected by September 23 while enforcing its “steel wall blockade” around Iran. The figure was reported as a CENTCOM claim and was not independently verified in the available evidence. The claim suggests maritime disruption extends well beyond individual vessel attacks, indicating broader commercial-routing effects around the Strait of Hormuz, though the reported tally should not be treated as independently confirmed. ET Now News
Sep 23 U.S. and Iranian representatives held their first mediator-facilitated contacts in months on the margins of the UN General Assembly. Abbas Araghchi communicated through mediators with Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner; there was no face-to-face meeting or reported shift in core positions. The contacts reopen a channel for possible de-escalation after the July ceasefire arrangement failed, but the lack of direct talks or concessions means they mark an opening rather than a negotiated breakthrough.
