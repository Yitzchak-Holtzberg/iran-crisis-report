Oct 15 Yemeni government officials cited by Reuters said Houthi forces fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden. Two security guards were reportedly killed, while Yemen’s prime minister and cabinet members at the complex were unharmed. The specific military target could not be independently verified. The attack extends the Saudi-Houthi retaliation cycle to a high-level government compound and produced the first reported fatalities in the Aden exchange, marking a further escalation beyond the previously reported damage to Saudi-linked facilities. Reuters
Oct 14 UKMTO recorded attacks on an inbound 50,000-ton container ship and an outbound 19,000-ton container ship on October 14. Both sustained minor damage, reported no casualties, and continued underway; the attacker was not identified. The incidents independently confirm repeated attacks on commercial shipping beyond Iran’s unverified mine-sinking claim and show that both directions of transit remained exposed. UKMTO did not attribute the attacks to Iran. UKMTO
Oct 14 Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Navy said a cargo ship sank after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz on October 14. Iranian state television gave few details and did not confirm the vessel’s identity, location, or crew status. If confirmed, this would be the first reported vessel sinking in the current maritime crisis and would raise the threat beyond the projectile attacks already recorded. The mine’s origin, timing, cause, and casualties remain unverified. Associated Press
Oct 14 Two regional officials told The Associated Press that a Houthi strike overnight damaged offices used by senior Aramco executives, reportedly including the chief executive’s office. No casualties were reported, and Saudi authorities and Aramco had not independently confirmed the account. The reported attack would represent rapid Houthi retaliation after Saudi strikes on Sanaa and would broaden the exchange toward a high-profile Saudi economic target. It remains unclear whether the reported damage is confirmed. Associated Press
Oct 13 Two Iranian officials told Reuters that Iran began using different offensive tactics against vessels it considers to be violating its restrictions on Strait of Hormuz traffic about ten days earlier. The officials did not disclose the tactics. The report indicates a possible deliberate Iranian operational adaptation behind the recent rise in maritime attacks and the collapse in commercial traffic. It does not establish Iranian responsibility for every incident or identify the new methods. Reuters via MarketScreener
