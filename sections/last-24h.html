Oct 29 Iranian state media, citing the IRGC, said three oil tankers struck mines and caught fire while attempting to leave the Strait of Hormuz by an unauthorized route. The vessels, damage and responsibility for laying mines were not independently confirmed. If confirmed, the incident would mark a major escalation in maritime disruption beyond isolated projectile attacks. The account remains an Iranian claim, with no independent confirmation of the number of ships, cause or operational impact. Associated Press
Oct 28 IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi said Iran had not attempted to repair or reconstruct nuclear facilities damaged in the 2025–2026 military attacks. He did not indicate that regular access or comprehensive inspections had resumed. The statement provides limited evidence against active rebuilding at damaged sites, but it does not close the wider verification gap or establish that Iran’s nuclear activities are fully accounted for. Associated Press
Oct 27 A Turkish parliamentary committee approved a defense agreement involving Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, following the October 26 visit by officials from the three countries to Tehran. The approval does not show that Turkish or Pakistani forces have entered combat. The committee action adds a formal institutional component to the regional security alignment surrounding the Iran crisis. It raises the political stakes around Gulf defense while leaving the mediation channel and the parties’ military intentions unresolved. Associated Press
Oct 27 The Iran-aligned Houthis accused Yemeni government forces of attacking a gas station near Taiz, reportedly killing at least two people. Independent confirmation of the incident and attribution was not available. The reported clash shows the Yemen front remains active and could complicate regional mediation efforts, but it does not establish a new Iranian strike or direct Iranian involvement. Associated Press
Oct 26 The Houthis claimed attacks on Riyadh airport and Saudi Arabia’s East-West crude-oil pumping stations. Reporting also said Iranian officials had discussed targeting Gulf undersea cables if Washington or Israel launched another attack; none of these claims was independently verified. The reported targets would broaden the threat to Saudi aviation, oil-export infrastructure and potentially regional communications, although no operational effects or damage were confirmed.
