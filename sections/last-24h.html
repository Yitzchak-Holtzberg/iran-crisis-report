Sep 8 Iran-backed Houthi forces attacked Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran with missiles and drones on Sept. 8, according to Saudi authorities and Reuters. Saudi officials reported 73 wounded, fires at energy facilities and temporary operational stoppages; the Houthis’ specific damage claims remain partly unverified. The attacks open a new active front against Saudi energy infrastructure, expanding the conflict beyond the U.S.–Iran maritime exchange and increasing risks to Gulf and Red Sea energy flows. Associated Press
Sep 7 Iranian officials said Tehran plans to establish an exclusion or restricted zone near the Strait of Hormuz and approve a new shipping corridor. The statements were accompanied by warnings that U.S. assets and regional energy infrastructure could face retaliation; no physical enforcement or formal navigation order was confirmed. The threatened policy would raise the risk of coercive inspections, warnings or selective interference with commercial shipping, further intensifying pressure on a waterway already experiencing suppressed traffic. Associated Press
Sep 6 Iranian officials promoted the claimed Qassem Basir missile and said it had been tested against a U.S. warship, while warning that Iran would act preemptively against perceived threats. The missile test and any effect against a vessel were not independently confirmed; Washington rejected Iran’s exclusion-zone claims. Although the capability claim is unverified, Iran’s stated shift from retaliation toward preemptive action raises escalation risks around U.S. forces and shipping in the Gulf. Associated Press
Sep 5 Iranian state media said four U.S. missiles struck a tanker about 10 kilometers from Kharg Island on Sept. 5, with no casualties. The United States had not confirmed the allegation; AP said Iran provided no evidence, while Reuters separately reported explosions of unknown origin nearby. The allegation places a reported new strike near Iran’s principal oil-export hub and could increase risks to tanker operations and energy exports. The incident, its cause and any damage remain unverified. Associated Press
Sep 4 The United States, Britain, France and Germany sought support for an IAEA Board resolution referring Iran to the U.N. Security Council over safeguards and nonproliferation obligations. The draft remained under negotiation and had not yet been submitted or adopted. The initiative would escalate the nuclear dispute toward Security Council involvement while inspectors lack access to sites affected by earlier strikes and cannot verify Iran’s enriched-uranium stockpile, further narrowing diplomatic space. Associated Press
