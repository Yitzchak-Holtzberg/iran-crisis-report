Sep 25 A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran would not offer concessions on its nuclear program even if Washington accepted the proposed Hormuz arrangement. Iran’s reported conditions include lifting the port blockade, releasing frozen funds, waiving oil sanctions and ending regional hostilities. The statement separates the nuclear dispute from the proposed maritime and ceasefire deal, narrowing prospects for a comprehensive settlement. It does not provide new IAEA verification or evidence of changed nuclear activity. Reuters via MarketScreener
Sep 25 UN agencies reported roughly 131,000–134,000 people displaced inside Yemen by September 22–25. WHO also reported 3,672 casualties from August 6 through September 18, while UNICEF cited severe access constraints, acute malnutrition and disruption to health and education services. The wider crisis is producing a substantial humanitarian spillover beyond the Hormuz theater, increasing displacement and pressure on Yemen’s already strained health, food and education systems. UN Geneva briefing
Sep 25 Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran presented Washington with a concrete seven-day plan to restore normal passage through the Strait, conditional on lifting the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports, easing oil sanctions and observing a ceasefire including Lebanon. The proposal adds a public timetable and clearer conditions to the exploratory Hormuz talks, but there is no reported U.S. acceptance, ceasefire or verified reopening. AP via Local 10
Sep 24 U.S. and Iranian negotiators reportedly explored a phased formula in which Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz while the United States lifted its blockade of Iranian ports and vessels, potentially with access to frozen Iranian assets. The framework remained preliminary and the sides were still far apart. The talks have advanced from initial indirect contacts to discussion of a concrete sequencing formula linking Hormuz access to blockade relief, though there is no agreement, ceasefire or confirmed concession. KSL.com
Sep 24 U.S. sanctions targeting Iran’s aviation sector took effect after a deadline barred foreign companies from continuing work with Iranian airlines. Iranian flights to Gulf destinations, including Dubai, appeared to be canceled, although the scale and precise causes were not independently established. The measure adds a new economic and civilian-connectivity pressure point to the crisis, potentially restricting Iran’s access to regional air links beyond the existing maritime and financial pressures. Reuters via AOL
