Oct 27 A Turkish parliamentary committee approved a defense agreement involving Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, following the October 26 visit by officials from the three countries to Tehran. The approval does not show that Turkish or Pakistani forces have entered combat. The committee action adds a formal institutional component to the regional security alignment surrounding the Iran crisis. It raises the political stakes around Gulf defense while leaving the mediation channel and the parties’ military intentions unresolved. Associated Press
Oct 27 The Iran-aligned Houthis accused Yemeni government forces of attacking a gas station near Taiz, reportedly killing at least two people. Independent confirmation of the incident and attribution was not available. The reported clash shows the Yemen front remains active and could complicate regional mediation efforts, but it does not establish a new Iranian strike or direct Iranian involvement. Associated Press
Oct 26 The Houthis claimed attacks on Riyadh airport and Saudi Arabia’s East-West crude-oil pumping stations. Reporting also said Iranian officials had discussed targeting Gulf undersea cables if Washington or Israel launched another attack; none of these claims was independently verified. The reported targets would broaden the threat to Saudi aviation, oil-export infrastructure and potentially regional communications, although no operational effects or damage were confirmed. ThePrint (Reuters report)
Oct 26 High-level Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish delegations traveled to Tehran and met Iranian officials. Discussions reportedly covered the U.S.-Iran framework, the Strait of Hormuz and the Houthi-Saudi conflict, but no agreement or ceasefire was announced. The mediation effort has broadened beyond Pakistan and Qatar to include Saudi Arabia and Turkey, creating a more substantial diplomatic channel amid the regional crisis. It does not yet demonstrate a U.S.-Iran meeting or de-escalation. Associated Press
Oct 26 Egypt’s parliament authorized President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi to provide military support to Saudi Arabia in defending against attacks attributed to Iran-backed Houthi forces. The authorization does not establish that Egyptian forces have deployed or entered combat. Cairo has moved from diplomatic positioning toward formal domestic authorization for possible support to Riyadh, increasing the political and regional stakes of the Houthi-Saudi conflict without yet confirming Egyptian military involvement. Associated Press
