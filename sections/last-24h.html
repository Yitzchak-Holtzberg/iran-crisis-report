Sep 22 Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian traveled to New York and said Tehran was wary of further talks, accusing Washington of seeking Iran’s surrender after failing to achieve it militarily. His statement did not rule out indirect negotiations. Iran is maintaining a public refusal-to-yield posture even as diplomatic contacts continue around the UN, narrowing the space for an immediate agreement while leaving channels for indirect talks open. Sep 21 A tanker conducting an inbound Strait of Hormuz transit was struck by an unknown projectile on September 21. Two crew members suffered minor injuries, and the vessel continued under its own power; UKMTO did not identify the attacker. The incident provides stronger evidence that limited Hormuz transit remains hazardous and could further deter commercial shipping, but it does not independently establish Iranian responsibility. Associated Press
Sep 20 Iran reiterated that it would not reopen the Strait of Hormuz until its conditions were met and U.S. commitments implemented. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf said Tehran would combine military action with diplomacy. The statement confirms that Tehran continues to use maritime access as a negotiating lever, while the new tanker incident shows that limited transit remains hazardous rather than restored. The Economic Times
Sep 18 South Korea said it was willing to make a “substantive contribution” to restoring free passage through the Strait of Hormuz after talks in Washington between Foreign Minister Cho Hyun and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Seoul announced no deployment or operational details. The statement shows widening international concern over the disruption and possible diplomatic support for reopening the waterway, but it does not yet amount to a military commitment or demonstrate restored freedom of navigation.
