Sep 10 Houthi forces reportedly entered or seized Mokha, Yemen, according to residents and Yemeni government sources cited by the Associated Press. The port is about 80 kilometers from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, but the territorial claim has not been independently verified. A Houthi foothold at Mokha could increase pressure on the Red Sea approach to Bab el-Mandeb, creating a potential second maritime chokepoint at risk alongside the Strait of Hormuz. Associated Press
Sep 9 Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said it fired ballistic missiles at a U.S.-used base in Jordan and attacked 10 vessels near the Strait of Hormuz, including two U.S. vessels and eight oil tankers, in retaliation for the U.S. tanker strikes. The claims and any resulting damage were not independently verified. The reported retaliation expands the latest escalation from tanker strikes to a U.S.-used military facility in Jordan and, if the maritime claims are confirmed, would substantially widen attacks on commercial shipping. Reuters via Investing.com
Sep 8 U.S. Central Command said it destroyed five Iranian crude-oil tankers on Sept. 8 after Iran’s Revolutionary Guard allegedly fired ballistic missiles at a U.S. Navy warship on two successive days. Strike video was released, but independent confirmation of the vessel count and damage was unavailable. The action is a major escalation beyond the three tankers reportedly struck on Sept. 5, raising the risk of further Iranian attacks on U.S. forces, tankers and shipping around the Strait of Hormuz. Associated Press
Sep 8 Iran-backed Houthi forces attacked Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran with missiles and drones on Sept. 8, according to Saudi authorities and Reuters. Saudi officials reported 73 wounded, fires at energy facilities and temporary operational stoppages; the Houthis’ specific damage claims remain partly unverified. The attacks open a new active front against Saudi energy infrastructure, expanding the conflict beyond the U.S.–Iran maritime exchange and increasing risks to Gulf and Red Sea energy flows. Associated Press
Sep 7 Iranian officials said Tehran plans to establish an exclusion or restricted zone near the Strait of Hormuz and approve a new shipping corridor. The statements were accompanied by warnings that U.S. assets and regional energy infrastructure could face retaliation; no physical enforcement or formal navigation order was confirmed. The threatened policy would raise the risk of coercive inspections, warnings or selective interference with commercial shipping, further intensifying pressure on a waterway already experiencing suppressed traffic. Associated Press
