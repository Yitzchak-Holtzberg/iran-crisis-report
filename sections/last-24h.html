Sep 21 Iran’s Revolutionary Guard warned that it would change its weapons, tactics and targets—and “change the geography of the war”—if the United States escalated. The statement did not announce a new Iranian attack. The warning raises the risk that any renewed U.S. or allied strikes could produce retaliation beyond the current battlespace, including against additional regional or U.S. interests. Associated Press
Sep 20 Iran reiterated that it would not reopen the Strait of Hormuz until its conditions were met and U.S. commitments implemented. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf said Tehran would combine military action with diplomacy. The statement confirms that Tehran continues to use maritime access as a negotiating lever, while the new tanker incident shows that limited transit remains hazardous rather than restored. The Economic Times
Sep 18 South Korea said it was willing to make a “substantive contribution” to restoring free passage through the Strait of Hormuz after talks in Washington between Foreign Minister Cho Hyun and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Seoul announced no deployment or operational details. The statement shows widening international concern over the disruption and possible diplomatic support for reopening the waterway, but it does not yet amount to a military commitment or demonstrate restored freedom of navigation. The Economic Times
Sep 18 Iranian state media, citing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, said Iranian forces struck the Togo-flagged oil tanker Trend while it was attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz. The Associated Press could not independently confirm the strike or assess the vessel’s condition. This is a new, specific claim of Iranian action against a commercial tanker and could further deter shipping through Hormuz. The vessel’s condition and the circumstances of the alleged attack remain unverified. Associated Press
Sep 18 Hundreds of thousands attended a government-organized rally in Tehran, described by the Associated Press as the largest such demonstration since the United States and Israel began the war in February. Participants pledged readiness to receive limited military training and defend Iran. The rally indicates an intensified official effort to project domestic cohesion and generate manpower amid prolonged war and economic pressure. Attendance estimates and voluntary participation were not independently verifiable. Associated Press
