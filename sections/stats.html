The Whole Picture

Six connected situations—not one stream of strikes
The shortest accurate summary

The United States and Israel dominate the conventional campaign, but Iran retains enough distributed capability and geographic leverage to sustain retaliation and disrupt commerce. The June pause did not settle Hormuz, the nuclear file, sanctions, regional deterrence, or the internal future of Iran. July's renewed attacks turned those unresolved questions back into active war.

Military campaign

Open briefing

Iran's conventional forces are heavily damaged. Missiles, drones, coastal systems, mines, small craft, and reconstitution pathways remain consequential.

Nuclear and diplomacy

Open briefing

The IAEA cannot account for the current enriched stockpile or verify that enrichment-related activity has stopped. Talks must address both immediate hostilities and lost verification.

Hormuz and commerce

Open briefing

The waterway is physically passable but commercially abnormal. Security, routes, volume, insurance, crewing, ports, and political rules are recovering at different speeds.

State and society

Open briefing

The state remains functional and more militarized. Civilian harm, repression, inflation, damaged services, water stress, and restricted communications compound one another.

Regional order

Open briefing

Gulf states want protection from Iran without permanent entrapment in an open-ended war. Israeli and U.S. thresholds for ending the campaign are not identical.

Paths ahead

Open briefing

An armed pause is easier to reach than a durable settlement. Democratic transition, managed elite adaptation, and fragmentation remain conditional paths with very different indicators.
