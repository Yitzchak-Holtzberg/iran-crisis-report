Operation Epic Fury — confirmed and assessed damage to Iranian targets (Feb 28–Mar 10, 2026)
Critical
Mar 10 — Israel Announces New Wide‑Scale Waves Across Iran

Israel said it carried out several wide‑scale waves of strikes across Iran overnight, focusing on IRGC long‑range missile launch sites and regime infrastructure. Crowds rallied in Tehran and other cities in support of the newly appointed Supreme Leader as strikes continued. — Sources: BBC (Tier‑4), Mar 10; CNN live updates (Tier‑4), Mar 9; The Jerusalem Post (Tier‑5 — framing), Mar 10

Mar 9 — Israel Launches Fresh Pre‑Dawn Strikes in Central Iran

Israel said it conducted new waves of strikes in the early hours of Monday, targeting command and missile infrastructure in central Iran. Concurrent IDF strikes continued against Hezbollah sites in Beirut. — Source: CNN live updates (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Mar 8–9 — Israeli Strikes Hit Iranian Fuel Depots; Toxic Smoke Over Tehran

Israel expanded targeting to oil storage and fuel depots around Tehran, triggering large fires and hazardous smoke. Local authorities and humanitarian groups warned of respiratory risks and possible acid rain from the plume. — Sources: The New York Times (Tier‑4), Mar 8; TIME (Tier‑4), Mar 8–9

Supreme Leader Khamenei Confirmed Dead — Mar 1, 2026

Iranian state media confirmed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei is dead. Israeli officials publicly claimed he was killed during strikes on a command bunker in Tehran. NBC News separately reported members of his family were also killed; this remains unverified due to communications disruptions. — Source: AP (Tier‑2), Reuters (Tier‑2), NBC News (Tier‑4), Mar 1

IAEA: Natanz Buildings Damaged; No Radiological Consequence — Mar 3

The International Atomic Energy Agency reported that buildings at Natanz sustained damage amid the strikes and that there was no radiological consequence observed. The IAEA is seeking access for verification of the current status of safeguarded materials and facilities. — Source: IAEA (Tier‑1), Mar 3

💀 Casualties — Updated Mar 9

After one week of strikes, the toll has risen to 1,332+ killed including at least 181 children (UNICEF). The Iranian Red Crescent reported over 6,668 civilian sites targeted. The worst single incident was the Shajareh Tayyebeh girls' school in Minab: ~150 schoolchildren among 165 killed (Feb 28). BBC imagery review also indicates collateral damage at Gandhi Hospital and sites near Golestan Palace following nearby strikes on government targets. Figures remain preliminary due to communications disruptions.

Additional fuel‑depot strikes on Mar 8 intensified urban hazards in Tehran. Independent verification remains difficult amid a partial blackout. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), BBC (Tier‑4), UNICEF (Tier‑1 UN agency), HRANA (Tier‑5 framing), TIME (Tier‑4), Mar 7–9

Gulf States: Iranian Retaliation Damage

Iran's ballistic missile and drone attacks affected US‑hosting Gulf nations — Feb 28–Mar 10, 2026
Active
Mar 8–9 — Bahrain Desalination Plant Damaged; US Orders Staff to Depart Saudi

Bahrain confirmed an Iranian drone strike damaged a water desalination plant (Mar 8). Separately, the US ordered non‑emergency staff to leave Saudi Arabia as regional strikes continued. — Sources: AP (Tier‑2), Mar 8; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Mar 9 — Renewed Iranian Strikes Across Region After Leadership Transition

Hours after Tehran named a new Supreme Leader, Iran launched fresh attacks on Israel and several Gulf states, sustaining pressure on regional air defenses. — Source: NPR/WWNO (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Mar 3 — Riyadh: U.S. Embassy Compound Struck by Drones

Reuters reporting indicates the U.S. embassy compound in Riyadh was hit by drone strikes, marking a direct attack on a U.S. diplomatic mission. Saudi and U.S. security forces responded; casualty figures not officially released as of publication. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 3

Iran's Full Retaliation — 941 Drones, 189 BMs, 8 Cruise Missiles (Cumulative)

Iran's retaliation expanded far beyond the initial ~170‑missile salvo. Cumulative totals: 941 drones, 189 ballistic missiles, and 8 cruise missiles launched at targets across the region. THAAD, Patriot, Arrow 3, and Iron Dome batteries activated. Iran also struck the Haifa oil refinery with Kheibarshekan missiles (tit‑for‑tat for Tehran fuel depot strikes). 6 US soldiers killed in a drone attack on Port Shuaiba, Kuwait — the most significant US military casualties. Note: Figures are compiled from regional reporting and may be updated as wire services confirm. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Al Jazeera (Tier‑5 framing), CBS News (Tier‑4), Mar 1–8

🇧🇭 Bahrain — US 5th Fleet Base Reported Hit

US Naval Support Activity Bahrain — home of the US 5th Fleet — was reported struck by Iranian missiles. Manama acknowledged missile activity over its territory. Damage and casualty assessments remain limited publicly. — Sources: Middle East Eye (Tier‑5 framing), Feb 28; Reuters (Tier‑2 regional roundup), Mar 1

Official response: Bahrain condemned the strikes as "a flagrant attack on Bahraini sovereignty and a direct assault on regional security."

🇦🇪 UAE — Abu Dhabi Casualty Confirmed

An Iranian missile struck near a US‑linked site in Abu Dhabi. One person was killed in Abu Dhabi and others were injured in separate incidents across the UAE. The UAE activated its Patriot and THAAD batteries; multiple intercepts were confirmed. — Sources: US News & World Report (Tier‑4), Feb 28; Al Jazeera (Tier‑5 framing), Feb 28

Official response: Formal protest to Iran and emergency National Security Council session; temporary airport closures reported.

🇶🇦 Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base Targeted

Al Udeid Air Base — the largest US air base in the Middle East and CENTCOM Forward HQ — was among targets in Iran's barrage. Patriot systems activated; initial reports indicated intercepts. US military has not confirmed base damage as of Mar 3. In parallel, Qatar declared force majeure on LNG exports after Iranian drone attacks disrupted output and logistics. — Sources: Al Jazeera (Tier‑5 framing), Feb 28; Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 1; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 7–8

Official response: Doha called for de‑escalation and offered to mediate; the Iranian ambassador was summoned.

🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia — Energy & Air Defense

Saudi Arabia activated Patriot systems as projectiles were tracked over its airspace. Impacts were reported on Saudi soil, including a strike affecting a major refinery (Mar 2) and drone hits on the U.S. Embassy compound in Riyadh (Mar 3). Reuters reports Ras Tanura refinery/export terminal closed due to attacks; assessments ongoing. The US later ordered non‑emergency personnel to depart Saudi Arabia amid continued threats. — Sources: NPR (Tier‑4), Mar 2; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 3 & Mar 7; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Regional coordination: GCC emergency summit convened in Riyadh; humanitarian and air defense cooperation expanded with US CENTCOM.

🇰🇼 Kuwait — 6 US Soldiers Killed ► MAR 8

A drone attack on Port Shuaiba killed 6 US soldiers — the most significant US military casualties of the conflict. Ali Al Salem Air Base remains on Force Protection Condition Delta (highest threat level). Note: CENTCOM later confirmed a seventh US service member has died in the broader conflict. — Sources: PBS (Tier‑4), Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 5–8; The Hill (Tier‑4), Mar 9

🇧🇭 Bahrain — Desalination Plant Hit ► MAR 8

An Iranian drone struck a water desalination plant in Bahrain on Mar 8, targeting civilian water infrastructure — a significant escalation beyond military targets. This follows earlier reporting of strikes near the US 5th Fleet base. — Source: AP (Tier‑2), Mar 8

Global Diplomatic Response

International reactions to Operation Epic Fury — Mar 1–10, 2026
Mar 10 — US Senate Democrats Demand Public Hearings on Iran War

A group of Democratic US senators called for immediate, public hearings on the Iran war and vowed to press administration officials under oath, citing concerns over strategy, civilian harm, and troop protection. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9–10

Mar 9 — Trump: 'Nowhere Near' Deciding to Send Troops Into Iran to Secure Nuclear Stockpile

President Trump said he is "nowhere near" deciding whether to deploy US troops into Iran to secure enriched uranium stockpiles, downplaying speculation of imminent ground operations. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9

🇺🇸 United States

President Trump confirmed "major combat operations" have begun against Iran, framing the operation as a last resort after talks failed. He warned of possible US casualties and placed regional forces on highest readiness. CENTCOM publicly named the campaign Operation Epic Fury. As threats persisted, the US ordered non‑emergency staff to depart Saudi Arabia, and a seventh US service member was later confirmed dead in the conflict. He also said he is not close to deciding on sending troops into Iran to secure nuclear materials. USAF B‑1B Lancers arrived at RAF Fairford to support intensified operations under Epic Fury. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9; The Hill (Tier‑4), Mar 9; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 9; Defense News (Tier‑3), Mar 9

💥 US Domestic — Texas Shooting (Iran‑Linked Probe)

A shooting incident near Joint Base San Antonio, Texas left multiple casualties on March 1. The FBI is leading the investigation; a suspect was apprehended. Officials cited suspected Iran‑linked connections consistent with pre‑existing threat warnings. Force protection levels were raised nationwide. — Sources: FBI (Tier‑1), DHS advisories (Tier‑1), Mar 1

🇷🇺 Russia

Russia condemned the operation as "a gross violation of international law" and called an emergency UN Security Council session, warning of "unpredictable consequences." — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1

🇨🇳 China

China called for immediate cessation of military activities and respect for Iranian sovereignty, aligning with Russia to convene the UN Security Council. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

The UK initially said it was monitoring closely and called Iran's nuclear program "an existential threat to regional security." By Mar 7, PM Starmer approved use of British bases for limited strikes on Iran's missile capabilities, reversing the defensive-only posture. HMS Diamond (Type 45) remains forward-deployed. — Sources: UK FCDO (Tier‑1 gov't claim), UK MoD (Tier‑1), Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1–7

🇫🇷 France & 🇩🇪 Germany

France and Germany called for maximum restraint and condemned Iranian retaliation on Gulf states as unacceptable. The EU convened an emergency foreign affairs council; evacuations of nationals began. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1–2

⚓ France deploys Charles de Gaulle (R91) carrier group — Mar 1, 2026

France is deploying aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle (R91) to the Eastern Mediterranean for maritime security and humanitarian protection. The air wing includes Rafale M and E‑2C assets; escorts include Forbin, Provence, Alsace, and a Rubis‑class SSN. — Source: French MOD (Tier‑1), Mar 1

🇹🇷 Turkey ► NEW (Mar 4)

Turkey condemned the strikes as dangerous and destabilizing, offered mediation, closed its airspace to mission‑related overflights, and put air defenses on alert along the Iranian border. — Source: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1

Iranian ballistic missiles struck Turkish soil near İncirlik Air Base on Mar 4, marking the first direct Iranian strike on a NATO member. President Erdoğan condemned the attack; NATO invoked Article 4 consultations; İncirlik Air Base (host to U.S. B61 nuclear gravity bombs) placed on heightened alert. Turkey reserved its right of self-defense and recalled its ambassador to Tehran. — Source: Reuters, Mar 4, 2026; NATO Article 4 Consultations

Iranian drones struck Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan exclave — hitting an airport and landing near a school. Azerbaijan withdrew diplomats and promised a military response. 🇬🇧 UK — Base Use Approved ► MAR 7

🇮🇷 Iran — Leadership Transition Recognized Domestically, Watched Abroad

Iran's Assembly of Experts elevated Mojtaba Khamenei as new Supreme Leader following Ali Khamenei's death. The move drew IRGC pledges of loyalty and international scrutiny over escalation risks. Capitals signaled vigilance rather than recognition moves as fighting continued. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 8; BBC (Tier‑4), Mar 8

🇨🇦 Canada

Ottawa issued a calibrated, allied‑aligned response: support for preventing a nuclear‑armed Iran, calls for civilian protection, and no direct military engagement. — Source: Atlantic Council (Tier‑3), Mar 8

Energy & Shipping Markets Shock — Updated Mar 10

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely disrupted into day 10. Brent crude surged above $100–$110/barrel as insurers and operators avoid transit, with analysts calling it the biggest oil supply disruption in history. New assessments indicate ~17 million bpd of supply effectively curtailed as tanker traffic stalls and LNG flows are hit; Qatar declared force majeure on LNG exports, and Saudi Aramco's Ras Tanura faced closures due to attacks. The US offered naval escorts and potential insurance backstops to re‑open lanes, but risks from sustained Iranian drone activity persist. — Sources: Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 7–9; CNBC (Tier‑4; Rapidan Energy assessment), Mar 9; World Oil (Tier‑3; citing S&P Global), Mar 9

🌌 UN Security Council — Emergency Session

Russia and China called an emergency UN Security Council session within hours of the strikes. A draft resolution demanding "immediate cessation of hostilities" was vetoed by the US. The UK abstained; France voted in favor. The UN Secretary‑General warned this is "the most dangerous escalation in the Middle East in decades". — Source: UN (Tier‑1), Mar 1
