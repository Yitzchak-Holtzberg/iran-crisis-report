Iran: Strike Damage Assessment

Operation Epic Fury — confirmed and assessed damage to Iranian targets (Feb 28–Mar 22, 2026)
Critical
Mar 22 — Wide‑scale strikes reported across Iran; Natanz hit; IAEA investigating

The latest wave of strikes on Mar 22 targeted missile production and defence‑industrial sites across Iran. Regional reporting and Iranian state outlets described damage at the Natanz enrichment complex; the IAEA said it is investigating and reported no detected rise in radiation levels outside the facility. Iranian ballistic missiles struck towns near Dimona and Arad, causing dozens of injuries in southern Israel. CENTCOM and allied statements describe renewed, intense targeting of regime military infrastructure. — Source: IAEA; Reuters; Al Jazeera; New York Times, Mar 22

Jun 18 — Iran signs Islamabad Memorandum and agrees to IAEA access

Multiple outlets report that Iran's leadership signed the Islamabad Memorandum on Jun 18 to end hostilities and that Tehran agreed to invite IAEA inspectors as part of implementation steps; Iranian statements framed the agreement as a tactical pause while some regional actors and domestic critics expressed skepticism. — Reuters; AP; Jun 18–19

Jun 22 — Iran agrees to IAEA inspections as talks progress

Update (Jun 22): U.S. negotiators in Switzerland and Iranian representatives reported "very good" progress and said Iran agreed to allow IAEA inspectors to return to Iran as part of continuing implementation steps and follow‑on negotiations; mediators called for further verification work to translate the commitment into access on the ground. — AP; CNBC; CBS; Jun 22–23

Jun 26 — U.S. strikes Iranian missile, drone and radar sites after ship attack

U.S. Central Command said on Jun 26 that U.S. forces carried out strikes on Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in retaliation for the drone attack on a commercial cargo vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz; officials described the strikes as self‑defense and released video of the operation, a development that tested the durability of the Islamabad Memorandum and prompted renewed diplomatic activity. — AP; Reuters; Jun 26

Jun 27 — Additional strikes and Iranian statements

Update (Jun 27): U.S. Central Command said it conducted additional strikes on Iranian military targets after another reported attack on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials said they struck U.S. facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait; those claims were reported by international outlets but had not been independently verified when published. — Reuters; AP; NYT; Jun 27

Update (Mar 17): Kharg Island was reported struck by U.S. forces on Mar 14; satellite imagery later showed two Iranian tankers loading roughly 2.7 million barrels and multiple storage tanks at Kharg appearing intact (Mar 16). The U.S. is planning a naval escort coalition to protect commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz, though several countries have declined participation. Militia and proxy attacks continue, including reporting of a likely drone strike on a French base in Erbil that reportedly killed one French service member. The UN refugee agency estimates roughly 3.2 million displaced inside Iran as of mid‑March. — CNN; ISW; AP; Reuters; Mar 15–17

Update (Mar 20–21): Reports indicate Iran fired ballistic missiles at Diego Garcia; the base was reported not to have been hit. Reuters additionally reported the IRGC has rebuilt Hezbollah's military command structure with embedded Iranian officers to prepare for wider conflict. — Times of Israel; Reuters, Mar 20–21

Update (May 12): Reuters reported on May 12 that Saudi Arabia carried out covert strikes on Iranian soil, marking a further regionalization of the conflict and complicating mediation efforts. — Reuters; May 12

Update (Jun 2–3): Reuters, AP, and the New York Times report that Iran launched missiles and drones toward regional targets and U.S. positions on Jun 2–3; the attacks damaged Kuwait International Airport, killing at least one civilian and injuring dozens, while U.S. Central Command said it struck Iranian radar and drone command sites on Qeshm Island in self‑defense, prompting renewed disruption of mediation. — Reuters; AP; New York Times; Jun 2–3

Update (Jun 5–6): U.S. Central Command said it shot down multiple Iranian one‑way attack drones launched toward the Strait of Hormuz and struck coastal radar installations (including positions near Goruk and on Qeshm Island) in self‑defense, underscoring persistent threats to Gulf maritime traffic and further disrupting mediation efforts. — Reuters; AP; Jun 5–6

Update (Jun 25): On Jun 25 a vessel was struck near Oman in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting regional alarm and a temporary pause in IMO evacuation operations while navies and operators reassessed transits. — Reuters; AP; Jun 25

Update (Jun 26): U.S. Central Command said U.S. forces conducted strikes on Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in response to a drone attack on a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz; U.S. officials framed the strikes as self‑defense and released video of the strikes, raising fresh questions about the durability of the Islamabad Memorandum and escorted transits. — AP; Reuters; New York Times; Jun 26

Update (Jun 27): U.S. Central Command said U.S. forces conducted additional strikes on Iranian military sites on Jun 27 after another attack on a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian statements that their forces struck U.S. bases in Bahrain and Kuwait were reported by international outlets but have not been independently verified. — Reuters; AP; NYT; Jun 27

Update (Jun 29): Update (Jun 29): AP and other outlets report U.S. and Iranian sides announced a pause in recent strikes and that delegations or envoys would be dispatched to Doha for talks to discuss implementation of the memorandum and steps to stabilize Strait transits. — AP; Jun 29

Update (Jul 1–2): Qatari and Pakistani mediators hosted separate U.S. and Iranian meetings in Doha on Jul 1–2; hosts reported "positive progress" and said technical talks would continue as negotiators worked to translate the Islamabad Memorandum into implementation steps. — AP; NPR; Jul 1–2

Update (Jul 5): AP reported Iran's top officials and brothers of the country's new supreme leader attended funeral prayers for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei in Tehran as multi‑day ceremonies began; state media and officials used the events to project unity while negotiators paused direct talks during the funerals. — AP; Jul 5

Casualties — Updated Mar 22

Casualty counts remain contested. Field documentation groups report thousands killed in the first 10–14 days; Iranian government figures are lower. International bodies note broad discrepancies and limited access for independent verification. The U.S. investigation into the elementary‑school strike has intensified scrutiny of civilian harm. — Sources: Hengaw (field documentation), Mar 10; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 12; NBC (Tier‑4), Mar 12

UN Secretary‑General António Guterres said there are "reasonable grounds" to believe attacks on oil and gas facilities may amount to war crimes, intensifying calls for legal scrutiny and restraint. — DW; CNN, Mar 20

New displacement estimates: UNHCR estimates roughly 3.2 million people displaced inside the country since the start of large-scale strikes. A U.S. KC-135 tanker crashed in Iraq on Mar 12; the crew was killed. The broader U.S. casualty picture includes multiple service‑member deaths and wounded across the theater. — Sources: LA Times (Tier‑4, citing UNHCR), Mar 15; Aviation Week (Tier‑3), Mar 13

Mar 19–22 media reporting indicates Iran carried out executions tied to the January protests: BBC and CBS report three men were executed, with one report saying a teenager was among them, underscoring continuing internal repression amid the conflict. — BBC; CBS, Mar 19–22

Al Jazeera's day‑18 summary reported at least 1,444 killed and 18,551 injured in US‑Israeli attacks on Iran since Feb 28 (Mar 17). — Al Jazeera (regional broadcaster), Mar 17

Update (Jun 16): Reuters reported that Iran executed two individuals described as armed leaders in the January unrest, naming them Javad Zamani and Abolfazl Saedi according to the judiciary's Mizan outlet; human-rights groups expressed concern about continued use of capital punishment in protest‑related cases. — Reuters; Jun 16
