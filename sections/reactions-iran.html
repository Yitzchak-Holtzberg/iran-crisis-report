Iran: Strike Damage Assessment

Operation Epic Fury — confirmed and assessed damage to Iranian targets (Feb 28–Mar 22, 2026)
Critical
Mar 22 — Wide‑scale strikes reported across Iran; Natanz hit; IAEA investigating

The latest wave of strikes on Mar 22 targeted missile production and defence‑industrial sites across Iran. Regional reporting and Iranian state outlets described damage at the Natanz enrichment complex; the IAEA said it is investigating and reported no detected rise in radiation levels outside the facility. Iranian ballistic missiles struck towns near Dimona and Arad, causing dozens of injuries in southern Israel. CENTCOM and allied statements describe renewed, intense targeting of regime military infrastructure. — Source: IAEA; Reuters; Al Jazeera; New York Times, Mar 22

Apr 6 — Iran rejects ceasefire proposal, submits 10‑point conditions

Reuters reported that Iran rejected the mediators' ceasefire proposal on Apr 6 and conveyed its own 10‑point response via intermediaries, demanding lifting of sanctions and broader guarantees as preconditions. — Reuters, Apr 6

Apr 7 — Iran accepts temporary two‑week ceasefire

Following initial back‑and‑forth over terms, multiple outlets report Iran agreed on Apr 7 to a two‑week ceasefire with the United States, tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz; Tehran also forwarded conditions that will be part of ongoing diplomatic talks. — Reuters; AP; NPR; Apr 7

Mar 15 — UN refugee agency: Millions displaced inside Iran; humanitarian needs surge

The UN refugee agency and humanitarian observers report a major internal displacement crisis in Iran, with estimates of roughly 3.2 million people displaced inside the country since the start of large-scale strikes. Aid organisations warn of urgent needs for shelter, medical care, and safe corridors. — Source: Los Angeles Times (Tier‑4, citing UNHCR), Mar 15

May 12 — Iran threatens weapons‑grade enrichment if attacked

Reuters reported on May 12 that Iranian lawmakers and officials warned Tehran could accelerate enrichment to weapons‑grade levels if it were attacked or if talks collapsed, increasing nuclear escalation risks and complicating mediation efforts. — Reuters; May 12

May 18 — Europol disrupts IRGC online propaganda network

Europol announced coordinated action across 19 countries removing thousands of online accounts and links tied to the IRGC's propaganda and recruitment network, and said it had helped suspend the group's primary X account in the EU. — Europol / Euronews; May 18

Jun 1 — U.S. conducts self‑defense strikes; Iran reports retaliation and halts talks

U.S. Central Command said it carried out self‑defense strikes on Iranian radar and drone command sites (Goruk, Qeshm) over the weekend; Iran reported retaliatory actions; separately, reporting on Jun 1 indicates Iran said it was suspending talks with U.S. mediators after Israeli strikes in Lebanon, disrupting negotiations. — Reuters; Washington Post; NPR; Jun 1

Jun 2 — Kuwait airport struck; talks suspended

AP and Reuters reported that Iranian missile/drone strikes damaged Kuwait International Airport on Jun 2, killing at least one person and injuring dozens; Iranian state media said attacks targeted U.S. facilities, and Iranian officials said they were halting talks amid expanded hostilities. — AP; Reuters; Jun 2

Jun 5–6 — CENTCOM shoots down attack drones; strikes radar sites

U.S. Central Command said it shot down multiple Iranian one-way attack drones launched toward the Strait of Hormuz and struck Iranian coastal radar installations in Goruk and on Qeshm Island in self‑defense, marking renewed tactical exchanges that risk undermining the ceasefire. — Reuters; AP; Jun 5–6

Jun 7–8 — Iran fires missiles at Israel; Israel strikes back inside Iran

Reuters, the New York Times, and AP report that Iran launched ballistic missiles at Israel on Jun 7 and that Israel carried out strikes on military targets in Iran on Jun 7–8; the exchanges are described as the most serious breakdown of the April ceasefire and have prompted urgent diplomatic engagement to prevent wider escalation. — Reuters; NYT; AP; Jun 7–8

Casualties — Updated Mar 22

Casualty counts remain contested. Field documentation groups report thousands killed in the first 10–14 days; Iranian government figures are lower. International bodies note broad discrepancies and limited access for independent verification. The U.S. investigation into the elementary‑school strike has intensified scrutiny of civilian harm. — Sources: Hengaw (field documentation), Mar 10; Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 12; NBC (Tier‑4), Mar 12

UN Secretary‑General António Guterres said there are "reasonable grounds" to believe attacks on oil and gas facilities may amount to war crimes, intensifying calls for legal scrutiny and restraint. — DW; CNN, Mar 20

New displacement estimates: UNHCR estimates roughly 3.2 million internally displaced inside Iran. A U.S. KC-135 tanker crashed in Iraq on Mar 12; the crew was killed. The broader U.S. casualty picture includes multiple service‑member deaths and wounded across the theater. — Sources: LA Times (Tier‑4, citing UNHCR), Mar 15; Aviation Week (Tier‑3), Mar 13

Mar 19–22 media reporting indicates Iran carried out executions tied to the January protests: BBC and CBS report three men were executed, with one report saying a teenager was among them, underscoring continuing internal repression amid the conflict. — BBC; CBS, Mar 19–22
