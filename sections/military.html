Iran's Remaining Military Capability

Post-Operation Epic Fury: degraded, decapitated — but not disarmed
🔴 Operation "Epic Fury" — Day 2 (March 1, 2026)

U.S.-Israeli strikes entered a second consecutive day on March 1, targeting nuclear, ballistic-missile, and IRGC command-and-control infrastructure. Iran launched its retaliatory first salvo on Feb 28 — the IRGC designated its response "True Promise 4." Per Critical Threats/ISW, the authoritative open-source assessment: the initial barrages targeting Israel involved fewer than 100 ballistic missiles, while broader regional attacks (targeting US bases across Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, and Jordan) combined ballistic missiles and drones totaling "hundreds" of projectiles across all theaters. Iranian state media confirmed the death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei on March 1. Regional air defenses — Arrow 3, Iron Dome, Patriot, THAAD, and Aegis BMD — were activated simultaneously across all five theaters. Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz remain disrupted as oil trading houses suspended crude, fuel, and LNG transit. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera; Critical Threats/ISW Evening Special Report, Feb 28, 2026; Stars & Stripes, Mar 1, 2026; Army Recognition, Mar 1, 2026

🆕 Admiral Cooper CENTCOM Video Briefing — Day 5 Operational Update (Mar 4, 2026)

CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper released a video operational assessment on Mar 4 — the most comprehensive public accounting of the operation to date. Key figures: nearly 2,000 Iranian targets struck since operations began; 17 Iranian naval vessels destroyed, including an Iranian submarine; 50,000+ U.S. troops participating in the operation; Iran's air defenses "severely degraded" with hundreds of ballistic missiles, launchers, and drones eliminated. Cooper stated: "Many of you may remember the Shock and Awe strikes of 2003. The first 24 hours of this operation were nearly double the scale." On one-way drones: "These drones were originally an Iranian design. We took them back to America, made them better and fired them right back at Iran." Cooper's overall assessment: "Our combat power is building. Iran's is declining. My overall operational assessment is that we are ahead of our game plan." — Source: Admiral Brad Cooper, CENTCOM video statement / ABC News, Mar 4, 2026

⚠️ The Command Vacuum: Iran Without Khamenei

Khamenei's death removes the singular authority that has controlled Iran's nuclear trigger, proxy activation orders, and all strategic military decisions for 35 years. This is not merely a symbolic loss — it creates a genuine and dangerous command vacuum over a still-armed state with ballistic missiles and drones. Iran's naval forces have been declared destroyed by CENTCOM (Mar 3), but the remaining missile and drone arsenal — and the proxy network — still represent serious threats.

Military Risks: Decapitation Without Disarmament

  • Iran retains an estimated 1,500–2,500 ballistic missiles after cumulative first-wave expends and strike attrition — with no single confirmed authority now controlling their release. — Times of Israel / IDF assessment, Mar 1, 2026; JINSA 6/26 Update; Alma Research Feb 2026; The Telegraph, Mar 4, 2026; ROIC AI/Reuters, Mar 4, 2026
  • IRGC corps commanders may hold pre-delegated launch authority for second-wave ballistic salvos — a "dead hand" scenario where tactical commanders fire without political oversight
  • The Quds Force, absent central command, may activate terrorist sleeper cells globally under pre-standing contingency orders — no authorization needed if Khamenei is confirmed dead
  • Hezbollah, Houthis, and Iraqi PMF forces may escalate independently — the decapitation of the Quds Force removes the coordinating authority but not the proxy arsenals
  • Risk of competing IRGC factions forming separate power centers — creating unpredictable, uncoordinated decision-making loops with no diplomatic interlocutor
  • Iran's cyber units (APT33/APT34) operate autonomously and may launch pre-planned destructive attacks against Gulf financial systems and energy infrastructure without central authorization

Succession Under Fire: Who Controls Iran Now?

  • Assembly of Experts: Constitutionally empowered to elect a new Supreme Leader — but the body is fragmented, some members may be dead or unreachable, and it has never convened under active air strikes
  • Mojtaba Khamenei (son): Had been discreetly groomed as a potential successor but holds no formal military, clerical, or governmental authority — IRGC generals are unlikely to defer to him
  • IRGC General Command Council: The most powerful surviving military body — may assert de facto control over ballistic missiles and strategic assets regardless of formal succession processes
  • President Masoud Pezeshkian: Reformist president constitutionally subordinate to the Supreme Leader — elevated in interregnum, but commands no IRGC loyalty and has no control over the missile force
  • Expediency Council / Judiciary: Could attempt to convene an interim governance structure — but both lack the IRGC's weapons and the IRGC's willingness to follow civilian orders
  • The Hardliner-Reformist Fault Line: The succession fight between IRGC hardliners and regime reformists could fracture what remains of Iranian state cohesion at the worst possible moment
The Most Dangerous 72 Hours

The window between Khamenei's death and the emergence of any successor authority is assessed by defense analysts as the most dangerous phase of the conflict. Pre-delegated launch protocols may allow IRGC corps commanders to fire second-wave ballistic salvos without political authorization — potentially larger than the initial first-day salvo. US and Israeli military planners are almost certainly accelerating strikes against remaining launch infrastructure before any successor can consolidate control and either escalate further or negotiate a ceasefire. The absence of a recognized decision-maker on the Iranian side also complicates any US back-channel ceasefire outreach. Critically, Iran has been withholding its most advanced hypersonic-maneuvering missiles (Fattah-1) in reserve — whether a successor authority deploys these in a second wave is now the central strategic question. — Chosun, Mar 5, 2026; Defense analysts cited by ISW/Critical Threats

The cumulative effect of the June 2025 Israeli-US strikes and the Feb 28–Mar 1 Operation Epic Fury strikes has fundamentally altered Iran's military posture. Iran is no longer a pre-strike power deterring an attack — it is a post-strike state that has fired its first salvo and is now assessing what remains. The central military question is no longer whether Iran will retaliate, but what authority, and with what remaining arsenal, Iran can mount a second wave.

The China Factor

China has emerged as Iran's primary military supply chain. Despite sanctions, Chinese companies provided dual-use components for missile guidance, drone manufacturing, and radar systems throughout 2025–26. The "Pickaxe Mountain" deep centrifuge facility near Isfahan was reportedly built with Chinese tunneling expertise. Beijing's calculus: a weakened-but-surviving Iran keeps US military resources tied down in the Middle East — away from the Taiwan Strait. China's Feb 24 agreement to supply additional anti-ship missiles was confirmed days before the Feb 28 strikes. Whether China continues supply into an active wartime Iran remains a critical open question — and a major source of US-China tension.
