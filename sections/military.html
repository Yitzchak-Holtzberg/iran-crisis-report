Iran's Remaining Military Capability

After the June 2025 strikes: degraded but not destroyed

The June 2025 Israeli-US strikes dealt devastating blows to Iran's nuclear infrastructure and air defenses. But Iran retains significant conventional capabilities — and is rebuilding faster than many analysts expected, largely with Chinese technical assistance.

Degraded
  • Air defenses: Russian S-300 systems repositioned around Tehran, but radar components possibly damaged — effectiveness uncertain
  • Nuclear facilities: Natanz, Isfahan heavily damaged; centrifuge cascades reduced by ~60%
  • Air force: Aging fleet of F-4s, F-14s, MiG-29s — no match for US 5th-gen fighters
  • Command & control: Multiple IRGC senior commanders killed; communications compromised
Still Operational
  • Missiles: ~1,500 ballistic/cruise missiles, ~200 mobile launchers — hardened in dispersed mountain tunnels
  • Bavar-373: Indigenous long-range SAM system (Iran's S-300 equivalent) — unknown quantity deployed
  • Drones: Shahed-136/238 production accelerated — proven in Ukraine via Russia
  • IRGC Navy: Intact — the Strait of Hormuz force was not targeted in June strikes
  • Cyber: Offensive cyber capabilities remain fully operational

The China Factor

China has emerged as Iran's primary military supply chain. Despite sanctions, Chinese companies are providing dual-use components for missile guidance, drone manufacturing, and radar systems. The "Pickaxe Mountain" deep centrifuge facility near Isfahan was reportedly built with Chinese tunneling expertise. Beijing's calculus: a weakened-but-surviving Iran keeps US military resources tied down in the Middle East — away from the Taiwan Strait.

⚡ Iran's Asymmetric Response Playbook

Iran has spent 40 years developing an asymmetric deterrence doctrine specifically designed to impose devastating costs on a superior conventional force. If attacked, Iran would not fight the US military head-to-head — it would trigger a cascading, multi-front response designed to make the cost of war intolerable.

The Missile Threat

  • Shahab-3: Range 2,000km — reaches Israel, Saudi Arabia, US Gulf bases
  • Emad/Ghadr: Precision-guided variants; accuracy improved post-2020
  • Kheibar Shekan: Hypersonic-maneuvering variant; can potentially evade Patriot/THAAD
  • Fattah-1: Claimed hypersonic glide vehicle — range 1,400km; limited inventory
  • ~200 mobile TEL launchers dispersed in mountain tunnels — pre-positioned before any strike

The Cyber Threat

  • APT33 / Elfin: IRGC-linked group with proven capability against Saudi Aramco (Shamoon 2012), US banks, Israeli infrastructure
  • APT34 / OilRig: Specializes in Gulf state energy sector penetration — has pre-positioned access
  • Iranian hackers have demonstrated SCADA/ICS attacks against water treatment and power facilities
  • Destructive wiper malware pre-positioned in adversary networks — similar to Russia's NotPetya playbook
  • An attack on Gulf financial clearing systems (SWIFT equivalents) would cause immediate global economic disruption
The Carrier Threat — Iran's Anti-Access/Area Denial

Iran has specifically invested in anti-ship capabilities designed to threaten US carriers — the platforms that would be central to any US strike. The Noor anti-ship missile (Chinese C-802 derivative, range 170km), the Qader (range 200km), and the Khalij Fars (anti-ship ballistic missile, range 300km) have all been demonstrated in live-fire drills. The IRGC Navy's swarm tactics — hundreds of fast-attack boats simultaneously targeting a carrier — are specifically designed to overwhelm close-in weapons systems. China's Feb 24 agreement to supply additional anti-ship missiles directly increases this threat. No US carrier has been tested in combat against this threat profile since the Cold War. The Pentagon acknowledges carriers may need to operate at greater stand-off distances — reducing their strike effectiveness.

💥 Iran's Retaliation — Who Was Attacked & How (Feb 28)

Within hours of the Operation Epic Fury strikes, Iran activated its pre-planned asymmetric retaliation doctrine. The response was simultaneous and multi-front — not limited to a single trajectory or weapon type.

Summary — Confirmed Retaliation (Feb 28)

Iran fired multiple ballistic missile barrages toward Israel and toward US military installations across four Gulf nations. Explosions were reported in Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. Air defense systems — Patriot, THAAD, and Arrow 3 — were activated. The full extent of damage and interception rates are not yet confirmed.

Targets Struck / Targeted

  • Israel — Cities & Military Sites
    Ballistic missile barrage toward Israeli cities and IDF installations. Arrow 3 and Iron Dome active; interceptions underway. Explosions heard. (Confirmed — BBC, Feb 28)
  • Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base
    CENTCOM forward HQ and primary US air operations hub targeted. Explosions reported. ~10,000 US personnel based here. (Reported — BBC, Feb 28; damage unconfirmed)
  • Bahrain — NSA Bahrain / US 5th Fleet HQ
    Home of US Naval Forces Central Command. Explosions reported in Bahrain. Air defenses activated. (Reported — BBC, Feb 28; damage unconfirmed)
  • Saudi Arabia — Prince Sultan Air Base
    Major US air hub housing F-16s, E-3 AWACS, and KC-135 tankers. Explosions in Saudi Arabia reported. (Reported — BBC, Feb 28; damage unconfirmed)
  • UAE — Al Dhafra Air Base
    F-22 Raptors and ISR drones based here. Explosions in UAE reported; air defenses activated. (Reported — BBC, Feb 28; damage unconfirmed)

Weapons Used

  • Shahab-3 / Ghadr-110
    Range: ~2,000 km. Liquid-fueled. Reaches all Gulf bases and Israel. Pre-positioned in mountain tunnels; fired from mobile TEL launchers.
  • Emad / Kheibar Shekan
    Precision-guided re-entry vehicle (MARV). Range 1,700–2,000 km. Designed to evade Patriot radar with terminal maneuvering.
  • Zolfaghar / Dezful
    Solid-fuel ballistic missile. Range 700–1,000 km. Can be repositioned quickly; confirmed in prior Iran barrages (2024 Israel strikes).
  • Shahed-136 / Shahed-238 Drones
    Loitering munitions ("kamikaze drones"). Used extensively in prior Iran attacks on Gulf/Israel. Status in this attack: unconfirmed.
  • Houthi Coordination (Yemen)
    Concurrent Houthi missile/drone attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure and Red Sea shipping reported; directly coordinated with Iran IRGC Quds Force. Unconfirmed independently.

Air Defense Responses

  • Israel: Arrow 3 (exo-atmospheric), Iron Dome (short-range), David's Sling (medium-range) — all activated
  • Qatar / UAE / Saudi: Patriot PAC-3 batteries on full alert; THAAD at Al Udeid activated
  • US Navy: Aegis BMD destroyers in Arabian Sea, Gulf, and Mediterranean engaged in missile defense track
