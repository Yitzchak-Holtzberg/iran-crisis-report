INTELLIGENCE — IRAN MOIS/VAJA REPORTED TO HAVE REACHED OUT TO US COUNTERPARTS ? UNCONFIRMED — TIER-5 ONLY
Multiple reports from opposition-aligned and regional outlets indicate that elements of Iran’s Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS/VAJA) — the civilian intelligence service, distinct from IRGC Intelligence — have made back-channel contact with US and European intelligence counterparts, reportedly to signal interest in ceasefire terms and nuclear verification arrangements independently of the frozen Oman channel. Iran International (Mar 3–4) and Al Arabiya (Mar 4) have reported the outreach; no Tier 1–2 outlet (Reuters, AP, AFP) has confirmed. The reports are consistent with the broader Vienna talks trajectory (Al Jazeera / IAEA, Mar 4) and with the pattern of mixed Iranian signals — Larijani’s public rejection of negotiations (Al Jazeera, Mar 3) alongside back-channel activity. Key ambiguity: it is unclear whether the contact represents MOIS institutional policy approved by the Mojtaba junta, a factional move by MOIS elements not aligned with the IRGC succession, or opposition-aligned fabrication. What confirmation would look like: a Tier 1–2 report (Reuters / AP) corroborating intelligence-to-intelligence contact; a US administration official confirming “back-channel communications are ongoing”; or a Vienna talks agenda including items only MOIS (not IAEA or diplomatic actors) could have tabled. This differs from the separate IRGC officer → CIA defection-channel report (Iran International, Tier-5, also unconfirmed) — that involves an individual defector, not the institutional intelligence service. — Sources: Iran International (Tier-5, opposition-aligned), Mar 3–4; Al Arabiya, Mar 4 — Tier-5 framing required; not confirmed by CENTCOM, State Dept, Reuters, or AP as of {{date}}

NUCLEAR — DEEP BUNKER BDA: "PICKAXE MOUNTAIN" & FORDOW UNDERGROUND HALLS BDA PENDING
Whether US GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrators successfully penetrated and destroyed the "Pickaxe Mountain" deep facility and Fordow's underground centrifuge halls remains unconfirmed. These facilities are estimated at 80–100m depth — at or beyond the weapon's effective penetration range. Iran acknowledged explosions at Fordow and Natanz on Mar 2, but described them as attacks on "military infrastructure." US officials claim "significant damage" but no independent BDA has been released. Open-source satellite imagery has been ordered but analysis is not yet complete. Commercial imaging companies confirm multiple crater signatures near known entry shafts, but subsurface penetration depth and hall destruction are not assessable from surface imagery alone. The most critical intelligence question of the campaign — whether Iran's nuclear programme was set back by months, years, or permanently — remains unanswered. — Sources: CSIS analysis; Reuters, Al Jazeera, Mar 2; BDA pending
CIVILIAN — SCHOOL STRIKE DEATH TOLL: MINAB & MULTIPLE CITIES FIGURE DISPUTED
Al Jazeera reported schools struck in Iran with 80+ killed as of Feb 28; a mass funeral in Minab was attended by thousands on Mar 3, confirming at minimum a significant casualty event involving schoolchildren. However, the total civilian death toll across all struck locations remains officially unconfirmed by US CENTCOM or independent on-the-ground reporting. Iranian government has not released an official figure. Human rights groups report receiving casualty reports from multiple provinces but cannot verify independently. Source access inside Iran is severely restricted, communications are disrupted, and foreign journalists have been barred or expelled. The figure of 555 total killed (Al Jazeera, Mar 2) includes combatant and civilian deaths and has not been corroborated by US or Israeli sources. — Sources: Al Jazeera, Feb 28 & Mar 2–3; mass funeral: Al Jazeera, Mar 3 — Figures disputed
IRAN'S CLAIM — 500 US-LINKED SITES STRUCK ACROSS REGION NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED
Iranian state authorities claimed retaliation has targeted approximately 500 sites linked to US interests across the region including bases, embassies, and Gulf infrastructure. The figure is extraordinary and has not been corroborated by independent assessment. US CENTCOM, Gulf state governments, and international media have confirmed discrete incidents — the embassy drone strike in Riyadh, tanker attacks near Hormuz, the Saudi refinery hit — but a count of ~500 strike sites is inconsistent with independently verified reports. The claim appears to conflate separate incidents, include proxy attacks by Iraqi militias and Houthi forces, and possibly overstate targeting. — Sources: Iran International (opposition-aligned), Mar 1; not corroborated by CENTCOM, ISW, or regional governments

Given Kharg's strategic significance, any confirmed strike would represent the most severe economic blow of the campaign, potentially cutting Iranian oil revenue by 90% and worsening global supply disruption. The absence of official confirmation is notable — both US and Israeli officials typically tout major economic target hits. — Sources: Newsweek, Mar 1; CENTCOM silent; commercial imagery pending

NUCLEAR — IRAN DENIES CIVILIAN NUCLEAR PROGRAMME TARGETED OR DESTROYED
Iranian officials acknowledged explosions at Fordow and Natanz on Mar 2 but officially characterise the strikes as hitting "military infrastructure" only — not Iran's civilian nuclear programme. This framing is contested: both Fordow and Natanz are IAEA-safeguarded civilian enrichment facilities under the NPT. Iran's position serves a dual purpose: domestically, it avoids admitting the nuclear programme was set back; internationally, it preserves legal standing under the NPT. US and Israeli officials describe the sites as weapons-relevant dual-use facilities. Independent verification of whether centrifuge enrichment capability has been destroyed is not possible without IAEA access, which Iran has not granted. — Status: Iranian claim contested by US, Israel, IAEA; independent verification blocked
NUCLEAR — RADIATION RELEASE FROM STRUCK ENRICHMENT SITES
Initial concerns that strikes on Fordow and Natanz might have caused a radiological release have not been borne out by current IAEA assessments. The watchdog stated that buildings at Natanz were damaged but "no radiological consequence [is] expected" based on available information. The IAEA is seeking access to both sites to complete verification; independent regional sensor data remains limited. — Sources: IAEA statement; Al Jazeera, Mar 3
LEADERSHIP — ASSEMBLY OF EXPERTS NAMES MOJTABA KHAMENEI NEW SUPREME LEADER LEGITIMACY CONTESTED
A partial session of the Assembly of Experts, convened under IRGC security escort, voted to name Mojtaba Khamenei as the new Supreme Leader on Mar 3, ending his 72-hour public absence. The vote is disputed on multiple grounds: Mojtaba holds no senior clerical rank or velayat-e faqih credentials; the quorum and independence of the session cannot be verified; numerous members were unreachable. Opposition figures including Reza Pahlavi and reformist clerics abroad have declared the appointment illegitimate. IRGC Commander Salami pledged immediate loyalty; the US and EU have not recognised the appointment. — Sources: Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera, Mar 3 — Appointment confirmed; legitimacy contested
LEADERSHIP — IRAN'S NEW LEADERSHIP COUNCIL LEGITIMACY CONTESTED
Following Khamenei's death, President Pezeshkian announced a three-man leadership council "has begun its work" on Mar 3, invoking constitutional succession procedures (Article 111). However, the mechanism's legitimacy is contested by multiple parties: the Assembly of Experts — the body constitutionally mandated to select a new Supreme Leader — has not publicly convened, with many members unreachable due to the active military situation and communications disruption. Iranian opposition figures abroad, including Reza Pahlavi, have rejected the council as an IRGC-backed manoeuvre. Several reformist clerics described the council as a caretaker mechanism only, not a permanent transfer of authority. The council's actual command authority over IRGC units in the field remains unclear. — Sources: AP, Mar 3; Reuters, Al Jazeera, Mar 3 — Legitimacy disputed
MARITIME — HORMUZ CLOSURE: IRGC CLAIM vs. OPERATIONAL REALITY
IRGC Commander publicly declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed" on Mar 2, with a threat to "set on fire" any ship attempting to pass. This declaration is contested in practice: the Strait remains technically navigable, with some vessels transiting under military escort or at high speed. The US Navy's 5th Fleet stated it "will not allow" Hormuz to be blocked and is conducting freedom-of-navigation patrols. While ~150 vessels are stranded and insurers have withdrawn cover, the strait is not physically obstructed. Iran's actual capability to close Hormuz to all traffic — as distinct from attacking individual vessels — is disputed by US naval analysts. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 2; USN 5th Fleet statement, Mar 2–3; maritime reality contested
TOTAL CASUALTY FIGURES — ALL SIDES DISPUTING NUMBERS
All sides are publishing divergent casualty figures for the conflict so far. Al Jazeera reported 555 total killed (Mar 2); Iran has not released an official figure and state media presents fragmentary, lower counts. US CENTCOM confirmed 6 US service members killed (CNBC/CBS, Mar 2) but has not addressed Iranian or civilian totals. Israel has not released its own figures. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs stated it cannot verify casualty data "due to access restrictions and the fog of war." Human rights groups (HRANA, Iran International) are compiling reports but note severe information blackout conditions inside Iran. All figures should be treated as preliminary estimates subject to significant revision. — Status: All reported figures disputed; OCHA cannot verify; independent access denied
