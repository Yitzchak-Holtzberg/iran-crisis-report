The Strait of Hormuz: Oil's Chokepoint

20 million barrels per day — one-fifth of global supply — passes through a 21-mile-wide strait Iran threatens to close
Strait Status (Mar 29): Partial reopening underway; first convoy transited Mar 27

Biggest supply disruption in history. The first US Navy-escorted convoy of 12 tankers transited Hormuz on Mar 27 without incident; Iran’s FM stated Iran would not fire on neutral-flagged commercial vessels. US Navy MCM vessels detonated two influence mines Mar 25–26; mine-clearing ongoing. Brent crude fell ~12% from peak. ~320 tankers still stranded (down from 480+ peak). War-risk insurance premiums easing but not fully restored. Full commercial traffic expected to take 3–6 more weeks if talks hold. — Sources: Bloomberg, Mar 27; USNI News, Mar 26; Reuters, Mar 27–28

Maritime Operations: Mine-Clearing Active; Second Convoy Forming

CENTCOM struck 50+ Iranian warships; Iranian navy operationally neutralized. US Navy MCM vessels have begun systematic mine-clearing in western Hormuz approaches; two influence mines safely detonated Mar 25–26. Three US carrier strike groups in region. A second convoy of tankers is forming for transit in coming days. Coastal ASCMs and remaining mine threat require continued caution. — Sources: USNI News, Mar 26; Reuters, Mar 27; CENTCOM briefing, Mar 27

The Strait of Hormuz is the single most important energy chokepoint on Earth. If Iran follows through on Khamenei's threats to close it — or if conflict disrupts shipping — the consequences would be immediate and global.

Who Depends on Hormuz

  • India: 50%+ of crude imports transit the Strait
  • Japan & South Korea: 70-80% of oil imports
  • China: 40% of crude — but also Iran's top buyer
  • EU: ~15% of gas imports (Qatari LNG)
  • Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Iraq: Nearly all exports

Remaining Hormuz Threats (Post Mar 3)

  • IRGC Navy surface fleet — status disputed: Iranian fast-attack craft and coastal units assessed degraded by strikes; blanket claims of total destruction are unconfirmed
  • Sea mines: Contact/influence mines pose the primary ongoing risk to transit; MCM activity reported but not officially detailed
  • Coastal anti-ship missiles: C-802/Noor/Qader batteries along the Iranian littoral — some may remain operational
  • Precedent: 2019 tanker attacks (“limpet mines”) — residual asymmetric capability cannot be fully ruled out
Economic Impact

Goldman Sachs pre-war estimate: full Hormuz closure pushes oil above $100 within days, triggering global recession. That scenario is now reality — Brent above $100 for 10+ days. Every $10/barrel increase costs the US economy ~$70B annually.
