Inside Iran: A Regime Under Siege

Seven converging crises threatening the Islamic Republic
Critical

☠ Crisis 1: The January Massacre

What began as economic protests on December 28, 2025 — driven by 40%+ general inflation and 70% food price inflation — escalated into the largest anti-government uprising in the Islamic Republic's history. On January 8-9, 2026, Khamenei ordered security forces to fire on protesters with live ammunition, tear gas, and prohibited weapons. What followed was the deadliest two-day protest massacre in modern history.

Death toll estimates vary enormously: the Iranian government acknowledged 3,177 deaths; HRANA documented 7,007 confirmed deaths (6,488 protesters including 236 children, 76 non-participating civilians, and 207 government/security forces); Iran International's investigation found evidence of 36,500+ killed, including 209 government-affiliated personnel.

HRANA "Crimson Winter" Report — Feb 23, 2026
HRANA's landmark 50-day report, based on 143,000+ verified reports, documents protests across 203 cities, 682 sites in all 31 provinces. Over 25,000 civilians and ~5,000 security personnel were injured. Detentions number in the tens of thousands with many still unaccounted for. HRANA methodology reports only confirmed minimum cases — the real toll is almost certainly higher. Tehran province accounts for 1,588 confirmed deaths alone. — hra-iran.org, Feb 23
Amnesty International Alert
At least 30 people, including several children, are at risk of execution in connection with the uprising. Several were under 18 at the time. Torture methods documented include: threats of sexual assault, mock executions, beatings with cables, burning with irons, and nail extraction.

✊ Crisis 2: The Student Uprising (Feb 21-26, Day 6)

As universities reopened, students organized 40-day memorial protests for those killed — a deeply significant tradition in Shia Islam marking the end of mourning. Starting Feb. 21, protests erupted at University of Tehran, Sharif University of Technology, Amirkabir University, Shahid Beheshti University, and campuses in Isfahan and Mashhad — now spreading to all 31 provinces.

By day five (today), demonstrations continue despite the regime's "red line" warnings, with class boycotts and campus occupations. Students chant "Death to the dictator," "Woman, life, freedom," and — significantly — chants calling for Reza Pahlavi to replace Khamenei. On Feb. 24, the Sharif University of Technology website was hacked, its name reverted to pre-revolution "Aryamehr University." A separate cyberattack sent 50,000 Iranians a text message: "To the oppressed people of Iran: the US president is a man of action, you will soon see." Iran's cyberpolice is investigating. Government spokesperson Mohajerani confirmed many universities remain closed, with some already moving classes fully online for the rest of the Iranian calendar year (ending March 20).

— Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, attempting to split the movement

📈 Crisis 3: Economic Freefall

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent admitted Washington deliberately engineered a dollar shortage to trigger runaway inflation. Testifying before the Senate Banking Committee, Bessent described the trigger event explicitly: "It came to a swift and, I would say, grand culmination in December, when one of the largest banks in Iran went under. There was a run on the bank. The central bank had to print money. The Iranian currency went into free fall, inflation exploded and hence we have seen the Iranian people out on the street." The rial has collapsed from 42,000 to over 1.65 million per dollar — effectively rendering purchasing power non-existent for imports.

Key economic indicators:

  • General inflation: 48.6% (October 2025) — likely higher now
  • Food price inflation: ~70%
  • IRGC controls an estimated 60-70% of the economy — profits insulated from suffering
  • EU designated entire IRGC a terrorist organization Feb 19 (EU Council formal decision — aligning with US 2019, Canada 2024); Iran retaliated by labeling European armies terrorist groups; UK sanctioned 10 officials including Interior Minister on Feb 2

The Patronage Crisis

The IRGC's loyalty has always been sustained by economic patronage — access to smuggling networks, construction contracts, import monopolies. With oil revenue collapsing and sanctions tightening, the regime can no longer afford to buy loyalty. This is why the IRGC power struggle (below) has become existential: it's not just about ideology, it's about shrinking revenue to divide among competing factions.

🔒 Crisis 4: The Digital Iron Curtain

On January 8, Iran imposed a near-total internet shutdown — the longest ever recorded in the country — cutting off 92 million citizens from the global web. Even phone and text messaging were restricted.

Although partially relaxed on January 28, the regime is now building something far more sinister: a two-tier internet system where only security-vetted elites receive global access, while 90 million citizens are locked inside a state-controlled intranet. Government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani confirmed international access won't be restored until at least late March.

Foreign Policy — Feb 24, 2026
"The regime is testing a two-tier internet where access becomes a vetted privilege... building a system that grants web access only to security-vetted elites, while locking 90 million citizens inside an intranet."

Georgia Tech researchers confirmed the blackout extended to Starlink satellite terminals, which Iran has been jamming. The shutdown is simultaneously concealing ongoing atrocities and destroying the economy — Iranian businesses report catastrophic losses from weeks without connectivity.

CIA Targets Iranian Insiders — Feb 24-25, 2026
The CIA released a Farsi-language video on X, Instagram, and YouTube offering detailed instructions for Iranian insiders to make secure contact: use a paid VPN registered outside Iran/Russia/China, a disposable device, private/incognito browsing, and access a dedicated Tor (.onion) portal. CIA Director Ratcliffe noted previous campaigns targeting Russian and Chinese speakers had yielded actionable results. Iran condemned the campaign as open interference. In a related development, Iranian authorities seized Starlink equipment found in a diplomat's luggage. — Sources: CNBC, Iran Int'l, US News, Feb 25

🇧🇭 Crisis 5: The Ethnic Periphery Erupts

While students protest in Tehran, Iran's ethnic minorities have borne the most brutal repression. The regime deployed heavy machine guns, armored vehicles, tanks, helicopter gunships in Kurdish, Baloch, and Arab-majority regions — a dramatically heavier response than in Persian cities.

Kurdistan

2,000+ Kurdish citizens arrested. Families report torture: threats of sexual assault, mock executions, beatings with cables, burning with irons, nail extraction. IRGC and Basij conducting warrantless raids and mass detentions.

Sistan-Baluchestan

Heavy military deployment with reports of armored vehicles and helicopter gunships used against civilians. The Baloch community, already among Iran's most marginalized, faces the most militarized crackdown.

Khuzestan (Arab)

Ahwaz Arabs face heavy artillery and military-grade suppression. The oil-rich province has long been a site of ethnic grievance, with communities demanding linguistic and cultural rights.

Azerbaijan (Turkish)

Ethnic Azeri Turks — Iran's largest minority — have joined the broader protest movement. The Congress of Nationalities of Federal Iran now represents all minority groups united against the regime.

💧 Crisis 6: The Water Catastrophe

Iran faces a water crisis of existential proportions that predates the current political upheaval — but now accelerates it. Decades of IRGC-directed dam-building, agricultural mismanagement, and climate change have turned one of the ancient world's most water-rich civilizations into a country running dry.

Lake Urmia, once the Middle East's largest lake (5,200 km²) and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, has shrunk to less than 10% of its former volume. Its exposed lakebed now creates toxic salt and dust storms carrying heavy metals across northwestern Iran, causing respiratory disease, crop failure, and mass displacement.

Root Cause: IRGC Dam Policy

The IRGC and its affiliated companies built 600+ dams across Iran — many without environmental review — diverting rivers that fed lakes and wetlands. The Zayandeh River that sustained Isfahan (pop. 2M) now runs dry for months each year. Farmers in Khuzestan (Crisis 5's Arab heartland) have seen the Karun River — Iran's largest — reduced to a trickle, fueling both the ethnic and water crises simultaneously.

Tehran itself (pop. 9M metro) faces acute water rationing. The Karaj Dam reservoir is at historic lows. Officials have warned of a "Day Zero" scenario — the point at which municipal taps stop flowing — as early as summer 2026. Meanwhile, Iranian agriculture consumes 92% of available water, much of it for IRGC-controlled pistachio and saffron export farms that prioritize foreign currency over domestic food supply.

The water crisis is now inseparable from the protest movement. The December 2025 economic protests were partly driven by water shortages in Isfahan, Khuzestan, and Sistan-Baluchestan. When the regime shut off internet access in January, it also shut off the water-monitoring apps that farmers relied on, leading to further crop losses.

🕵 Crisis 7: Axis of Resistance Unraveling

Iran's carefully built "Axis of Resistance" — its network of proxy forces across the Middle East — has suffered catastrophic losses since mid-2025. What was once Tehran's primary tool of asymmetric power projection is now fractured, depleted, and increasingly unreliable.

Hezbollah — Decimated

Hassan Nasrallah killed (Sep 2025) along with most of the senior command council. Israel's June-August 2025 twelve-day campaign destroyed 60%+ of Hezbollah's long-range missile arsenal. The Syria supply route — Iran's land bridge — has been severed since the fall of Assad in Dec 2024. Hezbollah is now a regional militia, not a strategic deterrent.

Houthis — Restructuring

Yemen's Ansar Allah has proven the most resilient proxy, still launching anti-shipping missiles into the Red Sea. But US/UK strikes since Jan 2024 have degraded their command infrastructure. Increasingly operating independently from Tehran — a sign of weakening control, not strength.

Iraqi PMF / Kataib Hezbollah

Iraqi Shia militias remain Iran's most intact proxy force. Kataib Hezbollah issued a Jan 2026 statement calling for "martyrdom operations" against US forces in Iraq and Syria. However, the Iraqi government is increasingly distancing itself, and US strikes on PMF positions have degraded coordination.

Syria — Collapsed

The fall of Assad (Dec 2024) eliminated Iran's most critical regional foothold. Syrian proxies — Liwa Fatemiyoun, NDF units, IRGC advisors — have been expelled or disbanded. The "land bridge" from Tehran to Beirut no longer exists. Iran lost billions in infrastructure investment overnight.

Strategic Implication

The collapse of the Axis of Resistance means Iran's primary deterrence strategy has failed. Tehran built this network specifically to threaten retaliation against US and Israeli targets without direct state-to-state warfare. With Hezbollah's leadership wiped out, Syria lost, and the land bridge severed, Iran must now either escalate to direct confrontation (which its degraded military cannot sustain) or accept a significantly weakened negotiating position in Geneva.

⚔ The IRGC Power Struggle

The severe repression has deepened internal rifts within Iran's security structure. Reports indicate growing moral and psychological strain on some Basij and provincial IRGC units — soldiers ordered to fire on their own people.

A recent IRGC command reshuffle, combined with public attacks among political factions and repeated calls for "unity," points to a governing system increasingly unable to maintain its own cohesion. The convergence of:

  • Internal IRGC command changes
  • Public factional infighting between hardliners and pragmatists
  • External military threat from the US
  • Economic collapse eroding the patronage networks that keep the regime loyal

...creates what analysts at the National Interest describe as a potential "Day After Khamenei" scenario where the 86-year-old Supreme Leader's inevitable succession becomes an IRGC power struggle rather than a democratic transition.
