Crisis 2: The Succession Crisis

Khamenei Confirmed Dead — Mar 1–2, 2026 (confirmation reported Mar 8)

Iranian state media and officials confirmed the death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei during US‑Israeli strikes on a command bunker in Tehran. Israeli officials publicly claimed responsibility. NBC News reported members of his family were also killed. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, NBC News, Mar 1–8

Mojtaba Khamenei Named New Supreme Leader — Legitimacy Contested (named Mar 8)

A partial session of the Assembly of Experts, convened under IRGC security escort, voted to name Mojtaba Khamenei — son of the late Ayatollah — as the new Supreme Leader. The appointment is deeply contested: Mojtaba holds no senior clerical credentials. Opposition figures, reformist clerics, and international observers have rejected the session's legitimacy. IRGC Commander Salami immediately pledged loyalty. The US and EU have not recognised the appointment. Israel publicly warned it may target anyone who seeks to consolidate a successor leadership. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, Politico, Mar 3–8

President Pezeshkian announced a three-man leadership council (Ayatollah Arafi, Pezeshkian, Chief Justice Mohseni‑Ejei) invoking constitutional succession. The council is already divided: Pezeshkian (pragmatist, may seek ceasefire) vs Mohseni‑Ejei and the IRGC (total mobilization). Ali Larijani, newly appointed head of the Supreme National Security Council, has emerged as a potential stabilizing figure.

Update (Mar 15): Mojtaba Khamenei issued wartime messages that remain contested; U.S. officials — including public remarks attributed to Defense Department figures — have suggested Mojtaba may have been wounded, a claim reported by U.S. media that remains unconfirmed in independent reporting. U.S. authorities have taken additional measures targeting senior Iranian officials: the U.S. State Department announced rewards of up to $10 million for information on senior Iranian figures, including Mojtaba Khamenei and senior IRGC officials. — Reuters; AP; CBS News; KSL; Mar 12–15

Crisis 4: The Uprising — 190 Cities and Counting

Three overlapping waves of protest have merged into the largest sustained challenge to the Islamic Republic in its history:

The January Massacre

Economic protests starting Dec 28 escalated when Khamenei ordered live fire on Jan 8–9. HRANA documented 7,007 confirmed deaths; Iran International found evidence of 36,500+ killed. Protests spread to 203 cities, all 31 provinces. At least 30 people, including children, face execution for participation.

The Student Uprising

Universities organised 40-day Shia memorial protests starting Feb 21. Now in its third week across all 31 provinces. 410+ detained. Students chant "Death to the dictator," "Woman, life, freedom," and — significantly — chants calling for Reza Pahlavi. Khamenei's death sparked street celebrations despite active strikes.

The Ethnic Periphery

Kurdish, Baloch, Arab, and Azeri minorities face the heaviest military repression — heavy machine guns, armored vehicles, helicopter gunships. Over 2,000 Kurdish citizens arrested with documented torture. The Congress of Nationalities of Federal Iran now unites all minority groups.

War-Era Status

Opposition sources reported the uprising spread to ~190 cities before the air campaign began. Large-scale organized protests have largely been suppressed under wartime conditions — the militarized crackdown and active bombing make mass demonstrations impractical. However, the underlying grievances remain and could reignite once strikes end.

"We will speak with the protesters but... rioters should be put in their place."
— the late Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, attempting to split the movement before his death
Amnesty International Alert
At least 30 people, including several children, are at risk of execution in connection with the January uprising. Several were under 18 at the time. Torture methods documented include: threats of sexual assault, mock executions, beatings with cables, burning with irons, and nail extraction.

Update (Mar 15): Iranian authorities have publicly warned against renewed anti‑establishment protests and increased Basij and police activity has been reported in major cities. Reporting by Reuters and the BBC continues to suggest that external bombing alone is unlikely to topple the clerical establishment absent broader elite fractures. — BBC; Reuters, Mar 12–15

Crisis 6: Regional Escalation — The War Spreads

The conflict has expanded well beyond Iran's borders. Iranian drones struck Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan exclave (airport and near a school, Mar 5) — Azerbaijan withdrew diplomats and promised a military response. A stray missile struck near İncirlik Air Base in Turkey (Mar 4), prompting NATO Article 4 consultations. An Iranian drone hit a Bahrain desalination plant (Mar 8), targeting civilian water infrastructure. Seven U.S. service members have now died in the conflict; six U.S. Army Reserve soldiers were killed by an Iranian drone strike on a makeshift operations center in Port Shuaiba, Kuwait.
