The Nuclear Question and the Search for an Off-Ramp

The dominant fact is not a known bomb or a solved program. It is the loss of verified knowledge about material, activity, and damaged sites.
What the IAEA can no longer establish

In its June safeguards reporting, the IAEA said it could not provide a current accounting of the size, composition, or whereabouts of Iran's enriched-uranium stockpile and could not verify whether enrichment-related activity had stopped. Inspectors visited Bushehr in early June, but the agency had not regained the access needed to reconstruct the wider program. The last-known 440.9 kilograms enriched to 60 percent is a historical inventory, not a verified current total.

Pickaxe Mountain

The deep complex near Natanz was publicly described before the war as a replacement for a sabotaged centrifuge-assembly facility. It has not been inspected. Its construction and hardening make it important; they do not establish what, if anything, is currently inside it.

AP explainer, 22 July; Institute for Science and International Security.

The counter-proliferation dilemma

Strikes can delay, damage, and expose a program. They can also destroy monitoring equipment, scatter material, harden political commitment, and increase the value Tehran assigns to a latent or actual deterrent. The military and verification tracks therefore interact, but they are not substitutes for one another.

IISS; Chatham House.

How the diplomatic track changed

BEFORE 28 FEBRUARY
Indirect nuclear talks had resumed, but enrichment, sanctions sequencing, verification, and the scope of any agreement remained contested.
28 FEBRUARY–7 APRIL
The opening war displaced the negotiating process. Strikes damaged facilities and monitoring continuity while widening the agenda from nuclear restrictions to war termination and regional security.
APRIL–MID-JUNE
Mediators worked across separate but linked files: ceasefire, blockade, navigation, sanctions relief, enriched material, inspections, and regional forces.
17 JUNE MEMORANDUM
The United States and Iran accepted a temporary framework that reduced hostilities and enabled partial commercial recovery. Public reporting did not establish a complete, durable settlement of enrichment, inspections, sanctions, Hormuz governance, or the blockade.
JULY BREAKDOWN
Renewed vessel attacks, U.S. strikes, Iranian retaliation, and wider targeting exposed the memorandum's unresolved sequencing. Mediation continued without a publicly established replacement agreement.

What a durable arrangement would have to do

A workable off-ramp would need a monitored incident channel; explicit sequencing for strikes, blockade measures, and attacks on shipping; rapid IAEA access with a plan to reconstruct material accounting; commercial rules that insurers and shipowners can rely on; and a mechanism for handling violations without automatically returning to sustained war. A declaration or memorandum can begin that process. It cannot substitute for it.
