Gulf States: Iranian Retaliation Damage

Iran's ballistic missile and drone attacks on US-hosting Gulf nations — Feb 28–Mar 12, 2026
Active
Jun 25 — Vessel struck near Oman; regional alarm

On Jun 25 a vessel was struck near Oman in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting regional alarm and a temporary pause in IMO evacuation operations while navies and operators reassessed transits. — Reuters; AP; Jun 25

Jun 26 — U.S. strikes Iranian missile, drone and radar sites after ship attack

U.S. Central Command said on Jun 26 that it conducted strikes on Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in retaliation for the drone attack on a commercial vessel; the strikes heightened regional alarm and tested the Islamabad Memorandum's implementation. — AP; Reuters; Jun 26

Jun 27 — Additional U.S. strikes and Iranian claims raise regional tensions

Update (Jun 27): U.S. Central Command said U.S. forces carried out additional strikes on Iranian military targets after another attack on a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting fresh concern among Gulf states over the memorandum's durability. Iranian officials said they had struck U.S. military sites in Bahrain and Kuwait; those claims were reported but not independently verified at the time of reporting. — Reuters; AP; Jun 27

Jul 7 — Three commercial vessels struck; U.S. launches retaliatory strikes

Update (Jul 7–8): AP and Reuters report that three merchant vessels were struck near the Strait of Hormuz; U.S. Central Command said it launched a series of strikes on Iranian military and port facilities in response, and the U.S. revoked an authorization permitting some Iranian oil sales, prompting renewed diplomatic and military alarm in Gulf capitals. — AP; Reuters; Jul 7–8

Jun 29 — Pause in strikes; envoys head to Doha

Update (Jun 29): AP and other outlets report U.S. and Iranian sides signaled a pause in recent strikes and said envoys or delegations would be dispatched to Doha to discuss implementation and verification steps tied to Strait transits; Tehran later issued statements denying direct meetings with U.S. negotiators had been finalized, producing caution among Gulf partners about immediate normalization. — AP; CNN; Jun 29

Jul 10 — Renewed strikes and U.S. demand for public Strait assurances

Update (Jul 10): Reports on Jul 10 say U.S. forces struck multiple Iranian targets after fresh attacks on vessels near the Strait of Hormuz; U.S. officials demanded Iran publicly declare the Strait open to commercial shipping, prompting urgent diplomatic outreach in Gulf capitals. — AP; Reuters; Jul 10

Jul 12 — Iran declares Strait closed; strikes exchanged

Update (Jul 12): Iranian state media announced the Strait of Hormuz was closed following renewed exchanges of strikes; U.S. Central Command publicly stated the Strait remained open to lawful transit and that U.S. forces were conducting operations to protect shipping, heightening alarm and prompting urgent diplomatic engagement across Gulf capitals. — Reuters; AP; Jul 12

Jul 14 — U.S. blockade reimposed; fresh strikes escalate regional alarm

Update (Jul 14–15): U.S. forces said they reimposed a naval blockade of Iranian ports and launched another round of strikes near the Strait of Hormuz; Gulf capitals cautioned that the move significantly raises the risk to commercial shipping and regional stability. — Reuters; AP; The Guardian; Jul 14–15

Jul 16 — U.S. disables tanker; renewed strikes near Hormuz

Update (Jul 16): Reuters and other outlets report U.S. forces disabled an unladen oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz and conducted additional strikes near Qeshm Island; regional governments warned the moves increase risk to commercial shipping and prompted urgent consultations among Gulf states. — Reuters; The Guardian; Jul 16

Jul 17 — Tanker struck off Oman; casualties reported

On Jul 17 a crude oil tanker, reportedly the Al Bahyah, was struck off Oman's coast near the Strait of Hormuz; one seafarer was killed and three injured, prompting renewed industry alarms and additional naval precautions. — CNBC; Jul 17

Jul 18 — U.S. troops killed in Jordan; U.S. launches strikes

Update (Jul 17–18): CENTCOM and AP report that two U.S. service members were killed and one is missing after Iranian attacks in Jordan; U.S. forces launched retaliatory strikes on Iran in response, renewing regional alarm. — AP; CENTCOM; Jul 17–18

Jul 20 — U.S. completes further strikes; Houthis expand maritime operations

Update (Jul 20): AP and other outlets report U.S. Central Command said it completed another round of strikes against Iranian targets late on Jul 20 after President Trump warned Tehran; Houthi-aligned forces announced expanded operations threatening Red Sea transits (including a declared blockade of Saudi-linked shipping), widening maritime and regional alarm. — AP; CNBC; Jul 20

Jul 22 — Houthis claim strikes on Saudi tankers; U.S. strikes continue

Update (Jul 22): Houthi-aligned forces claimed strikes on two Saudi oil tankers in the southern Red Sea and reports said U.S. forces conducted additional strikes on Iran the same day, broadening the maritime front and triggering urgent consultations among Gulf states. — ISW; CNBC; Jul 22

Jul 23 — U.S. conducts 13th consecutive night of strikes; Red Sea attacks continue

Update (Jul 23): U.S. Central Command said it completed a 13th straight night of strikes on Iranian military targets; Houthi-aligned forces continued attacks in the Red Sea, claiming strikes on Saudi-linked tankers on Jul 22–23, further broadening maritime risk. — CENTCOM; CNN; Jul 23

Jul 25 — Saudi–Houthi exchanges escalate; regional strikes

Update (Jul 25): Houthi forces claimed missile and drone strikes against Saudi targets and Saudi-led forces conducted retaliatory strikes in Yemen; regional governments and mediators warned the exchanges risk widening the conflict and complicating ongoing diplomatic efforts. — AP; CNBC; The Guardian; Jul 25

Military Targets Hit

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — US 5th Fleet: Naval Support Activity Bahrain struck by Iranian missiles (Feb 28). Bahrain condemned the strikes as "a flagrant attack on sovereignty." — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base: Largest US air base in the Middle East and CENTCOM Forward HQ targeted. Patriot systems intercepted. US has not confirmed base damage. — Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 1
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Abu Dhabi: Missile struck near a US-linked site; 1 killed, others injured. Patriot and THAAD batteries activated; multiple intercepts confirmed. UAE intercepted 9 BMs and 35 drones on Mar 10. — US News (Tier‑4), Feb 28
  • 🇰🇼 Kuwait — 12+ Killed: Drone attack on Port Shuaiba killed 6 US soldiers (Mar 5). On Mar 10, fresh barrages killed 12 more including 6 US service members and 2 Kuwaiti soldiers. Ali Al Salem Air Base on FPCON Delta. — NBC/Euronews, Mar 10; Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 5–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia: Impacts on Saudi soil including a major refinery (Mar 2) and drone hits on the US Embassy compound in Riyadh (Mar 3). Ras Tanura refinery/export terminal closed. US ordered non-emergency staff to depart. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 3 & 7; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Civilian & Economic Impacts

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — Iranian drone struck a water desalination plant (Mar 8), targeting civilian infrastructure. Bapco Energies declared force majeure on some products. — AP (Tier‑2), Mar 8; Axios (Tier‑4), Mar 9
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — declared force majeure on LNG exports after drone attacks disrupted output and logistics. Doha offered mediation; Iranian ambassador summoned. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 7–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia — GCC emergency summit convened in Riyadh; humanitarian and air-defense cooperation expanded with CENTCOM. Airport closures reported across the region.
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Emergency National Security Council session; temporary airport closures. Formal protest to Iran filed.

Update (Jul 12): Iranian announcements that the Strait of Hormuz was closed after renewed exchanges of strikes triggered immediate industry concern; CENTCOM and maritime groups reported that commercial transits continued in some corridors under escort, but insurers and operators warned of heightened risk and potential short‑term pauses in some routes. — Reuters; AP; Jul 12

Jul 14 — Blockade reimposed; shipping disruption intensifies

Update (Jul 14–15): The U.S. reimposed a naval blockade of Iranian ports and launched fresh strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, prompting immediate industry warnings about disrupted lanes, rising insurance premiums, and an expectation that routine transits could be suspended or rerouted until risks abate. — Reuters; AP; The Guardian; Jul 14–15

Jul 22 — Red Sea attacks expand shipping risk

Update (Jul 22): Reports that Houthi-aligned forces struck two Saudi-linked oil tankers in the southern Red Sea on Jul 22 have further constrained alternative routing options and prompted fresh insurer and operator pauses for vessels rerouting around the Bab el‑Mandeb. — CNN; CNBC; Jul 22
