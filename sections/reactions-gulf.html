Gulf States: Iranian Retaliation Damage

Iran's ballistic missile and drone attacks on US-hosting Gulf nations — Feb 28–Mar 12, 2026
Active
Jun 25 — Vessel struck near Oman; regional alarm

On Jun 25 a vessel was struck near Oman in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting regional alarm and a temporary pause in IMO evacuation operations while navies and operators reassessed transits. — Reuters; AP; Jun 25

Jun 26 — U.S. strikes Iranian missile, drone and radar sites after ship attack

U.S. Central Command said on Jun 26 that it conducted strikes on Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in retaliation for the drone attack on a commercial vessel; the strikes heightened regional alarm and tested the Islamabad Memorandum's implementation. — AP; Reuters; Jun 26

Jun 27 — Additional U.S. strikes and Iranian claims raise regional tensions

Update (Jun 27): U.S. Central Command said U.S. forces carried out additional strikes on Iranian military targets after another attack on a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting fresh concern among Gulf states over the memorandum's durability. Iranian officials said they had struck U.S. military sites in Bahrain and Kuwait; those claims were reported but not independently verified at the time of reporting. — Reuters; AP; Jun 27

Iran's Retaliation — 500+ BMs, ~2,000 Drones Since Feb 28

Iran has fired over 500 ballistic/naval missiles and ~2,000 drones since Feb 28, with ~40% aimed at Israel and ~60% at US regional targets. Half of Iran's missiles at Israel carried cluster bomb warheads (IDF assessment). Gen. Caine says Iran's missile launches are down 90% and drones down 83% from initial rates. — Sources: Compiled reporting (Tier‑2/3/4 mix), Mar 10–12

Mar 21 — Saudi Arabia expels Iranian military attache and staff

Saudi Arabia declared Iran's military attache, his assistant, and three embassy staff persona non grata and ordered them to leave within 24 hours, citing recent Iranian attacks on Saudi territory. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 21

May 12 — Saudi covert strikes widen regional involvement

Reuters reported on May 12 that Saudi forces carried out covert strikes on Iranian soil as the wider conflict expanded, a development that could draw additional Gulf states into kinetic retaliation and complicate diplomatic channels. — Reuters; May 12

Jun 2 — Kuwait airport struck, prompting regional alarm

AP and Reuters reported on Jun 2 that Iranian missiles and drones damaged Kuwait International Airport, killing at least one civilian and injuring dozens; the strike raised concerns about escalation and civilian harm in the Gulf theater. — AP; Reuters; Jun 2

Jun 5 — Continued drone/missile activity toward Hormuz

Reuters reported on Jun 5 that Iranian forces launched multiple drones and low‑flying threats toward the Strait of Hormuz over recent days and that U.S. and coalition assets intercepted and shot down several of those systems, underscoring the persistent threat to Gulf maritime traffic. — Reuters; Jun 5

Military Targets Hit

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — US 5th Fleet: Naval Support Activity Bahrain struck by Iranian missiles (Feb 28). Bahrain condemned the strikes as "a flagrant attack on sovereignty." — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 1
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — Al Udeid Air Base: Largest US air base in the Middle East and CENTCOM Forward HQ targeted. Patriot systems intercepted. US has not confirmed base damage. — Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 1
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Abu Dhabi: Missile struck near a US-linked site; 1 killed, others injured. Patriot and THAAD batteries activated; multiple intercepts confirmed. UAE intercepted 9 BMs and 35 drones on Mar 10. — US News (Tier‑4), Feb 28
  • 🇰🇼 Kuwait — 12+ Killed: Drone attack on Port Shuaiba killed 6 US soldiers (Mar 5). On Mar 10, fresh barrages killed 12 more including 6 US service members and 2 Kuwaiti soldiers. Ali Al Salem Air Base on FPCON Delta. — NBC/Euronews, Mar 10; Stars and Stripes (Tier‑3), Mar 5–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia: Impacts on Saudi soil including a major refinery (Mar 2) and drone hits on the US Embassy compound in Riyadh (Mar 3). Ras Tanura refinery/export terminal closed. US ordered non-emergency staff to depart. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 3 & 7; CNBC (Tier‑4), Mar 9

Civilian & Economic Impacts

  • 🇧🇭 Bahrain — Iranian drone struck a water desalination plant (Mar 8), targeting civilian infrastructure. Bapco Energies declared force majeure on some products. — AP (Tier‑2), Mar 8; Axios (Tier‑4), Mar 9
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar — declared force majeure on LNG exports after drone attacks disrupted output and logistics. Doha offered mediation; Iranian ambassador summoned. — Reuters (Tier‑2), Mar 7–8
  • 🇦🇮 Saudi Arabia — GCC emergency summit convened in Riyadh; humanitarian and air-defense cooperation expanded with CENTCOM. Airport closures reported across the region.
  • 🇦🇪 UAE — Emergency National Security Council session; temporary airport closures. Formal protest to Iran filed.
Jun 25 — Vessel struck; IMO pauses evacuation

A vessel was struck near Oman in the Strait of Hormuz on Jun 25, prompting the International Maritime Organization to pause evacuation operations and briefly halt some commercial transits. — Reuters; AP; Jun 25

Jun 26 — U.S. retaliatory strikes after vessel attack complicate transits

Update (Jun 26): U.S. Central Command said it struck Iranian missile, drone and coastal radar sites in response to the Jun 25 drone attack on a commercial vessel; the strikes briefly renewed concerns about escorted transits and insurance clearance timelines even as some movements continued. — Reuters; AP; Jun 26

Jun 27 — Additional strikes renew shipping uncertainty

Update (Jun 27): U.S. forces conducted additional strikes on Iranian military targets after another reported attack on commercial shipping, prompting operators and insurers to warn that routine normalization of transits and insurance clearances could be delayed further. — Reuters; AP; Jun 27

Jun 10 — U.S. disables third oil tanker attempting to breach blockade

CENTCOM reported that U.S. forces disabled a third oil tanker in the Gulf of Oman on Jun 10 after the vessel attempted to violate the blockade; the action underscores persistent enforcement and has increased diplomatic tensions with countries whose crews or owners were affected. — CENTCOM; Jun 10

Jun 18 — Blockade lifted as memorandum takes effect; limited transits resume

CENTCOM and multiple outlets report that, following the Islamabad Memorandum signed on Jun 18, U.S. forces began lifting naval blockade measures and phased resumption of escorted transits through the Strait of Hormuz began; operators warned normal traffic levels and insurance clearances could take weeks to restore. — CNBC; Reuters; Jun 18

Apr 20 — Shipping again near standstill after shots and ship seizure

Reuters reported on Apr 20 that commercial ship traffic largely avoided the Strait of Hormuz after an escalation over the weekend in which Iran fired warning shots and U.S. forces seized an Iran‑flagged vessel; operators remained cautious and selective about transits. — Reuters; Apr 20

Jun 20 — Iran says Strait reclosed; shipping sees mixed signals

Update (Jun 20): The New York Times and BBC report that Iran announced it had reclosed the Strait of Hormuz on Jun 20 even as commercial traffic earlier that day had shown signs of uptick; navies and operators cautioned that while some escorted transits occurred, routine normalization and insurance clearances remained uncertain. — NYT; BBC; Jun 20
