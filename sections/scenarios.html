Scenario 3

Regime Collapse — Revolution From Within

Likelihood: {{scenarioRevolutionPct}}% — Most Likely Post-Strike Outcome
🔴 Inflection Point — Khamenei Confirmed Dead (Mar 1)

Khamenei's death removes the single node through which all IRGC factional disputes were ultimately arbitrated. For 35 years, his authority was the answer to the question "who decides?" — on military escalation, on nuclear negotiation, on which commander gets which contract. That answer no longer exists. Every IRGC faction, every provincial commander, every senior cleric is now simultaneously calculating: Is the regime salvageable? And if not, what do I do with my weapons? — Sources: Reuters, AP, NBC News, Mar 1

The Islamic Republic has always operated on what analysts call the "avalanche model" of loyalty: its power appears solid until it suddenly isn't. The regime survives because everyone believes everyone else is loyal. The moment a significant unit visibly defects — and survives — that fiction evaporates. Provincial garrison commanders who were on the fence now calculate that the regime cannot punish them all. The cascade begins. Khamenei's death, combined with ongoing strikes, mass protests in all 31 provinces, and economic freefall, has pushed the system to the edge of that threshold.

The Signals to Watch

  • IRGC 32nd Corps (Kurdistan) and 17th Qods Corps (Sistan-Baluchestan) — provincial units in ethnic minority regions have the lowest morale after months of ordering fire on their own communities. These are the units most likely to stand down first.
  • Bank Melli payroll failure — Iran's largest state bank handles government employee salaries, including IRGC. If soldiers' paychecks fail for even one cycle with the rial at 1.7M/USD, units that were holding will recalculate loyalty within hours.
  • Mojtaba Khamenei moves publicly — The Supreme Leader's 54-year-old second son controls IRGC intelligence and the Basij ideological apparatus. If he attempts to publicly claim succession authority, it triggers the factional war (Sc3). If he recedes into the shadows, a junta forms around him instead (Sc6). His next public move is the single most important signal.
  • First IRGC general contacts CIA channel — The Tor (.onion) portal and recruitment video are live. The first confirmed defection of a general-rank officer will be the visible cascade trigger that convinces others the system is collapsing.
  • Protest–military merger — Post-strike protests are now merging with anti-war sentiment, reaching IRGC families. When wives and mothers of soldiers begin joining demonstrations, the calculation for provincial commanders changes fundamentally.

The Transition Problem

  • No unified opposition command — collapse without a pre-positioned transitional authority risks a Libya-style vacuum. Pahlavi's Prosperity Project (see Sc4) is the closest thing to a ready plan, but it has no organizational infrastructure inside Iran.
  • IRGC controls 60–70% of the economy — the Revolutionary Guards' construction companies, import monopolies, and pension funds involve millions of families. Dismantling this overnight destroys livelihoods, not just a power structure.
  • Nuclear material security gap — 1,000+ kg of 60–90% enriched uranium must be secured within hours, not days. If central authority collapses before US/IAEA teams can move in, proliferation risk is acute. This is the scenario that keeps Pentagon planners awake. — Source: IAEA (program status background), Mar 3
  • The Mojtaba wildcard — Even in a collapse scenario, Khamenei's son likely has the weapons and intelligence networks to contest any transition. A "collapse" that doesn't neutralize him risks becoming a protracted civil war, not a clean revolution.
  • Historical precedent is grim: Libya 2011 collapsed in eight months and has not recovered in 13 years. Iraq 2003's institutional collapse produced a civil war that killed 150,000+ civilians. Iran has four times Iraq's population and an intact nuclear program.
The CIA's Play: Accelerating the Cascade

The CIA's Feb 24–25 Farsi-language recruitment video was not intelligence-gathering theater — it was a public commitment device. By broadcasting the existence of a secure defection channel (VPN outside Iran/Russia/China, disposable device, Tor .onion portal), the Agency signaled to every IRGC officer that the option to leave exists. This matters because one of the key barriers to defection is not ideology but coordination: officers won't defect if they believe they'll defect alone. The video solves the coordination problem. If senior IRGC commanders begin defecting in the next 72 hours — even one or two high-profile names — the cascade psychology takes over. The "wide tent" offer from Pahlavi targets the same audience with the same logic: there is a safe landing, if you move now.

🔴 Who Would Actually Run the Junta

Under the Islamic Republic's Article 131, when the Supreme Leader dies, a Leadership Council of Three — the First VP, the parliament speaker, and the judiciary chief — manages the country for 50 days while the Assembly of Experts selects a permanent successor. In theory, this means Mohammad Mokhber (First VP, a technocrat) assumes executive power. In practice, four figures hold the actual levers:

  • Mojtaba Khamenei (54, Supreme Leader's second son) — controls IRGC intelligence directorate and Basij ideological corps. Not elected to any position, but has spent years building a parallel power network. The most likely shadow ruler of a junta scenario. Reports from Iran watchers (including Karim Sadjadpour at Carnegie) suggest he was systematically positioned to inherit his father's mantle. His legitimacy problem: he is not a senior cleric, cannot claim velayat-e faqih authority, and would be transparently a dynastic power grab — exactly what the 1979 revolution was supposedly against. — Source: Carnegie Endowment, Mar 2
  • Hossein Salami (IRGC Commander-in-Chief) — hardline ideologue, commands the military apparatus. Would be the junta's enforcer, not its public face. Lacks clerical legitimacy to serve as Supreme Leader. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2
  • Esmail Qaani (Quds Force Chief) — focused on preserving external proxy networks; priority is keeping Houthis and Iraqi PMF funded to maintain external leverage even if the home front fractures. — Source: ISW, Mar 2
  • Ahmad Jannati (Guardian Council chief) — provides clerical cover for constitutional legitimacy; the Assembly of Experts could be managed to produce a Mojtaba-aligned outcome. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2

Most likely junta structure: Mokhber as constitutional president (figurehead); Mojtaba as shadow Supreme Leader with clerical cover; Salami commanding security; Qaani preserving external leverage. — Sources: Reuters, NBC News, Carnegie Endowment, Mar 1–2

Following confirmation that Supreme Leader Khamenei is dead, a security-led succession emerges in Tehran. The Assembly of Experts names an interim leadership structure while IRGC commanders and security organs effectively run the state, imposing emergency law to crush protests and continue confrontation under new leadership. This scenario is Sc3's dark mirror: the same conditions that could produce collapse could instead produce consolidation, if the IRGC acts faster than the protest movement.

The Mojtaba Factor

  • Mojtaba Khamenei is the single most important figure in determining whether this scenario succeeds or collapses into Sc3. His assets: IRGC intelligence files on every senior commander, Basij mobilization capability, and years of cultivated loyalty networks. His liabilities: zero public legitimacy, no constitutional role, and every opposition faction — including reformists inside the system — sees him as exactly the corrupt dynastic succession the revolution was against.
  • If Mojtaba moves in the next 48 hours to control the succession process, he either consolidates power (Sc6) or triggers the factional civil war that makes collapse (Sc3) inevitable. There is no middle path for him.
  • Russia and China are wild cards. Both have incentives to back a junta that maintains continuity — chaos in Iran threatens their energy and trade relationships more than a compliant junta does. Expect quiet signaling of support for Mokhber/Mojtaba continuity. — Source: Reuters, Mar 3

Why the Junta Could Fail

  • IRGC fractional math — the IRGC has six independent corps (Aerospace, Ground Forces, Navy, Quds, Intelligence, Basij). They are not a unified command; they are competing fiefdoms. A junta requires all six to agree on who is in charge — under wartime pressure.
  • Economic collapse accelerates under junta — the rial slide, disrupted oil exports, and isolation worsen immediately under comprehensive sanctions and global risk aversion. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2
  • International rejection — a Mojtaba-backed junta would face rapid non-recognition and expanded sanctions from the US/EU/UK/Canada.
  • Provincial commanders' survival calculus — if consolidation looks untenable, commanders defect rather than go down with a sanctioned, isolated junta.
If the Junta Consolidates: Consequences

A stable IRGC junta — if it achieves internal cohesion within 72 hours — would: impose emergency law and a communications blackout; detain prominent protest leaders; condition IAEA access on sanctions relief; activate proxies (PMF, Houthis) to signal deterrence; and seek recognition from Russia/China while buying time economically. Nuclear risk rises if enriched material survives and weaponization accelerates under existential threat logic. — Sources: IAEA (access statements), Mar 3; ISW, Mar 2

Scenario 4

Pahlavi Returns — Democratic Transition

Likelihood: {{scenarioPahlaviPct}}% — Rising as regime fractures under strike pressure
NEW (Mar 1) — The "Prosperity Project": What It Actually Proposes

Reza Pahlavi's "Prosperity Project", unveiled March 1 as strikes continued, is a three-phase governance plan — not a manifesto:

  • Phase 1 — Emergency (Days 1–30): Provisional Government of National Unity declared; release of all political prisoners immediately; UN Security Council resolution recognizing the transitional authority; IRGC officers who stand down receive amnesty and pension protection; diaspora professionals invited back to fill civil service vacancies
  • Phase 2 — Reconstruction (Days 30–100): Unlock $100B+ in frozen assets via US/EU sanctions relief; IMF/World Bank emergency reconstruction facility; Gulf state co-investment in energy and infrastructure; "Contract with Iran" — constitutionally binding guarantees for minority rights, women's rights, religious freedom, and press freedom; IRGC economic empire placed under civilian oversight, not dissolved overnight
  • Phase 3 — Democracy (Post-100 days): Free elections with international monitoring within 18 months; constitutional convention with representatives from all 31 provinces, ethnic groups, and diaspora; military placed under civilian command; secular democratic republic — explicitly not a monarchy

The key differentiator from 1979: Pahlavi explicitly frames this as "One Iran" — not federalization — while offering the maximum autonomy guarantees for Kurdish, Baloch, Arab, and Azeri communities within a unified state. This is designed to close the one fracture point that destroyed every previous opposition coalition. — Sources: Times of Israel (summary), Mar 1; Washington Post, Mar 2

The US–Israel strike campaign fatally weakens the Islamic Republic's military and economic foundations. The IRGC fractures — senior commanders, heeding Pahlavi's "wide tent" offer and the CIA's recruitment campaign, choose the people over the regime. Reza Pahlavi returns to Iran for the first time since 1979 to lead a 100-day transitional process toward the first free elections in the Islamic Republic's history. This scenario requires Sc3 (collapse) to not descend into chaos — it is specifically the ordered version of regime change, where the IRGC's stand-down is coordinated rather than anarchic.

What Would Trigger This

  • IRGC command fractures in coordination — not chaotic defection, but 3–5 senior generals collectively announcing support for a transitional authority, removing the coordination barrier for others
  • Khamenei death — CONFIRMED (Mar 1) — succession vacuum active; no figure with equivalent authority to order the IRGC to resist
  • Trump administration recognition — Witkoff back-channel becomes official; White House endorses Pahlavi's transitional council, giving it international legitimacy before it has domestic control — Source: POLITICO, Mar 1
  • Gulf state financial support — Saudi Arabia and UAE pledge reconstruction financing; removes the economic collapse fear that keeps IRGC loyalists on the fence — Source: Reuters, Mar 3
  • Protests provide cover — a mass uprising in Tehran gives commanders political justification to say they were "responding to the people's will," not surrendering to foreign pressure

Critical Obstacles — and Why They Matter Now

  • Mojtaba Khamenei's network — even if the formal IRGC stands down, the Supreme Leader's son controls IRGC intelligence and Basij ideological units. He doesn't need legal authority to spoil a transition if he retains armed loyalists in Tehran.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi fracture (Feb 27) — friction between Pahlavi's circle and Kurdish dissident groups is public. Kurdish armed groups (PJAK) in the northwest may not honor an amnesty they weren't part of negotiating, creating a low-grade insurgency in the transition's first weeks. — Source: BBC, Feb 27
  • Nuclear material as leverage — whatever IRGC faction controls the remaining enriched uranium holds a decisive bargaining chip, unlikely to be surrendered absent binding security guarantees and phased amnesty.
  • No party, no bureaucracy, no network — Pahlavi has lived in exile since age 17. A transitional government without a functioning civil service will rely on the same technocrats who served the Islamic Republic, creating legitimacy problems from Day 1.
Historical Parallel — and Why February–March 2026 Is Different

Every previous Iranian opposition effort since 1979 failed against the same wall: IRGC loyalty to the regime. Five things have simultaneously changed in February–March 2026 that have never been simultaneously true before: (1) the IRGC's economic base is being disrupted, removing the financial incentive for loyalty; (2) the protest movement has named a single leader, giving fence-sitting commanders a face to surrender to; (3) the CIA has opened a verified, operational defection channel — not rhetoric but a live Tor portal; (4) the US has signaled willingness to recognize a transitional government; (5) Khamenei's death removes the authority figure who could have ordered the IRGC to resist regardless of material incentives. The question is whether Pahlavi and the opposition can convert conditions into a coordinated institutional move before the Mojtaba/junta factions fill the vacuum. — Sources: CBS/60 Minutes, Mar 3; POLITICO, Mar 1

What a Free Iran Looks Like: The 10-Year Trajectory

A democratic Iran — even an imperfect one — fundamentally reshapes the Middle East within a decade. Year 1: Hormuz reopens under UN maritime governance; proxy networks lose state funding and degrade within 18 months. Year 2–3: $100B+ in frozen assets plus IFI reconstruction financing lift growth. Years 3–5: diaspora returns capital and talent; WTO path opens. Years 5–10: normalized relations with Europe and neighbors; nuclear program wound down under IAEA monitoring. Proxy wars recede and regional trade deepens. — Sources: Atlantic Council, Mar 2; IAEA (monitoring baseline), Mar 3
