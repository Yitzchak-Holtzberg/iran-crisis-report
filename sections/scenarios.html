Scenario 1

Regime Collapse — Revolution From Within

Likelihood: {{scenarioRevolutionPct}}% — Most Likely Post-Strike Outcome
🔴 Day 6–7 Inflection: IRGC Fractures Watch; Succession Resolved but Contested

Previously tracked fracture indicators remain pivotal. Reports of a 17th Qods Corps (Sistan‑Baluchestan) stand‑down in select garrisons continue to circulate (unconfirmed, tier‑4/5); Bank Melli payroll disruptions for IRGC‑linked entities have introduced acute loyalty risk as the rial weakens; and the Assembly of Experts named Mojtaba Khamenei Supreme Leader on Mar 3 (Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera) — resolving the vacancy but activating a legitimacy crisis due to his Hojjat ol‑Islam rank. The Day‑7 Gulf barrage signals an externalization strategy that can temporarily rally loyalists, but does not fix the regime’s internal cohesion or paychain problems. — Sources: Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera, Mar 3; Reuters (garrison reports not fully wire‑confirmed), Mar 4; Wall Street Journal (payroll), Mar 3; Janes (Gulf barrage), Mar 3

The Islamic Republic runs on an “avalanche model” of loyalty: strength appears monolithic until visible defection shatters the perception equilibrium. The regime survives because each actor believes others will enforce compliance. A first visible senior defection that survives retaliation unlocks a rapid cascade. Khamenei’s death, sustained strikes, expanding protests, and a currency shock are converging. The Day‑7 proxy escalation buys time but strains logistics and finances — the very levers sustaining regime loyalty.

  • The Mojtaba wildcard — even amid collapse, armed loyalists in Tehran/Qom could prolong violence.
  • Historical precedent — Iraq 2003 and Libya 2011 warn against unmanaged institutional collapse.
Scenario 2

Pahlavi Returns — Democratic Transition

Likelihood: {{scenarioPahlaviPct}}% — Requires ordered collapse, not chaotic one
Day 6–7: Prosperity Project Visibility Grows; Diplomatic Scaffolding Tentative

Reza Pahlavi’s “Prosperity Project” remains the most articulated post‑regime framework in circulation. International media attention and US political signaling continue; White House language has edged toward “the Iranian people deserve a choice,” and back‑channel contacts with advisers persist (POLITICO, Mar 3; Washington Post, Mar 2). Omani‑facilitated technical talks in Vienna are tentatively slated, keeping a narrow verification track alive that could intersect with a transition if collapse accelerates (CBS News, Mar 2; Ynetnews, Mar 4 — tier‑4). These are enablers, not commitments; success still hinges on IRGC coordination and clerical acquiescence.

⚠ Kurdish Fracture Risk: PJAK Still Not On Board

The PJAK (Iranian Kurdish armed group) has not publicly endorsed the Prosperity Project, maintaining a spoiler risk in the northwest if a transition is declared without binding autonomy guarantees (BBC, Feb 27). Parallel reporting indicates thousands of Kurdish fighters in northern Iraq are preparing for potential cross‑border operations with U.S. backing (AP, Mar 3). If Kurdish armed moves proceed outside an agreed transitional framework, Sc2 viability declines and Sc4 risk rises. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; AP News, Mar 3

Sc2 succeeds only if Sc1’s collapse is coordinated rather than chaotic: IRGC commanders must pivot together, an interim authority must secure clerical neutrality or support, and external partners must underwrite immediate stabilization. The Day‑7 proxy barrage marginally improves junta rallying but also pressures IRGC logistics and pay, potentially accelerating coordination incentives if a credible amnesty and nuclear security plan exist.

  • Vienna technical track + IAEA access — verification pathway aligns with transition milestones. — Sources: CBS News, Mar 2; IAEA statements
  • Kurdish alignment — PJAK and other groups endorse framework to avoid a northwest insurgency.

Critical Obstacles

  • Mojtaba’s network — IRGC Intelligence and Basij ideological units can spoil absent neutralization or co‑optation.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi gap — PJAK non‑endorsement persists; AP reports Kurdish preparations for independent action. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; AP, Mar 3
  • Nuclear leverage — any IRGC faction controlling enriched uranium can bargain hard; requires rapid IAEA/US security operations.
  • Institutional vacuum — no party machine or bureaucracy ready to govern; reliance on regime technocrats risks legitimacy.

Scenario 4

Likelihood: {{scenarioCivilWarPct}}% — Chaotic collapse variant; rises if Sc1 goes ungoverned
⚠ The Chaos Fork Widening: Independent Kurdish Moves Would Tip the Balance

Sc4 is Sc1 without coordination. New reporting that thousands of Kurdish fighters in northern Iraq are preparing for operations inside Iran with U.S. backing (AP, Mar 3) increases the risk that regional armed actors seize vacated space before any transitional authority coheres. The Day‑7 proxy barrage can further fragment chains of command as IRGC resources are diverted externally.

Sc4 activates when collapse outpaces political organization: IRGC corps do not pivot in unison, ethnic militias assert local control, and Mojtaba‑aligned loyalists wage urban insurgency in Tehran/Qom. Nuclear material custodianship becomes contested across multiple actors — a proliferation worst case.

  • Secure‑and‑remove window shrinks — Pentagon/IAEA planning assumes an interlocutor; fragmentation removes it.

What Prevents Fragmentation
