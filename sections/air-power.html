The Asymmetry That Matters

The US has overwhelming conventional superiority — Iran cannot contest the air, sea, or open battlefield. But Iran's remaining threats are deliberately asymmetric: drone swarms that exhaust interceptor stocks, cyber attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure, proxy forces operating independently, and Hormuz mines that a destroyed navy can still deploy. The war's trajectory depends less on force balance and more on whether these asymmetric tools can impose costs faster than coalition strikes degrade them.
