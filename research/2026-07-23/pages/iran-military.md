# Iran Military Pilot — Research Record

As of: 2026-07-23 ET  
Implementation status: rewritten; build and visual QA pending

## Questions answered

- What conventional capacity has clearly been degraded?
- What systems and operational methods remain strategically relevant?
- What can and cannot be counted from public battle-damage claims?
- How should command succession and IRGC influence be described?
- How did Iran's retaliation change across the conflict?
- Why did destruction of conventional naval assets not normalize Hormuz?
- What evidence would materially change the assessment?

## Claims changed

- Replaced a confident remaining-inventory model with a demonstrated-capacity model.
- Replaced aggregate loss totals with confidence-qualified category judgments.
- Separated platform damage from operational effect and reconstitution.
- Separated the nuclear-verification problem from conventional battle damage.
- Reframed Hormuz as a security, commercial, and political system.
- Replaced opening-week retaliation speculation with observed behavior through July.

## Claims removed

- Exact remaining missile, drone, launcher, and aircraft counts.
- Claims that Iran's navies, cyber capability, or partner network are wholly destroyed or wholly intact.
- Claims that non-use proves an intentionally withheld strategic reserve.
- Unsupported aircraft-loss, war-cost, and naval-loss aggregates.
- Time-expired “next 100 hours” and second-wave forecasts.
- Old March status callouts presented as current.

## Unresolved uncertainties

- Exact remaining launch and production capacity.
- Degree of command centralization under Mojtaba Khamenei.
- Pace of repair and procurement during pauses.
- Complete naval and air-defense loss ledger.
- Current Hormuz traffic and commercial-risk data after the latest July strikes.
- Nuclear material location and Pickaxe Mountain activity.

## Source coverage

Primary: CENTCOM, U.S. Department of War, IAEA findings, UKMTO/JMIC, IEA, EIA, OCHA.  
Wire: AP and Reuters reporting.  
Analysis: CSIS, RAND, IISS, CTP–ISW, Carnegie, RUSI, CFR, Chatham House.

## Next review triggers

- A verified IAEA inspection or material accounting.
- A sustained, independently measured fall or rise in Iranian launch generation.
- A published Hormuz navigation agreement.
- Independent loss imagery that materially changes a category judgment.
- A confirmed change in command authority or significant military defection.
