## Terminology guide

- **Confirmed:** supported by a suitable direct source or multiple strong independent sources.
- **Belligerent claim:** an assertion by a party conducting the war. It may be important but is not independent confirmation.
- **Assessment:** an interpretation linking facts; attribute it.
- **Forecast:** a conditional statement about a future outcome; state horizon and indicators.
- **Destroyed / damaged / struck:** never use interchangeably.
- **Inventory / available / operational / effective:** separate categories.
- **Open Strait:** physical passage is possible.
- **Commercial normalization:** ordinary volumes, routing, insurance, crewing, port access, and predictable legal conditions have returned.
- **Control of Hormuz:** use only as an attributed political or analytical claim; specify whether it means physical denial, routing influence, fee collection, or recognized authority.
- **Nuclear capability:** specify enrichment, material, equipment, expertise, weaponization, or delivery capability.
- **Regime:** acceptable for the governing system; use named institutions when the distinction matters.
- **Opposition:** never use as if it were a unified organization.

## Existing-site contradiction inventory

| Existing pattern | Problem | Replacement rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Remaining capability" stated as a confident inventory | Old order-of-battle minus claimed losses is not a valid operational estimate | Separate prewar baseline, supported loss, observed use, and unknown remainder |
| Large exact totals sourced to CENTCOM or secondary aggregations | Belligerent counts are presented as settled facts | Attribute and date them; remove unsupported aggregate totals |
| Hormuz described as open, closed, controlled, or 90% down without a common measure | Different pages use incompatible metrics | Use a layered status and date-specific data |
| Scenario percentages | No defensible method and false precision | Replace with ordinal judgments and indicators |
| "Regime under siege" framed as imminent collapse | Conflates strain with a forecast | Describe simultaneous resilience and vulnerability |
| Opposition page centered on Pahlavi as the default successor | Overstates organization and internal support | Present Pahlavi as one consequential current in a fragmented field |
| Nuclear damage language implies the program is solved | Physical damage is conflated with material and verification | Make verification failure the organizing fact |
| Analysis page as institution-by-institution cards | Produces a think-tank scrapbook rather than synthesis | Organize by contested propositions and use institutions as evidence |
| Home page dominated by a huge timeline, health/freshness, evidence counts, and statistics | Explains the update process rather than the situation | Orient around the whole picture; latest developments remain compact |

## Publication control

Before publishing a consequential claim, answer:

1. Is this a fact, assessment, or forecast?
2. What happened, when did it happen, and when was it reported?
3. Is the source direct, independent, a belligerent, or an advocate?
4. What is the geographic and category scope?
5. Is there conflicting evidence?
6. Does another page use the same claim with a different value or confidence?
7. What event or date makes the claim expire?
