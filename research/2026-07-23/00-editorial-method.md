# Editorial Re-foundation Method

As of: 2026-07-23 ET  
Status: active research protocol

## Core instruction

The current report is a topic inventory and technical shell, not a factual or analytical authority.

Every consequential claim, framing choice, statistic, assessment, and forecast must be re-established from current external evidence before it is retained.

## Synthesis replacement rule

When the current site contains a synthesis, replace it with a newly researched synthesis.

Do not replace synthesis with:

- a source-by-source reading list;
- a string of quotations;
- an unranked card collection;
- or a list of recent headlines.

A replacement synthesis must identify:

- what is established;
- what is assessed;
- where credible analysts agree;
- where they disagree;
- which assumptions explain the disagreement;
- what changed since the previous synthesis;
- what remains unresolved;
- and what evidence would change the conclusion.

### Time-bounded exception

If an existing block is explicitly a historical or time-bounded snapshot, preserve that function.

Do not silently modernize a historical snapshot into a timeless statement. Instead:

1. Retain or reconstruct the snapshot with a visible `as of` date.
2. Verify that it accurately represents what was knowable at that time.
3. Add a separate current synthesis when readers need the present state.
4. Never use an expired forecast as if it were current analysis.

## Institutional-harvest rule

Before rewriting the analysis, scenarios, or overview:

1. Build a broad corpus from the major institutions.
2. Index all materially relevant publications, not only the first search results.
3. Read the substantive argument, not only the title or summary.
4. Record each institution's thesis, evidence, assumptions, caveats, and policy preference.
5. Distinguish house position from an individual author's argument.
6. Track changes in an institution's view over time.
7. Treat recurring claims as hypotheses until their underlying evidence is verified.

Priority corpus:

- CSIS
- RAND
- IISS
- ISW and the Critical Threats Project
- CFR
- Carnegie Endowment
- Brookings
- Atlantic Council
- International Crisis Group
- Chatham House
- Middle East Institute
- Washington Institute
- SIPRI
- RUSI
- specialist nuclear, maritime, energy, humanitarian, and Iranian-society research

The institutional corpus supplies assessments. It does not replace primary reporting, official records, wire reporting, or data.

## Evidence classes

### Fact

A directly supported event, statement, number, position, or observable condition.

### Assessment

An attributed interpretation of facts. Record the analyst, institution, assumptions, and confidence.

### Forecast

A conditional judgment about what may happen. Record the time horizon, triggers, counterevidence, and indicators that would change the outlook.

## Reader-facing rule

Research-process metadata belongs in the research files, not in the default reader interface.

The reader should receive:

- an intelligible situation picture;
- concise source attribution;
- visible uncertainty where it matters;
- and optional access to deeper evidence.

The reader should not receive:

- search-result counts;
- evidence-domain inventories;
- update-health widgets;
- internal confidence machinery;
- or a wall of institutional logos and excerpts.

## Completion gate

No reader-facing synthesis is ready until:

- the institutional corpus for its domain has been harvested;
- the underlying factual claims have been independently checked;
- major disagreements have been mapped;
- time-bounded material has been separated from standing analysis;
- and the synthesis can explain its conclusion without hiding uncertainty.
