Inside Iran: A Regime Under Siege

Six converging crises threatening the Islamic Republic — Day 8 of Operation Epic Fury
Critical

🔥 Crisis 1: Operation Epic Fury — Day 8

The US-Israeli campaign that began February 28 has entered its second week with no signs of stopping. What started as precision strikes on nuclear and missile sites has expanded into a full-spectrum air campaign targeting military infrastructure, command nodes, naval assets, and — as of March 7 — civilian oil infrastructure for the first time.

Pentagon officials confirmed B-2 stealth bombers dropped 2,000-lb bombs on buried Iranian targets, and that Iran's ballistic missile and drone launches have fallen by roughly 90%. US Army HIMARS launchers conducted deep strikes destroying ballistic-missile silos. The latest wave struck Mehrabad Airport, Ekbatan, the Sharan and Shah Rey fuel depots in Tehran, and facilities across Alborz province. — Sources: CENTCOM, AP, Reuters, CBS News, Gulf News, Mar 6–7

Iran's Retaliation — Diminishing but Dangerous

Iran launched ~170 ballistic missiles in its initial retaliatory salvo (Mar 1), targeting Israel and US bases across the Gulf. But with missile production sites destroyed and launch capability degraded by 90%, Tehran has shifted to asymmetric retaliation: IRGC drone strikes on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, proxy attacks via Iraqi militias, and President Pezeshkian's declaration that all US military installations in the Persian Gulf are "legitimate targets." Iran formally apologised to Gulf neighbors for stray missiles striking the UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain. — Sources: Reuters, CBS News, CNN, Mar 1–7

⚔ Crisis 2: The Succession Crisis

Khamenei Confirmed Dead — Mar 1–2, 2026

Iranian state media and officials confirmed the death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei during US-Israeli strikes on a command bunker in Tehran. Israeli officials publicly claimed responsibility. NBC News reported members of his family were also killed. — Sources: Reuters, AP, NBC News, Mar 1–2

Mojtaba Khamenei Named New Supreme Leader — Legitimacy Contested

A partial session of the Assembly of Experts, convened under IRGC security escort, voted to name Mojtaba Khamenei — son of the late Ayatollah — as the new Supreme Leader. The appointment is deeply contested: Mojtaba holds no senior clerical credentials. Opposition figures, reformist clerics, and international observers have rejected the session's legitimacy. IRGC Commander Salami immediately pledged loyalty. The US and EU have not recognised the appointment. — Sources: Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera, Mar 3–6

President Pezeshkian announced a three-man leadership council (Ayatollah Arafi, Pezeshkian, Chief Justice Mohseni-Ejei) invoking constitutional succession. Whether this council actually commands IRGC forces in the field — or whether field commanders are operating autonomously — remains the critical unknown. The convergence of a contested succession, active military strikes degrading command nodes, and economic collapse eroding patronage networks has created what analysts call an existential fracture in regime cohesion.

  • Internal IRGC command changes and competing power centers
  • Provincial IRGC commanders — unclear if they follow Mojtaba or the council
  • Active strikes degrading the very command infrastructure needed for succession
  • Economic collapse eroding patronage networks that sustain loyalty

Eight days of sustained bombardment have created Iran's worst humanitarian crisis since the Iran-Iraq War. The Iranian Red Crescent Society reported over 6,668 civilian units targeted including hospitals, schools, and residential areas. UNICEF confirmed at least 181 children killed. BBC footage showed the scale of destruction across Tehran — cratered roads, destroyed buildings, and overwhelmed hospitals.

Communications Blackout — Total Information Shutdown

The partial internet restoration following the January blackout was reversed within hours of the first strikes on Feb 28. 92 million citizens are again cut off from the global web. Foreign journalists and NGOs have lost all VPN-based contact. The regime's two-tier internet system means only security-vetted elites retain global access. The blackout simultaneously conceals the scale of casualties and prevents civilian coordination for evacuation or aid. — Sources: Netblocks, Reuters, Foreign Policy, Mar 1–7

Independent casualty verification is effectively impossible. Source access inside Iran is severely restricted, communications are disrupted, and foreign journalists have been barred or expelled. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs stated it cannot verify casualty data "due to access restrictions and the fog of war." All figures should be treated as preliminary estimates subject to significant revision.

The Patronage Crisis

The IRGC's loyalty has always been sustained by economic patronage — access to smuggling networks, construction contracts, import monopolies. With oil revenue collapsing, infrastructure being bombed, and sanctions tightening, the regime can no longer afford to buy loyalty. This is why the succession crisis has become existential: it's not just about ideology, it's about shrinking revenue to divide among competing factions.

US Treasury Secretary Bessent admitted Washington deliberately engineered a dollar shortage to trigger runaway inflation: "It came to a swift and grand culmination in December, when one of the largest banks in Iran went under. There was a run on the bank... inflation exploded and hence we have seen the Iranian people out on the street." — Sources: Sunday Guardian, CNBC, Reuters, Axios, Mar 1–7

✊ Crisis 5: The Uprising — 190 Cities and Counting

Three overlapping waves of protest have merged into the largest sustained challenge to the Islamic Republic in its history:

The January Massacre

Economic protests starting Dec 28 escalated when Khamenei ordered live fire on Jan 8–9. HRANA documented 7,007 confirmed deaths; Iran International found evidence of 36,500+ killed. Protests spread to 203 cities, all 31 provinces. At least 30 people, including children, face execution for participation.

The Student Uprising

Universities organised 40-day Shia memorial protests starting Feb 21. Now in its third week across all 31 provinces. 410+ detained. Students chant "Death to the dictator," "Woman, life, freedom," and — significantly — chants calling for Reza Pahlavi. Khamenei's death sparked street celebrations despite active strikes.

The Ethnic Periphery

Kurdish, Baloch, Arab, and Azeri minorities face the heaviest military repression — heavy machine guns, armored vehicles, helicopter gunships. Over 2,000 Kurdish citizens arrested with documented torture. The Congress of Nationalities of Federal Iran now unites all minority groups.

War-Era Expansion

The uprising has now spread to ~190 cities with hit-and-run battles. The IRGC faces an impossible dilemma: suppress protests with forces needed for national defense, or defend against strikes while cities burn behind them. AP reports widespread arrests as the crackdown intensifies.

"We will speak with the protesters but... rioters should be put in their place."
— the late Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, attempting to split the movement before his death
Amnesty International Alert
At least 30 people, including several children, are at risk of execution in connection with the January uprising. Several were under 18 at the time. Torture methods documented include: threats of sexual assault, mock executions, beatings with cables, burning with irons, and nail extraction.

🌎 Crisis 6: Regional Escalation — The War Spreads

The conflict has broken out of Iran's borders, threatening to engulf the entire region:

NATO ARTICLE 4 — Iranian Missiles Strike Turkey (Mar 4)

Iranian ballistic missiles struck Turkish soil near İncirlik Air Base — host to US B61 nuclear gravity bombs — marking the first direct Iranian strike on a NATO member. President Erdoğan condemned the attack. NATO invoked Article 4 consultations. Turkey recalled its ambassador to Tehran and reserved its right of self-defense. — Sources: Reuters, NATO, Mar 4

Hezbollah — Decimated

Nasrallah killed (Sep 2024), 60%+ of long-range missiles destroyed. Syria supply route severed since Assad fell Dec 2024. Israel struck Beirut weapons facilities (Mar 3, 6). No longer a strategic deterrent.

Iraqi Militias — Active Front

Iran-backed militias claimed drone attacks on US troops at Baghdad airport and Erbil (Mar 6), turning Iraq into a new frontline. Kataib Hezbollah called for "martyrdom operations." — Sources: Foreign Policy, Mar 6

Houthis — Threatening

Houthi leader al-Houthi vowed support for Iran and pledged to resume Red Sea attacks: "our hands are on the trigger." Still launching anti-shipping missiles. — Sources: Al Jazeera, Foreign Policy, Mar 5

Gulf States — Under Fire

US Embassy Riyadh hit by drones. UAE: 1 killed. Bahrain 5th Fleet base struck. Qatar's Al Udeid targeted. Iran apologised for stray missiles — an extraordinary diplomatic gesture. — Sources: Reuters, CNN, Mar 1–7

Strategic Implication

The collapse of the Axis of Resistance means Iran's primary deterrence strategy has failed. Tehran built its proxy network to threaten retaliation without direct state-to-state warfare. With Hezbollah decimated, Syria lost, and the land bridge severed, Iran's only remaining tool was its ballistic missile arsenal — now degraded by 90%. The ISW notes this likely ends the "Axis of Resistance" as a coherent strategic concept. — Sources: ISW, Critical Threats, Mar 1–7
