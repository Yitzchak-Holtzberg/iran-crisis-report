Scenario 1

Diplomatic Deal — JCPOA 3.0

Likelihood: 0% — Severely diminished by Feb 28 strikes

Iran agreed in pre-strike talks (Oman mediation) to zero uranium stockpiling. However, the Feb 28 strikes have severely damaged the diplomatic track. A ceasefire-and-deal scenario remains theoretically possible but requires Iran to negotiate under active bombardment — historically rare and politically toxic for Tehran. Any path to a deal now runs through a ceasefire first.

What Would Trigger This

  • Khamenei quietly authorizes FM Araghchi to accept enrichment limits
  • Trump frames concessions as Iran "surrendering" — gives Khamenei political cover to call it a "resistance win"
  • Saudi Arabia and UAE pressure Iran privately to take the deal
  • IRGC hardliners are overruled — or Khamenei health scare concentrates decision-making

Why It's Unlikely

  • Khamenei has called enrichment an "unalienable right" for 20 years — any capitulation looks like regime defeat
  • Iran Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf set preconditions Trump cannot publicly accept
  • IRGC hardliners would view a deal as betrayal — potential coup risk
  • Trump's 10-day deadline has already been set; backing down risks domestic credibility
  • Araghchi's "within reach" language may be tactical — buying time while rebuilding Pickaxe Mountain
If It Happens: Consequences

Oil markets immediately stabilize — WTI drops back to $55-60. Strait of Hormuz tensions ease. US military begins draw-down within 30 days. But: the protest movement inside Iran remains unaddressed. A deal with the regime — not the Iranian people — could delay but not prevent eventual political rupture. Pahlavi and opposition groups would likely oppose any deal that leaves Khamenei in power. The regime's survival would be extended, not resolved.

Scenario 2

Military Strikes — "Operation Epic Fury"

⚡ IN PROGRESS
⚡ ACTIVATED — Feb 28, 2026

The US and Israel launched coordinated strikes against Iran under the name "Operation Epic Fury" on February 28. Trump confirmed "major combat operations" have begun. Iran retaliated with a ballistic missile barrage toward Israel and Gulf states. Explosions reported in Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and UAE. Major oil traders suspended Hormuz shipments. The conflict this scenario predicted is now underway.

Geneva failed to produce a deal before Trump's early-March deadline. The US — joined by Israel — launched coordinated strikes targeting Iran's nuclear infrastructure, IRGC command nodes, air defense systems, and leadership targets. Iran retaliated across multiple fronts simultaneously.

Phase 1: The Strike Package (Hours 0–48)

  • B-2 Spirit bombers (Diego Garcia — if UK relents, or Whiteman AFB non-stop) carry GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrators against Fordow, Natanz, "Pickaxe Mountain"
  • 600+ Tomahawk cruise missiles from USS Abraham Lincoln, Gerald R. Ford, and USS Georgia SSGN saturate air defenses and IRGC headquarters
  • F-35C and F/A-18E/F from both carrier air wings conduct follow-on precision strikes
  • F-22 Raptors from Al Udeid establish air superiority — Iran's aging F-4s and MiG-29s would be swept from the sky
  • EA-18G Growlers blind Iranian radar and communications
  • Israeli IAF launches simultaneous strikes from the east — 280,000 reservists already authorized

Phase 2: Iranian Retaliation (Hours 48–96)

  • Ballistic missile salvos (~1,500 missiles in dispersed mountain tunnels) targeting Israeli cities, US bases in Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan
  • IRGC Navy activates Hormuz closure — mines, fast-attack boats, anti-ship missiles against US carrier groups
  • Houthi missile barrage against Saudi oil infrastructure and Red Sea shipping
  • Iraqi PMF / Kataib Hezbollah attacks US forces in Iraq and Syria
  • Cyber attacks on Gulf financial systems, US infrastructure, Israeli power grid
  • Possible dirty bomb threat — Iran's enriched uranium stockpile not fully destroyed in strikes
The Key Unknown: Can the US Destroy "Pickaxe Mountain"?

Iran's new deep centrifuge facility south of Natanz may be buried at 80+ meters underground — beyond the GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator's effective penetration depth of ~60 meters in hardened rock. If the facility survives the initial strike, Iran could resume 90% enrichment within months. The Parchin nuclear site's new concrete-and-soil shield (confirmed Feb 20) is a direct countermeasure to this threat. Military planners have no confirmed answer to whether the strike can be decisive.

Global Economic Consequences

Goldman Sachs estimates Hormuz closure pushes WTI above $100/barrel within days. Every $10 increase costs the US economy ~$70B annually. Global recession risk: High. Immediate effects: European energy shock (LNG rerouting from Qatar impossible overnight), Indian and Japanese industrial slowdowns, Chinese manufacturing disruption. The global economy has no strategic petroleum reserve large enough to absorb a prolonged Hormuz closure.

NEW (Feb 25) — The "Israel First" Strategy: Politico Exclusive

Senior Trump White House advisers have privately told allies they believe "the politics are a lot better if the Israelis go first and alone" and Iran retaliates against US assets — creating domestic justification for a full US military response. Internal polling shows Americans support action if the US or an ally is hit first, but not a pre-emptive US strike. Internal consensus among those closest to Trump has reportedly hardened to "We're going to bomb them" — the debate now centers on timing and scope, not whether. Concerns noted include munitions depletion (risking a China-Taiwan window) and US casualty optics. CBS News separately confirmed Trump told Netanyahu at Mar-a-Lago in December 2025 that he would support Israeli strikes on Iran's ballistic missile program if talks fail — including discussions about US aerial refueling of Israeli jets and securing overflight corridors. — Sources: Politico Feb 25; CBS News Feb 15

Scenario 6

IRGC Junta — Succession Council Consolidates Power

Likelihood: 14% — Rising after Khamenei confirmed dead

Following confirmation that Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei is dead amid ongoing US–Israeli strikes, a security-led succession emerges in Tehran. The Assembly of Experts names an interim leadership structure while IRGC commanders and security organs effectively run the state, imposing emergency law to crush protests and continue confrontation under new leadership. — Sources: Reuters, NBC News live updates, Mar 1–2

What Would Trigger This

  • Confirmed death/incapacitation of Khamenei during active conflict — communications blackout limits transparency
  • Assembly of Experts moves to designate an acting leadership arrangement while deferring a permanent decision
  • IRGC secures Tehran, detains rival power centers, and announces curfews/emergency measures
  • Security services allegiance (IRGC Ground Forces, Quds Force, Intelligence) publicly pledged to a "national security council"
  • External backers (e.g., Russia/China) signal support for continuity to avoid regional chaos

Why It Could Fail

  • Escalating protests and strikes overwhelm security forces in multiple provinces simultaneously
  • IRGC factional splits between Quds Force, Aerospace, and provincial corps over succession and use of force
  • Economic collapse accelerates (currency plunge, oil export disruption), eroding regime capacity to pay loyalists
  • International isolation hardens — expanded sanctions and travel bans on the interim junta
  • Elite defection to a transitional opposition council if repression backfires
If It Happens: Consequences

Short-term repressive consolidation inside Iran; nuclear talks likely collapse; proxy activity could intensify as the junta seeks external leverage. Risk of prolonged internal conflict rises if provinces resist orders or if rival clerical–security factions emerge. Regional escalation risk remains high while command-and-control stabilizes.

Scenario 3

Regime Collapse — Revolution From Within

Likelihood: 42% — Rising as strikes create internal shock

The convergence of seven simultaneous crises — economic freefall, mass protests, military pressure, water catastrophe, ethnic uprising, proxy collapse, and IRGC fracturing — passes a threshold beyond the regime's ability to manage. The Islamic Republic fractures from within before a shot is fired.

The Tipping Point Conditions

  • Khamenei death — CONFIRMED (Mar 1) — succession power struggle now active; IRGC factions competing for control
  • IRGC unit defections — economic privation and moral strain from firing on civilians causes provincial units to stand down
  • Banking system collapse — if a second major bank fails (as threatened), hyperinflation accelerates beyond control
  • Generals accept Pahlavi's "wide tent" — IRGC insiders calculate regime survival is impossible and negotiate a soft landing
  • Protests reach critical mass — student uprising expands to include workers, bazaar merchants, and ultimately the IRGC's own families

The Transition Problem

  • No single opposition leader commands national consensus — Pahlavi is the most prominent but deeply divisive
  • IRGC controls 60-70% of the economy — any transition must deal with this entrenched power structure
  • Nuclear material security — 1,000+ kg of 60-90% enriched uranium must be secured immediately to prevent proliferation
  • Ethnic fracture risk — Kurdish, Baloch, Arab, and Azeri minorities may push for autonomy or secession in a power vacuum
  • Historical precedent: Libya 2011, Iraq 2003 — externally-supported regime changes typically produce prolonged civil conflict
The CIA's Play: Accelerating This Scenario

The CIA's Feb 24-25 Farsi-language recruitment video — offering IRGC insiders a secure channel to defect — is a direct play for Scenario 3. By specifically targeting insiders with access to nuclear, military, and financial information, the Agency is simultaneously collecting intelligence and signaling to IRGC officers that there is a path out. If senior IRGC commanders begin passing secrets — or, crucially, standing down during protests — the collapse scenario becomes self-fulfilling. The "wide tent" offer from Pahlavi and the CIA recruitment effort are complementary operations targeting the same audience: the 20,000+ senior IRGC officers who must decide which side of history they want to be on.

Scenario 4

Pahlavi Returns — Democratic Transition

Likelihood: 16% — Rising as regime fractures under strike pressure

The US–Israel strike campaign fatally weakens the Islamic Republic's military and economic foundations. The IRGC fractures — senior commanders, heeding Pahlavi's "wide tent" offer and the CIA's recruitment campaign, choose the people over the regime. Reza Pahlavi returns to Iran for the first time since 1979 to lead a 100-day transitional process toward the first free elections in the Islamic Republic's history.

✈ The Moment — Pahlavi Steps Off the Plane in Tehran

Pahlavi has publicly pledged his 100-day transition plan: a provisional government of national unity, an emergency constitutional convention, international monitors for a free referendum, and immediate dismantlement of the theocratic governance structure. He has told US media the regime is on its "last leg" and has already briefed the Trump administration's Witkoff on the transition roadmap. The CIA's Farsi-language IRGC recruitment campaign and Pahlavi's "wide tent" letter to IRGC generals are complementary operations aimed at making this scenario self-fulfilling.

What Would Trigger This

  • IRGC command fractures — senior generals accept Pahlavi's amnesty offer and stand down during mass protests
  • Khamenei death — CONFIRMED (Mar 1) — succession vacuum is now active; collapse timeline has accelerated materially
  • US/Israel strikes destroy IRGC economic nodes — removes the IRGC's financial stranglehold over the transition
  • Protesters demand Pahlavi by name — already happening since Feb 22; gives him democratic legitimacy the regime cannot counter
  • Trump administration recognition — Witkoff back-channel becomes official; White House endorses transitional council
  • Opposition coalition holds — Women Life Freedom movement, CNFI ethnic minorities, and labor unions rally around a secular democratic framework

The 100-Day Transition Roadmap

  • Days 1–10: Provisional Government of National Unity declared; Pahlavi takes role as non-partisan transitional head of state; emergency UN Security Council session
  • Days 10–30: IRGC formally dissolved or converted into a conventional military under civilian command; nuclear sites secured under IAEA and US monitoring
  • Days 30–60: Emergency constitutional convention — representatives from all provinces, ethnic minorities, women's groups, labor unions, and diaspora
  • Days 60–100: UN-monitored referendum on constitutional framework; provisional free press laws; political prisoners released
  • Post-100 days: First free parliamentary elections; international economic reconstruction packages (Gulf states, EU, US) unlock; sanctions lifted

Why This Scenario Is Now Plausible

  • Students are already chanting Pahlavi's name across all 31 provinces — unprecedented in 46 years
  • Witkoff–Pahlavi secret meeting confirmed (Axios, Jan 13) — White House has a direct channel
  • CIA Farsi-language IRGC recruitment video (Feb 24–25) explicitly targets insiders — Pahlavi's "wide tent" is the off-ramp
  • Pahlavi's Feb 26 open letter to IRGC generals framing the choice: "heroes of Iran's freedom" vs. "face justice"
  • Jared Kushner's Palm Beach meetings (Feb 5) with Iranian-American business leaders suggest the US has already begun transition planning
  • Pahlavi has explicitly rejected restoring the monarchy — his secular democratic platform makes him more broadly acceptable

Critical Obstacles

  • Opposition fragmentation — MEK's presence poisons any coalition; Kurdish federalists demand constitutional guarantees Pahlavi hasn't fully committed to
  • IRGC economic empire — the Revolutionary Guards control 60–70% of Iran's economy; dismantling this without economic collapse is a generational challenge
  • Nuclear material security — 1,000+ kg of 60–90% enriched uranium must be secured immediately; requires US military presence on Iranian soil
  • No organizational infrastructure inside Iran — Pahlavi has lived in exile since age 17; has no party, no bureaucracy, no provincial network
  • "Made in Washington" stigma — US backing could delegitimize him domestically; Iranians have painful memories of the 1953 CIA-backed coup
  • Ethnic fracture risk — Kurdish (PJAK), Baloch (Jaish al-Adl), and Arab (ASMLA) armed groups may push for secession in a power vacuum
Historical Parallel — and Why This Time May Be Different

Every previous Iranian opposition effort since 1979 has failed against the same wall: IRGC loyalty to the regime. What has changed in February 2026: (1) the IRGC's economic base is being destroyed by US/Israel strikes, removing the financial incentive for loyalty; (2) the protest movement has named a single leader for the first time — Pahlavi — giving fence-sitting commanders a face to surrender to, not just a movement; (3) the CIA has opened a direct, secure defection channel for IRGC insiders; (4) the Trump administration has indicated — via the Witkoff back-channel — that it will support a transitional government. The convergence of military pressure, popular mandate, international backing, and an IRGC off-ramp is historically unprecedented. The question is not whether these conditions exist — they do. The question is whether they occur simultaneously before the moment passes.

What a Free Iran Looks Like: The Long Game

A democratic Iran — even an imperfect one — fundamentally reshapes the Middle East. The Strait of Hormuz becomes a multilateral waterway under Iranian democratic government rather than an IRGC chokepoint. Iran's $400B+ in frozen assets and natural resources (world's second-largest proven gas reserves) become available for reconstruction. The proxy network — Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi PMF — loses its state sponsor and collapses organically. A secular Iran would almost certainly seek normalized relations with Israel, end the nuclear program voluntarily, and apply for IMF/World Bank reconstruction financing. The Iranian diaspora — 5 million people, heavily educated, concentrated in the US and Europe — would return capital and talent. This is the scenario that every US president since Carter has dreamed of and none has achieved. Operation Epic Fury, for the first time, makes it structurally possible.

Scenario 5

Prolonged Standoff — Ceasefire & Attrition

Likelihood: 0%

Strikes cause limited damage; neither side achieves a decisive outcome. International pressure mounts for a ceasefire. The conflict becomes a “frozen” exchange — Iran retaliates asymmetrically (Hormuz, proxies, cyber) while US/Israel maintain air supremacy. Talks resume under fire, but progress is measured in months, not days.

Why This Is Least Likely

Trump has personally staked his credibility on the "10-day" ultimatum. A prolonged standoff would require him to publicly back down — something his political brand makes nearly impossible. Iran, meanwhile, cannot afford to keep its military on high alert and its economy in free fall indefinitely. The economics alone force a resolution within weeks. Iran's foreign exchange reserves are estimated at under $10B accessible (the rest is frozen under US sanctions, per OFAC estimates and Congressional Research Service reporting), meaning the current burn rate makes a standoff untenable beyond April 2026.
