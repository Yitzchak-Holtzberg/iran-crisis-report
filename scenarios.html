Scenario 3

Regime Collapse — Revolution From Within

Likelihood: 73% — Most Likely Post-Strike Outcome
🔴 Day 4–5 Inflection: First Visible IRGC Fractures Reported

Three previously-watched indicators have now triggered. First: the 17th Qods Corps (Sistan-Baluchestan) has reportedly stood down in at least two garrison towns — units in the ethnically Baloch southeast, historically the IRGC's most reluctant enforcers, appear to have halted operations without orders. Second: Bank Melli payroll failed for IRGC subsidiary accounts — a one-cycle failure, but soldiers who did not receive pay in a rial at 1.7M/USD are now calculating loyalty in real time. Third: Mojtaba Khamenei has made no public appearance in 72 hours — a silence with no confirmed explanation that is being read across Iranian intelligence circles as either incapacitation, deliberate optionality preservation, or loss of effective command authority. None of these is irreversible; together, they constitute the leading edge of an avalanche. — Sources: Reuters, AP (garrison reports unconfirmed tier-4/5), Mar 3–4; Wall Street Journal, Mar 3

The Islamic Republic has always operated on what analysts call the "avalanche model" of loyalty: its power appears solid until it suddenly isn't. The regime survives because everyone believes everyone else is loyal. The moment a significant unit visibly defects — and survives — that fiction evaporates. Provincial garrison commanders who were on the fence now calculate that the regime cannot punish them all. Khamenei's death, ongoing strikes, mass protests in all 31 provinces, and economic freefall have pushed the system to the threshold. Day 4–5 data suggests that threshold is being crossed.

Remaining Signals to Watch

  • Mojtaba Khamenei reappears — or doesn't — a further 24 hours of silence pushes this from "suspicious" to "operationally significant." If he is incapacitated, the junta loses its shadow authority figure and Sc3 probability rises sharply.

  • Nuclear material security window — if collapse accelerates, the 1,000+ kg of 60–90% enriched uranium must be secured within hours. Pentagon planning teams are on a 48-hour alert posture for this contingency. — Source: IAEA background, Mar 3

The Transition Problem

  • No unified opposition command inside Iran — collapse without a pre-positioned transitional authority risks a Libya-style vacuum. Pahlavi's Prosperity Project (Sc4) is the closest ready plan but lacks organizational infrastructure inside Iran.
  • IRGC controls 60–70% of the economy — the Guards' construction, import monopolies, and pension funds involve millions of families. Dismantling this overnight destroys livelihoods, not just a power structure.
  • Nuclear material security gap — 1,000+ kg of 60–90% enriched uranium must be secured in hours, not days. If central authority collapses before US/IAEA teams move in, proliferation risk is acute. — Source: IAEA, Mar 3
  • The Mojtaba wildcard — even in a collapse scenario, if Khamenei's son retains armed loyalists in Tehran, a "collapse" risks becoming a protracted civil conflict rather than a clean revolution.
  • Historical precedent is grim: Libya 2011 collapsed and has not recovered in 13 years. Iraq 2003's institutional collapse produced a civil war. Iran has four times Iraq's population and an intact nuclear program.
The CIA's Play: Defection Channel Now Reportedly Active

The CIA's Feb 24–25 Farsi-language recruitment video was a public commitment device — broadcasting the existence of a secure defection channel (VPN, disposable device, Tor .onion portal) to signal to every IRGC officer that the option to leave exists. As of Day 4–5, the channel has reportedly received its first contact from a mid-level IRGC officer (unconfirmed, tier-5 sourcing). This matters less for the intelligence value of one mid-level officer and more for what it signals: the psychological barrier to first contact has been broken. Every officer watching will recalculate. The "wide tent" amnesty offer embedded in Pahlavi's Prosperity Project targets the same audience: there is a safe landing, if you move now. — Sources: Intelligence community background, Mar 3; CIA public recruitment video, Feb 24–25

Scenario 4

Pahlavi Returns — Democratic Transition

Likelihood: 15% — Requires ordered collapse, not chaotic one
Day 4–5: Prosperity Project Gains International Traction — Saudi $500M Confirmed

Reza Pahlavi's "Prosperity Project", unveiled March 1, has generated significant international media coverage through Day 4–5. Critically: Saudi Arabia confirmed a $500M humanitarian aid pledge contingent on a recognized transitional authority (Reuters, Mar 3) — the first concrete Gulf financial commitment to a post-regime framework. The Trump administration back-channel is developing: Witkoff's team has held a second informal contact with Pahlavi's advisers (POLITICO, Mar 3), and White House press secretary language is shifting toward "the Iranian people deserve a choice." These are early-stage signals, not commitments — but they change the international legitimacy calculus for fence-sitting IRGC commanders. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 3; POLITICO, Mar 3; Washington Post, Mar 2

⚠ Kurdish Fracture Risk: PJAK Has NOT Endorsed the Prosperity Project

As of Day 4–5, the PJAK (Party for a Free Life in Kurdistan) — the armed Kurdish opposition group in northwestern Iran — has not publicly endorsed the Prosperity Project and has not responded to outreach from Pahlavi's circle. This is a significant spoiler risk: if Kurdish armed groups do not receive binding commitments on autonomy before a transitional authority is declared, PJAK and affiliated factions may pursue their own armed agenda in the northwest during the transition window. Pahlavi's "One Iran" framing explicitly offers maximum autonomy within a unified state — but PJAK's threshold for "binding" is higher than a public framework document. This fracture, unresolved, could produce a low-grade northwest insurgency in the transition's first weeks — exactly the kind of instability that allows IRGC remnants to claim a security emergency. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; Reuters, Mar 3

This scenario requires Sc3 (collapse) to not descend into chaos — it is specifically the ordered version of regime change, where IRGC stand-down is coordinated rather than anarchic. The Prosperity Project provides the political architecture; the CIA's defection channel provides the coordination mechanism; Saudi financial commitment and the Trump back-channel provide the international scaffolding. The race is against Sc3's chaotic variant and Sc6's junta consolidation.

What Would Trigger This

  • IRGC command fractures in coordination — not chaotic defection, but 3–5 senior generals collectively announcing support for a transitional authority, removing the coordination barrier for others
  • Khamenei death — CONFIRMED (Mar 1) — succession vacuum active; no figure with equivalent authority to order resistance — Source: AP, Mar 1
  • Trump administration recognition — Witkoff back-channel becomes official; White House endorses Pahlavi's transitional council, giving it international legitimacy before it has domestic control — Source: POLITICO, Mar 3
  • Saudi $500M pledge confirmed — removes the economic collapse fear that keeps IRGC loyalists on the fence — Source: Reuters, Mar 3
  • PJAK alignment — Kurdish armed groups endorse the Prosperity Project, closing the northwest spoiler risk

Critical Obstacles

  • Mojtaba Khamenei's network — even if the formal IRGC stands down, Khamenei's son controls IRGC intelligence and Basij ideological units. He doesn't need legal authority to spoil a transition if he retains armed loyalists.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi fracture — PJAK non-endorsement is public and unresolved. Kurdish armed groups may not honor an amnesty they weren't part of negotiating. — Source: BBC, Feb 27
  • Nuclear material as leverage — whatever IRGC faction controls enriched uranium holds a decisive bargaining chip unlikely to be surrendered absent binding security guarantees and phased amnesty.
  • No party, no bureaucracy, no network — Pahlavi has lived in exile since age 17. A transitional government without a functioning civil service will rely on Islamic Republic technocrats from Day 1, creating legitimacy problems.
Historical Parallel — Five Simultaneously Unprecedented Conditions

Every previous Iranian opposition effort since 1979 failed against the same wall: IRGC loyalty to the regime. Five things have simultaneously changed in February–March 2026: (1) the IRGC's economic base is disrupted, removing the financial incentive for loyalty; (2) the protest movement has named a single leader, giving fence-sitting commanders a face to surrender to; (3) the CIA has opened a verified, operational defection channel with reportedly active contacts; (4) the US has signaled willingness to recognize a transitional government; (5) Khamenei's death removes the authority figure who could have ordered resistance regardless of material incentives. The question is whether Pahlavi and the opposition can convert conditions into a coordinated institutional move before Sc3 goes chaotic or Sc6 consolidates. — Sources: CBS/60 Minutes, Mar 3; POLITICO, Mar 3

Scenario 6

IRGC Junta — Succession Council Consolidates Power

Likelihood: 12% — Declining as IRGC fractures emerge
Day 4–5: Pezeshkian's "Leadership Council" Is the Junta's Constitutional Facade

President Masoud Pezeshkian's statement that a leadership council "has begun its work" (AP, Mar 3) is constitutionally correct under Article 131 — but in practice, the structure he is describing is the IRGC's attempt to impose constitutional legitimacy on a security-organ power seizure. The council's authority is theoretical; real control remains with whoever commands the guns. As of Day 4–5, 12 of Iran's 31 provinces have significant disruption to normal IRGC operations — a number that has doubled in 48 hours. The junta's window is closing. — Sources: AP, Mar 3; Reuters, Mar 4

⚠ The 48-Hour Window: Consolidate Now or Lose It

A junta succeeds by moving faster than the collapse dynamic. The IRGC has approximately 48 hours from the current moment to: (1) achieve visible internal consensus on a shadow leadership structure around Mojtaba Khamenei or an alternative figure; (2) impose emergency law with sufficient force to halt the protest–garrison merger; and (3) produce a public-facing constitutional narrative credible enough to split the opposition. If all three do not happen within this window, provincial commanders will individually calculate that the junta cannot protect them — and the cascade tips irrevocably toward Sc3. The 17th Qods Corps stand-down reports are the clearest signal that this window is already eroding. — Sources: Reuters, AP, Mar 3–4

🔴 Who Would Actually Run the Junta

Under Article 131, when the Supreme Leader dies, a Leadership Council of Three — the First VP, the parliament speaker, and the judiciary chief — manages the country for 50 days while the Assembly of Experts selects a successor. In theory: Mohammad Mokhber (First VP) assumes executive power. In practice, four figures hold the actual levers:

  • Mojtaba Khamenei — controls IRGC intelligence directorate and Basij ideological corps. His 72-hour public absence is the most significant datapoint in the current picture: either he is dead or injured; deliberately staying hidden to preserve optionality; or has lost effective command authority. None of these is consistent with a successful junta consolidation. — Source: Open-source monitoring, Mar 4
  • Hossein Salami (IRGC Commander-in-Chief) — hardline ideologue, commands the military apparatus. Would be the junta's enforcer, not its public face; lacks clerical legitimacy to serve as Supreme Leader. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2
  • Esmail Qaani (Quds Force Chief) — focused on preserving external proxy networks; priority is keeping Houthis and Iraqi PMF funded to maintain external leverage even if the home front fractures. — Source: ISW, Mar 2
  • Ahmad Jannati (Guardian Council chief) — provides clerical cover for constitutional legitimacy; Assembly of Experts could theoretically be managed toward a Mojtaba-aligned outcome. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2

Most likely junta structure if it coalesces: Mokhber as constitutional president (figurehead); Mojtaba as shadow Supreme Leader (if he reappears); Salami commanding security; Qaani preserving external leverage. — Sources: Reuters, NBC News, Carnegie Endowment, Mar 1–3

This scenario is Sc3's dark mirror: the same conditions that could produce collapse could instead produce consolidation, if the IRGC acts faster than the protest movement. The 48-hour window analysis above suggests the IRGC is losing that race — but a single decisive move (Mojtaba reappearing, a mass arrest of protest leaders, or a dramatic proxy escalation) could reverse the momentum.

The Mojtaba Factor — Day 4–5 Update

  • Mojtaba's 72-hour public absence is the defining intelligence gap. His assets: IRGC intelligence files on every senior commander, Basij mobilization capability, cultivated loyalty networks. His liabilities: zero public legitimacy, no constitutional role, and every opposition faction sees him as the corrupt dynastic succession the revolution was against.
  • If he reappears and moves publicly to claim succession authority, it triggers the factional war that makes Sc3 inevitable. If he remains absent, the junta has no shadow authority — and Sc3 probability rises further. There is no middle path for him.
  • Russia and China have incentives to back a junta that maintains continuity — chaos in Iran threatens their energy and trade relationships. Expect quiet signaling of support for Mokhber/Mojtaba continuity, but neither will commit publicly to a losing bet. — Source: Reuters, Mar 3

Why the Junta Is Failing

  • IRGC fractional math — the IRGC's six independent corps (Aerospace, Ground Forces, Navy, Quds, Intelligence, Basij) are competing fiefdoms. A junta requires all six to agree on who is in charge — under wartime pressure and with payroll failing.
  • 12/31 provinces disrupted — provincial operational disruption has doubled in 48 hours. A junta that cannot project authority into 12 provinces by Day 5 is not consolidating; it is contracting. — Source: Reuters, Mar 4
  • Economic collapse accelerates under junta — rial slide, disrupted oil exports, and isolation worsen immediately. — Source: Reuters, Mar 2
  • International rejection — a Mojtaba-backed junta faces rapid non-recognition and expanded sanctions from the US/EU/UK. No financial lifeline beyond Russia/China, which will be cautious.
If the Junta Somehow Consolidates: Consequences

A stable IRGC junta — if it achieves internal cohesion within the remaining 48-hour window — would: impose emergency law and communications blackout; detain prominent protest leaders; condition IAEA access on sanctions relief; activate proxies (PMF, Houthis) to signal deterrence; and seek recognition from Russia/China. Nuclear risk rises sharply if enriched material survives and weaponization accelerates under existential threat logic. — Sources: IAEA (access statements), Mar 3; ISW, Mar 2

The strikes continue and are consequential, but they are now a backdrop to the political transition dynamics unfolding in Sc3, Sc4, and Sc6. The "Pickaxe Mountain" BDA: Day 4–5 Status

Iran's deep centrifuge facility south of Natanz — potentially buried at 80+ meters, beyond the GBU‑57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator's effective penetration depth in hardened rock — remains the decisive unresolved question. As of Day 4–5: (1) IAEA access has been requested but not yet granted; (2) no ELINT confirmation of centrifuge spin-down; (3) no political declaration of program destruction from Washington. A surviving site implies rapid 90% enrichment reconstitution within months — which would mean this strike campaign has degraded but not eliminated Iran's nuclear capability, and a Phase III cycle becomes more likely. — Sources: IAEA, Mar 3; CSIS, Mar 2–3

Scenario 1

Diplomatic Deal — JCPOA 3.0

Likelihood: 0% — Eliminated by strikes and succession vacuum

Iran's pre-strike Oman-mediated talks had produced real progress — including a reported "breakthrough" agreement in principle on zero uranium stockpiling (Mar 2). That channel is now frozen. The IRGC junta consolidation attempt has made any commitment impossible: no single authority can speak for Iran, bind the IRGC, or absorb the domestic cost of a concession-based agreement under active bombardment. The Oman channel's agreement in principle has no counterparty who can honor it. — Sources: Reuters, Feb 26–Mar 2; NYT, Mar 1

The Oman "Zero Enrichment" Agreement: Frozen, Not Dead

The Mar 2 Oman-brokered agreement in principle on zero uranium stockpiling — the most significant diplomatic progress in years — is now frozen for at least two reasons: (1) Succession vacuum: no Iranian authority has the political standing to ratify any commitment; Pezeshkian's "leadership council" is constitutionally provisional and militarily controlled, making any enrichment commitment politically impossible. (2) IRGC consolidation dynamics: any IRGC faction that accepted enrichment limits under active bombardment would be signing its own death warrant politically. A post-conflict arrangement could emerge in 6–18 months — but it would be terms dictated to a defeated adversary, not a negotiated JCPOA analog. This scenario is archived, not merely paused. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 2; NYT, Mar 1; AP, Mar 3

Scenario 5

Prolonged Standoff — Ceasefire & Attrition

Likelihood: 0% — Eliminated by the pace of events

A frozen standoff required that strikes produce limited damage and neither side achieve a decisive outcome — allowing international pressure to mount for a ceasefire. Four events by Day 4–5 have eliminated this path: Khamenei confirmed dead (no authority to authorize a ceasefire); Iran fired ~170 ballistic missiles with 90%+ intercepted (neither side can claim limited engagement); war-risk re-pricing has effectively throttled Hormuz flows (the economic clock is running); and IRGC fracture signals in 12/31 provinces mean the "regime holds steady at a lower level" premise no longer holds. The situation is resolving in weeks, not months. — Sources: AP, Mar 1; Reuters, Mar 1–4; CNBC, Mar 3

Why the Economics Foreclosed This Scenario

A standoff requires both sides to absorb the cost of indefinite confrontation. Iran's accessible foreign exchange is limited; disrupted crude flows intensify fiscal stress rapidly. Washington faces domestic pressure to show rapid results with US casualties now confirmed. The global economy cannot absorb a 90-day Hormuz disruption — creating pressure from Europe, China, India, and Japan on all parties. This resolves in weeks, not months. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 2; CNBC, Mar 3
