SC1

Declared Victory — "Mission Accomplished" Drawdown

Likelihood: 23%
Day 20: CSIS Base Case — Campaign Nears Declared End; USS Ford Withdrawal Complicates Timeline

CSIS's base case remains: the military campaign ends by late March after degrading Iran's power projection and defense industrial base, without regime change. Trump declares victory, forces draw down to a deterrence posture. Markets continue pricing this outcome. However, the USS Gerald R. Ford's withdrawal for repairs (Mar 18) temporarily reduces carrier posture, and Iran's sustained retaliatory campaign — including strikes after Larijani's killing — means the "victory" narrative remains contested. The regime survives in weakened form; sanctions stay until nuclear/missile concessions materialize. — Sources: CSIS, Mar 2026; Axios, Mar 10

This remains the path of least resistance: U.S. declares strategic objectives achieved (degraded military, destroyed nuclear infrastructure, eliminated senior IRGC leadership), pivots to containment, and avoids occupation. Iran limps along under tightened sanctions, Mojtaba or successor consolidates with dramatically diminished capacity. The IDF's 200+ strikes on Mar 19 and Caspian naval neutralization demonstrate the coalition continues degrading targets at scale.

What Makes This Likely

  • Domestic political incentive — Trump can claim historic military success; mounting US casualties (13 killed — 7 by enemy fire, 6 in a KC-135 crash; ~140 wounded) create pressure to wrap up. — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Mission scope achieved — 15,000+ targets struck; IRGC leadership decimated (Larijani, Soleimani, Khatib killed); Caspian naval assets destroyed Mar 19. — Sources: CENTCOM; Jerusalem Post, Mar 19
  • Oil market pressure — Brent above $100; US gas at $3.72/gal; economic blowback is mounting. — Source: CNN, Mar 15
  • No appetite for occupation — RAND's "Iraq analogy" warns against extended engagement. — Source: RAND, Mar 2026

What Could Prevent It

  • Iran refuses to stop retaliating — heavy strikes after Larijani killing (Mar 18); FM Araghchi: "we never asked for a ceasefire; ready to defend as long as it takes." — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Carrier posture weakened — USS Ford withdrawal to Crete reduces available strike capacity; declares victory while under fire is politically risky. — Source: CNN, Mar 18
  • Nuclear program not fully neutralized — IAEA disclosed new Isfahan complex; ~440 kg enriched uranium unaccounted for; White House weighing seizure operation. — Sources: CSIS Nuclear Network; AP/Reuters, Mar 18
  • Hormuz still disrupted — declaring victory while tanker traffic is halted undermines the narrative.
SC2

Negotiated Deal — Ceasefire + Nuclear Concessions

Likelihood: 31%
Day 20: Iran's Retaliation After Larijani Killing Signals No Imminent Deal

Iran's heavy retaliatory strikes following the Mar 17 killing of Ali Larijani (Mar 18) demonstrate that diplomatic off-ramps remain publicly foreclosed. Oman, Turkey, and Egypt continue quiet mediation efforts but public postures on both sides have hardened. The IAEA nuclear disclosures (new Isfahan complex, ~440 kg of 60% HEU) further complicate verification requirements for any deal — raising the bar that must be cleared for a negotiated settlement. — Sources: CNBC, Mar 18; Times of Israel, Mar 15

A negotiated deal would involve a ceasefire, nuclear/missile program concessions (IAEA access, enrichment caps, surrender of the newly disclosed Isfahan complex), and phased sanctions relief. The killing of senior leaders on both sides of the Iranian hierarchy makes identifying a credible negotiating partner more difficult — yet China's continued oil purchases (WSJ, Mar 18) give Beijing leverage to broker. CSIS's oil-disruption Scenario A: a quick deal within 4 weeks, Brent settles to ~$70.

Key Triggers

  • Oman/Turkey mediation success — back-channel produces framework agreement despite Iran's public retaliation posture.
  • Oil price pressure — sustained $100+ oil forces Trump to seek a deal that reopens Hormuz. — Source: Fortune, Mar 15
  • IAEA access offer — Iran trades inspection access to Isfahan complex and known enriched-uranium sites for a halt to strikes.
  • China broker role — Beijing, buying nearly all Iranian crude, facilitates face-saving deal framework. — Source: WSJ, Mar 18

Critical Obstacles

  • Iran's retaliatory posture — heavy strikes launched after Larijani killing; "never asked for a ceasefire" stance reinforced by action, not just rhetoric. — Source: CNBC, Mar 18
  • Regime legitimacy crisis — Mojtaba status uncertain; intelligence minister reportedly killed; unclear who can credibly negotiate.
  • Verification gap widened — new Isfahan complex means verification scope just expanded; IAEA monitoring near-zero since June 2025. — Source: ISIS, Feb 2026
  • Israeli opposition — Israel struck 200+ targets on Mar 19 alone; no sign of restraint that would accompany deal pursuit. — Source: Times of Israel, Mar 19
SC3

Democratic Revolution — Popular Uprising Succeeds

Likelihood: 19%
Day 20 — NAWRUZ: All Cascade Precursors Active; Payroll Crisis Decision Point Reached

March 20 is Nawruz — the most significant cultural and financial date in Iran's calendar. The Bank Sepah data center was destroyed Mar 10–11, disrupting IRGC payroll processing. The regime faced a critical test to demonstrate it could pay its security forces. Intelligence Minister Khatib — the person responsible for detecting and suppressing internal defections — is reportedly dead (Mar 18–19). The "avalanche model" of loyalty may be approaching its inflection point: the first visible defection that survives retaliation. Reza Pahlavi's "Prosperity Project" remains the most articulated post-regime framework. — Sources: Axios, Mar 10; POLITICO, Mar 3; Jerusalem Post, Mar 11

SC3 succeeds only if collapse is coordinated: multiple IRGC commanders pivot together, an interim authority secures clerical neutrality, and external partners underwrite nuclear security. As of Mar 19, six simultaneous pressures are active: (1) IRGC finances under acute pressure with payroll uncertain; (2) opposition has a focal leader (Pahlavi); (3) defection channel reportedly exists; (4) U.S. signaling more permissive; (5) Khamenei's death removed ultimate authority; (6) intelligence ministry reportedly decapitated — the entity that detects and punishes defections.

Key Triggers

  • Nawruz payroll failure (Mar 20) — DECISION POINT — if IRGC/Basij personnel are not paid on Nawruz, it is the most concrete signal of financial collapse and the likely catalyst for the first general-rank defection. — Sources: Jerusalem Post, Mar 11; Polemology, Mar 2026
  • First general-rank defection — the catalytic signal flipping provincial command calculus; now more likely without the intelligence ministry enforcing loyalty.
  • Kurdish alignment — PJAK/Komala endorse a unified framework. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; POLITICO, Mar 8
  • U.S. recognition signal — back-channel becomes formal endorsement of transitional authority.
  • Mojtaba confirmed dead/incapacitated — removes the successor around whom loyalists coalesce; intelligence reporting suggests he may already be incapacitated.

Critical Obstacles

  • No unified opposition command inside Iran — unmanaged collapse risks a Libya-style vacuum; Khatib's death removes an obstacle but does not create coordination.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi gap — PJAK demands no-fly zone as precondition; non-endorsement persists. — Source: POLITICO, Mar 8
  • Nuclear material security — ~440 kg of 60% HEU at newly disclosed Isfahan complex and other sites must be secured; White House weighing covert seizure operation. — Sources: CFR, Mar 9; AP/Reuters, Mar 18
  • IRGC economic capture (60–70%) — dismantling the Guards' empire endangers livelihoods alongside power structures.

SC4

Managed Transition — Venezuela Model

Likelihood: 15%
Day 20: Leadership Vacuum Opens Pragmatist Window — But Who Negotiates?

Brookings analysts note the US could replicate the Venezuela playbook: strike a deal with Mojtaba or a post-Mojtaba successor, offering sanctions relief and regime survival in exchange for nuclear concessions and regional de-escalation. The reported deaths of Ali Larijani, Gholamreza Soleimani, and Intelligence Minister Khatib (Mar 17–19) create a leadership vacuum that could allow a pragmatist faction to emerge — but also makes it deeply unclear who holds authority to negotiate. Unlike SC2 (full deal), this path preserves the Islamic Republic's structure with new, more compliant leadership. Unlike SC1 (declared victory), this path secures concrete concessions. — Source: Brookings, Mar 2026

The Venezuela model works when the regime is too strong to topple but too weak to resist concessions. Current conditions may fit: Mojtaba's junta retains coercive tools but faces economic freefall, lost oil revenue, and degraded military capacity — and the intelligence apparatus that would detect and suppress any pragmatist faction has reportedly just been decapitated. A pragmatist IRGC economic faction could emerge willing to trade nuclear/missile programs for survival, particularly if the Nawruz payroll crisis (Mar 20) fractures internal cohesion.

Key Triggers

  • Pragmatist faction emerges — Khatib's reported death removed the hardline intelligence enforcer; IRGC economic interests may push commanders toward a deal. — Source: BBC, Mar 18–19
  • U.S. casualties mount — 13 killed (7 enemy fire, 6 KC-135 crash) and ~140 wounded create political pressure for a faster exit. — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Mojtaba incapacitated — a successor more amenable to negotiation takes over; intelligence and reporting point to incapacitation already. — Source: Fox News, Mar 15
  • China/Russia broker — Beijing (buying nearly all Iranian crude) or Moscow offer to facilitate face-saving framework. — Source: WSJ, Mar 18

Critical Obstacles

  • No clear interlocutor — Larijani, Soleimani, and Khatib all reportedly killed in 72 hours; it is unclear who holds enough authority to credibly negotiate on behalf of the regime. — Sources: Haaretz; NYT, Mar 17–19
  • IRGC hardliners — nuclear program concessions threaten existential deterrent; hardliner faction may resist despite Khatib's removal.
  • Verification challenge — IAEA access near-zero since June 2025; new Isfahan complex just disclosed, widening the verification scope. — Source: ISIS, Feb 2026
  • Domestic backlash — both US and Iranian publics may reject a deal that preserves the regime responsible for the current conflict.
SC5

Regime Collapse — Chaotic Implosion

Likelihood: 12%
Day 20: Intelligence Ministry Decapitation Dramatically Raises Fragmentation Risk

SC5 is the chaotic version of SC3: collapse outpaces political organization. The reported killing of Intelligence Minister Khatib (Mar 18–19) removes the primary enforcer of internal loyalty — the entity that detects defections, punishes dissent, and maintains coercive coherence across the IRGC and Basij. Combined with the Nawruz payroll crisis (Mar 20), escalating desertions, and Mojtaba's uncertain status, the conditions for uncoordinated fragmentation are more acute now than at any previous point in the war. RAND's "Iraq analogy" is the governing risk: a militarily weakened state that fragments rather than transitions. Nuclear material custodianship becomes contested across multiple actors. — Sources: RAND, Mar 2026; Axios, Mar 10

A 90M-person state fragmenting under sanctions, war, and supply collapse produces outsized humanitarian harm. Regional spillover is likely: proxy networks lose central coordination, ethnic flashpoints activate, and nuclear security — with ~440 kg of 60% enriched uranium now confirmed at a new underground Isfahan complex — becomes the urgent international crisis.

Fragmentation Flashpoints

  • Kurdistan / Northwest (PJAK) — no Prosperity Project endorsement; NFZ precondition; operational prep reported. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; POLITICO, Mar 8
  • Sistan-Baluchestan — Mobarizoun Popular Front (Baloch militia coalition) attacked law enforcement in Zahedan (Mar 3); provincial garrison control thinning. — Source: Critical Threats, Mar 4
  • Khuzestan (Arab Ahvazi groups) — oil infrastructure becomes a prize amid Hormuz disruption.
  • Mojtaba loyalist redoubt — Basij/IRGC-Intel cadres sustain urban insurgency post-collapse; Khatib's death may reduce their command coherence but also their restraint.

Nuclear Proliferation Risk

  • ~440 kg of 60% enriched uranium at risk — IAEA confirmed new underground Isfahan complex (Mar 18); White House weighing covert seizure operation. Fragmentation could see this material compete among multiple actors. — Sources: CFR, Mar 9; AP/Reuters, Mar 18
  • No interlocutor — secure-and-remove planning assumes a state to negotiate with; senior leadership has now been stripped away (Larijani, Soleimani, Khatib).
  • External accelerants — Russia/China hedge across factions; Gulf states may back regional proxies.
  • ISIS estimate: ~50% medium-term breakout probability — chaotic conditions raise this further. — Source: ISIS, Feb 2026
What Prevents Fragmentation

Prevention requires: (1) coordinated IRGC stand-down to a single transitional authority (SC3 path); (2) binding autonomy guarantees for Kurdish/Baloch/Ahvazi groups before collapse; (3) rapid U.S./IAEA nuclear security response — critical given the Isfahan disclosure; (4) Mojtaba neutralization or confirmed incapacitation. If the Nawruz payroll crisis (Mar 20) triggers uncoordinated defections rather than a pivoting-in-unison cascade, SC5 is what SC3 becomes. — Sources: Carnegie; Reuters, Mar 3–19
