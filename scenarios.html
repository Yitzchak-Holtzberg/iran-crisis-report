SC1

Declared Victory — "Mission Accomplished" Drawdown

Likelihood: 35%
Day 15: CSIS Base Case — High-Intensity Campaign Ends, Regime Weakened But Intact

CSIS's base case: the military campaign ends by late March after degrading Iran's power projection and defense industrial base, without regime change. Trump declares victory, forces draw down to a deterrence posture. Markets are betting on this outcome. The regime survives in weakened form; sanctions stay in place until nuclear/missile concessions materialize. — Sources: CSIS, Mar 2026; Axios, Mar 10

This is the path of least resistance: U.S. declares strategic objectives achieved (degraded military, destroyed nuclear infrastructure), pivots to containment, and avoids the quagmire of occupation or prolonged engagement. Iran limps along under tightened sanctions, Mojtaba or a successor consolidates with diminished capacity.

What Makes This Likely

  • Domestic political incentive — Trump can claim the biggest military success since Bin Laden; mounting US casualties (13 killed — 7 by enemy fire, 6 in a KC-135 crash; ~140 wounded) create pressure to wrap up. — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Mission scope achieved — 15,000+ targets struck; IRGC C2 degraded; Kharg Island military facilities destroyed. — Sources: CENTCOM; Ynet, Mar 13
  • Oil market pressure — Brent above $100; US gas at $3.72/gal; economic blowback is mounting. — Source: CNN, Mar 15
  • No appetite for occupation — RAND's "Iraq analogy" warns against extended engagement. — Source: RAND, Mar 2026

What Could Prevent It

  • Iran refuses to stop retaliating — FM Araghchi: "we never asked for a ceasefire; ready to defend as long as it takes." — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Proxy attacks continue post-drawdown — Hezbollah, PMF, Houthis still active; declaring victory while under fire is politically risky.
  • Nuclear program not fully neutralized — CSIS debate: "disruption or dismantlement?" Fordow underground facility may survive. — Source: CSIS Nuclear Network
  • Hormuz still disrupted — declaring victory while tanker traffic is halted undermines the narrative.
SC2

Negotiated Deal — Ceasefire + Nuclear Concessions

Likelihood: 20%
Day 15: Back-Channel Diplomacy Active; Iran Publicly Rejects Ceasefire

Regional intermediaries (Oman, Turkey, Egypt) are pushing for a diplomatic end; back-channel U.S.–Iran talks are reportedly possible. Israel–Lebanon ceasefire talks expected with French mediation, with Hezbollah disarmament on the table. However, Iran's FM publicly rejected a ceasefire. The gap between public posturing and private channels is characteristic of Middle East negotiations. — Sources: Haaretz, Mar 14; Times of Israel, Mar 15

A negotiated deal would involve a ceasefire, nuclear/missile program concessions (IAEA access, enrichment caps), and phased sanctions relief. Brookings notes the "Venezuela model" could morph into a deal if Washington decides regime change is too costly. CSIS's oil-disruption Scenario A: a quick deal within 4 weeks, Brent settles to ~$70.

Key Triggers

  • Oman/Turkey mediation success — back-channel produces framework agreement; both sides need a face-saving off-ramp.
  • Oil price pressure — sustained $100+ oil forces Trump to seek a deal that reopens Hormuz. UK considering "any options" for Strait security. — Source: Fortune, Mar 15
  • IAEA access offer — Iran trades inspection access for sanctions relief and a halt to strikes.
  • Hezbollah disarmament track — Lebanon deal as a template for broader regional settlement. — Source: Times of Israel, Mar 15

Critical Obstacles

  • Iran's public stance — "never asked for a ceasefire" — any deal requires saving face for a regime under bombardment.
  • Regime legitimacy — Mojtaba's contested succession makes it unclear who can credibly negotiate.
  • Verification gap — near-zero IAEA monitoring since June 2025; rebuilding verification takes months. — Source: ISIS, Feb 2026
  • Israeli opposition — Israel still has "thousands of targets" and may resist a deal that leaves the regime intact. — Source: Times of Israel, Mar 15
SC3

Democratic Revolution — Popular Uprising Succeeds

Likelihood: 18%
Day 15: Cascade Precursors Persist; Prosperity Project Framework Active

The Islamic Republic runs on an "avalanche model" of loyalty: cohesion appears monolithic until a visible defection survives retaliation. Reza Pahlavi's "Prosperity Project" remains the most articulated post-regime framework. Axios identifies "regime collapse" as one of five endgame paths, noting the economy has collapsed and the largest demonstrations since 1979 are occurring. This scenario is the ordered variant where collapse leads to democratic governance. — Sources: Axios, Mar 10; POLITICO, Mar 3

SC3 succeeds only if collapse is coordinated: multiple IRGC commanders pivot together, an interim authority secures clerical neutrality, and external partners underwrite nuclear security. Five simultaneous shifts persist: (1) IRGC finances under acute pressure; (2) opposition has a focal leader; (3) defection channel reportedly exists; (4) U.S. signaling more permissive; (5) Khamenei's death removed the ultimate authority figure.

Key Triggers

  • First general-rank defection — the catalytic signal flipping provincial command calculus.
  • Nawruz payroll crisis (Mar 20) — US-Israeli cyberattack destroyed Bank Sepah's data center (Mar 10–11), disrupting IRGC salary processing; analysts doubt the regime can make the critical Nawruz payday. — Sources: Jerusalem Post, Mar 11; Polemology, Mar 2026
  • Kurdish alignment — PJAK/Komala endorse a unified framework. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; POLITICO, Mar 8
  • U.S. recognition signal — back-channel becomes formal endorsement of transitional authority.
  • Mojtaba confirmed dead/incapacitated — removes the successor around whom loyalists coalesce.

Critical Obstacles

  • No unified opposition command inside Iran — unmanaged collapse risks a Libya-style vacuum.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi gap — PJAK demands no-fly zone as precondition; non-endorsement persists. — Source: POLITICO, Mar 8
  • Nuclear material security — HEU/LEU stockpiles must be secured in a fluid environment. — Sources: CFR, Mar 9
  • IRGC economic capture (60–70%) — dismantling the Guards' empire endangers livelihoods alongside power structures.
Cascade Indicators
  • Widespread desertions — CONFIRMED. 14% desertion rate in western Artesh units; group desertions documented; some units down to 20 rounds per 2 soldiers. Basij commanders faking their own deaths. — Sources: Iran International, Mar 12; Israel National News
  • Bank Sepah data center destroyed — CONFIRMED (Mar 10–11). IRGC payroll processing disrupted; Nawruz payday (Mar 20) in doubt. — Sources: Jerusalem Post, Mar 11; Polemology
  • Mojtaba succession — LEGITIMACY CRISIS ACTIVE; status uncertain per Trump statement. — Sources: Reuters, Mar 3; Fox News, Mar 15
SC4

Managed Transition — Venezuela Model

Likelihood: 15%
Day 15: Brookings Flags the Maduro Parallel

Brookings analysts note the US could replicate the Venezuela playbook: strike a deal with Mojtaba or a post-Mojtaba successor, offering sanctions relief and regime survival in exchange for nuclear concessions and regional de-escalation. Unlike SC2 (full deal), this path preserves the Islamic Republic's structure with a new, more compliant leadership. Unlike SC1 (declared victory), this path secures concrete concessions. — Source: Brookings, Mar 2026

The Venezuela model works when the regime is too strong to topple but too weak to resist concessions. Current conditions may fit: Mojtaba's junta retains coercive tools but faces economic freefall, lost oil revenue, and degraded military capacity. A pragmatic faction could emerge willing to trade nuclear/missile programs for survival.

Key Triggers

  • Pragmatist faction emerges — IRGC economic interests push commanders toward a deal over continued destruction.
  • U.S. casualties mount — 13 killed (7 enemy fire, 6 KC-135 crash) and ~140 wounded create political pressure for a faster exit. — Source: NPR, Mar 15
  • Mojtaba incapacitated — a successor more amenable to negotiation takes over.
  • China/Russia broker — Beijing or Moscow offer to facilitate as face-saving intermediary.

Critical Obstacles

  • IRGC hardliners — concessions on nuclear program threaten existential deterrent; faction opposed to any deal.
  • No Maduro equivalent — Mojtaba's status is uncertain; unclear who would negotiate on behalf of the regime.
  • Verification challenge — same as SC2; IAEA monitoring gap since June 2025.
  • Domestic backlash — both US and Iranian publics may reject a deal that preserves the regime responsible for the current conflict.
SC5

Regime Collapse — Chaotic Implosion

Likelihood: 12%
Day 15: The Worst-Case Variant — SC3 Without Coordination

SC5 is the chaotic version of SC3: collapse outpaces political organization. IRGC corps do not pivot in unison, ethnic militias assert local control, and Mojtaba-aligned loyalists wage urban insurgency. RAND warns of the "Iraq analogy" — a militarily weakened state that fragments rather than transitions. Nuclear material custodianship becomes contested across multiple actors. — Sources: RAND, Mar 2026; Axios, Mar 10

A 90M-person state fragmenting under sanctions, war, and supply collapse produces outsized humanitarian harm. Regional spillover is likely: proxy networks lose central coordination, ethnic flashpoints activate, and nuclear security becomes the urgent international crisis.

Fragmentation Flashpoints

  • Kurdistan / Northwest (PJAK) — no Prosperity Project endorsement; NFZ precondition; operational prep reported. — Sources: BBC, Feb 27; POLITICO, Mar 8
  • Sistan-Baluchestan — Mobarizoun Popular Front (Baloch militia coalition) attacked law enforcement in Zahedan (Mar 3); provincial garrison control thinning. — Source: Critical Threats, Mar 4
  • Khuzestan (Arab Ahvazi groups) — oil infrastructure becomes a prize amid Hormuz disruption.
  • Mojtaba loyalist redoubt — Basij/IRGC-Intel cadres sustain urban insurgency post-collapse.

Nuclear Proliferation Risk

  • Dispersed stockpiles — IAEA access unresolved; HEU/LEU at multiple sites risks multi-actor competition. — Source: CFR, Mar 9
  • No interlocutor — secure-and-remove planning assumes a state to negotiate with; fragmentation removes it.
  • External accelerants — Russia/China hedge across factions; Gulf states may back regional proxies.
  • ISIS estimate: ~50% medium-term breakout probability — chaotic conditions raise this further. — Source: ISIS, Feb 2026
What Prevents Fragmentation

Prevention requires: (1) coordinated IRGC stand-down to a single transitional authority (SC3 path); (2) binding autonomy guarantees for Kurdish/Baloch/Ahvazi groups before collapse; (3) rapid U.S./IAEA nuclear security; (4) Mojtaba neutralization. If these fail, SC5 is what SC3 becomes. — Sources: Carnegie; Reuters, Mar 3–14
