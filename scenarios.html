Scenario 3

Regime Collapse — Revolution From Within

Likelihood: 42% — Most Likely Post-Strike Outcome
🔴 Inflection Point — Khamenei Confirmed Dead (Mar 1)

Khamenei's death removes the single node through which all IRGC factional disputes were ultimately arbitrated. For 35 years, his authority was the answer to the question "who decides?" — on military escalation, on nuclear negotiation, on which commander gets which contract. That answer no longer exists. Every IRGC faction, every provincial commander, every senior cleric is now simultaneously calculating: Is the regime salvageable? And if not, what do I do with my weapons? — Sources: Reuters, AP, NBC News, Mar 1

The Islamic Republic has always operated on what analysts call the "avalanche model" of loyalty: its power appears solid until it suddenly isn't. The regime survives because everyone believes everyone else is loyal. The moment a significant unit visibly defects — and survives — that fiction evaporates. Provincial garrison commanders who were on the fence now calculate that the regime cannot punish them all. The cascade begins. Khamenei's death, combined with ongoing strikes, mass protests in all 31 provinces, and economic freefall, has pushed the system to the edge of that threshold.

The Signals to Watch

  • IRGC 32nd Corps (Kurdistan) and 17th Qods Corps (Sistan-Baluchestan) — provincial units in ethnic minority regions have the lowest morale after months of ordering fire on their own communities. These are the units most likely to stand down first.
  • Bank Melli payroll failure — Iran's largest state bank handles government employee salaries, including IRGC. If soldiers' paychecks fail for even one cycle with the rial at 1.7M/USD, units that were holding will recalculate loyalty within hours.
  • Mojtaba Khamenei moves publicly — The Supreme Leader's 54-year-old second son controls IRGC intelligence and the Basij ideological apparatus. If he attempts to publicly claim succession authority, it triggers the factional war (Sc3). If he recedes into the shadows, a junta forms around him instead (Sc6). His next public move is the single most important signal.
  • First IRGC general contacts CIA channel — The Tor (.onion) portal and recruitment video are live. The first confirmed defection of a general-rank officer will be the visible cascade trigger that convinces others the system is collapsing.
  • Protest–military merger — Post-strike protests are now merging with anti-war sentiment, reaching IRGC families. When wives and mothers of soldiers begin joining demonstrations, the calculation for provincial commanders changes fundamentally.

The Transition Problem

  • No unified opposition command — collapse without a pre-positioned transitional authority risks a Libya-style vacuum. Pahlavi's Prosperity Project (see Sc4) is the closest thing to a ready plan, but it has no organizational infrastructure inside Iran.
  • IRGC controls 60–70% of the economy — the Revolutionary Guards' construction companies, import monopolies, and pension funds involve millions of families. Dismantling this overnight destroys livelihoods, not just a power structure.
  • Nuclear material security gap — 1,000+ kg of 60–90% enriched uranium must be secured within hours, not days. If central authority collapses before US/IAEA teams can move in, proliferation risk is acute. This is the scenario that keeps Pentagon planners awake.
  • The Mojtaba wildcard — Even in a collapse scenario, Khamenei's son likely has the weapons and intelligence networks to contest any transition. A "collapse" that doesn't neutralize him risks becoming a protracted civil war, not a clean revolution.
  • Historical precedent is grim: Libya 2011 collapsed in eight months and has not recovered in 13 years. Iraq 2003's institutional collapse produced a civil war that killed 150,000+ civilians. Iran has four times Iraq's population and an intact nuclear program.
The CIA's Play: Accelerating the Cascade

The CIA's Feb 24–25 Farsi-language recruitment video was not intelligence-gathering theater — it was a public commitment device. By broadcasting the existence of a secure defection channel (VPN outside Iran/Russia/China, disposable device, Tor .onion portal), the Agency signaled to every IRGC officer that the option to leave exists. This matters because one of the key barriers to defection is not ideology but coordination: officers won't defect if they believe they'll defect alone. The video solves the coordination problem. If senior IRGC commanders begin defecting in the next 72 hours — even one or two high-profile names — the cascade psychology takes over. The "wide tent" offer from Pahlavi targets the same audience with the same logic: there is a safe landing, if you move now.

Scenario 2

Military Strikes — "Operation Epic Fury" Phase II

⚡ IN PROGRESS
⚡ Phase II — Strikes Continue, Day 2 (Mar 1)

Strikes entered Day 2 on March 1. Trump confirmed "major combat operations" are ongoing. Iran retaliated with ~170 ballistic missiles — roughly 11% of its estimated 1,500-missile inventory — targeting Israel and US regional bases. The 28% probability represents the narrow path in which strikes achieve decisive nuclear degradation, the regime survives in some form, but a controlled political outcome emerges — without tipping into Sc3 (collapse), Sc4 (democratic transition), or Sc6 (junta). This is the "technically succeeded, politically ambiguous" scenario. — Sources: Reuters, USNI, Feb 28–Mar 1

The central analytical question for Phase II is not military — it is political. The US military can almost certainly sustain air superiority over Iran indefinitely. The harder question is whether the strikes produce a political outcome that Trump can declare a "win," that Iran can respond to without regime-ending humiliation, and that the international community can tolerate. These three conditions have never simultaneously existed in the history of US confrontation with Iran.

Phase I: What Was Hit, What Is Unknown

  • Confirmed struck: Multiple IRGC Ground Force HQ buildings; Parchin surface-level structures (concrete-soil shield targeted directly); air defense radar nodes across Tehran approaches; IRGC command bunkers in western Tehran
  • Struck, BDA pending: Natanz enrichment hall (surface structures visible, deep centrifuge arrays uncertain); Isfahan nuclear research center; IRGC Aerospace Force command bunker
  • "Pickaxe Mountain" — unconfirmed: The new deep centrifuge facility south of Natanz is the single most important target. Its depth (estimated 80+ meters) may have exceeded the GBU-57's effective penetration. The BDA will take 24–48 hours. See callout below.
  • Khamenei eliminated — leadership strike succeeded; succession vacuum now active and feeding Sc3/Sc6 dynamics

Phase II: Analytical Positions (Not Open Questions)

  • Iran's next missile salvo will be more targeted, not larger. The 170-missile saturation attempt failed to penetrate Israeli/US air defenses. Expect the IRGC Aerospace Force's next attempt to prioritize specific high-value targets: USS Abraham Lincoln, Haifa port, Saudi Aramco Abqaiq. Iran knows saturation failed — precision is the next tactical evolution.
  • Hormuz closure is already underway economically. With major traders suspended, the disruption is real regardless of whether the strait is physically mined. The strategic petroleum reserve release buys 30–45 days. After Day 10, Asian buyers (China, India, South Korea, Japan) begin hoarding alternate supply — and the psychological closure is complete even before the IRGC fires a mine.
  • With Khamenei dead, who authorizes the next Iranian strike? IRGC Aerospace operates semi-autonomously, but major escalation requires political authorization. Mokhber (First VP, constitutional successor) may lack the authority to order it; Mojtaba may not be publicly legitimate. This creates a 48–72 hour escalation pause — a potential diplomatic window, if anyone is looking for one.
The "Pickaxe Mountain" BDA: The 72-Hour Tell

Iran's deep centrifuge facility south of Natanz may be buried at 80+ meters — beyond the GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator's effective penetration depth of ~60 meters in hardened rock. The intelligence community's answer will arrive within 72 hours via three channels: (1) If Iran blocks IAEA emergency access requests, the facility likely survived; (2) If US electronic intelligence picks up centrifuge spin-up acoustic signatures within 30 days, the facility is intact; (3) If Trump publicly declares the nuclear program "destroyed," treat it as a political statement, not a verified fact. A surviving Pickaxe Mountain means Iran can resume 90% enrichment within months — and makes Phase III strikes politically necessary for the Trump administration's credibility.

What Military Victory Actually Looks Like for This Scenario

For Scenario 2 to stabilize at 28% rather than cascading into Sc3 or Sc6, three things must be simultaneously true: (1) Nuclear mission credibly complete — Battle Damage Assessment confirms Fordow and Pickaxe Mountain are destroyed or rendered inoperable for 3–5 years; (2) Iranian retaliation is absorbed — US/Israeli missile defenses hold, carrier groups sustain no catastrophic losses, and Hormuz does not close for more than 14 days; (3) Political endgame is named — Trump or the State Department articulates what Iran must do to end the campaign (unconditional surrender is not a plan; a specific set of verifiable demands with a diplomatic off-ramp is). Without all three, this scenario tips: nuclear failure → Phase III strikes → Sc3 cascade; or catastrophic retaliation → domestic political crisis → Trump overreach into Sc6 conditions.

Global Economic Consequences: Days, Not Weeks

Goldman Sachs estimates Hormuz closure pushes WTI above $100/barrel within days. The cascade: Day 1–3, traders pause (already happening); Day 3–7, SPR releases announced; Day 7–14, Asian buyers begin panic-buying alternate sources; Day 14–30, European gas markets spike as Qatar LNG becomes unavailable; Day 30+, global recession probability exceeds 60% per Goldman's model. Every $10 increase costs the US economy ~$70B annually. The US economy cannot sustain a 90-day Hormuz closure without entering recession — which is precisely why this scenario resolves quickly, one way or another.

Scenario 6

IRGC Junta — Succession Council Consolidates Power

Likelihood: 14% — Rising after Khamenei confirmed dead

This public move raises the near-term likelihood of Sc6 if security organs rapidly impose order under a junta-style framework. — Source: AP, Mar 3

🔴 Who Would Actually Run the Junta

Under the Islamic Republic's Article 131, when the Supreme Leader dies, a Leadership Council of Three — the First VP, the parliament speaker, and the judiciary chief — manages the country for 50 days while the Assembly of Experts selects a permanent successor. In theory, this means Mohammad Mokhber (First VP, a technocrat) assumes executive power. In practice, four figures hold the actual levers:

  • Mojtaba Khamenei (54, Supreme Leader's second son) — controls IRGC intelligence directorate and Basij ideological corps. Not elected to any position, but has spent years building a parallel power network. The most likely shadow ruler of a junta scenario. Reports from Iran watchers (including Karim Sadjadpour at Carnegie) suggest he was systematically positioned to inherit his father's mantle. His legitimacy problem: he is not a senior cleric, cannot claim velayat-e faqih authority, and would be transparently a dynastic power grab — exactly what the 1979 revolution was supposedly against.
  • Hossein Salami (IRGC Commander-in-Chief) — hardline ideologue, commands the military apparatus. Would be the junta's enforcer, not its public face. Lacks clerical legitimacy to serve as Supreme Leader.
  • Esmail Qaani (Quds Force Chief, Soleimani's replacement) — focused on preserving external proxy networks; his priority is keeping Houthis and Iraqi PMF funded to maintain Iran's external leverage even if the home front collapses.
  • Ahmad Jannati (Guardian Council chief, 98 years old) — provides the clerical rubber-stamp for constitutional legitimacy. The Assembly of Experts he chairs would be responsible for naming a new Supreme Leader — and could be managed to produce a Mojtaba-aligned candidate.

The most likely junta structure: Mokhber as constitutional president (figurehead); Mojtaba as shadow Supreme Leader with clerical cover from a compliant Assembly of Experts; Salami commanding military operations; Qaani managing external leverage. — Sources: Reuters, NBC News, Carnegie Endowment, Mar 1–2

Following confirmation that Supreme Leader Khamenei is dead, a security-led succession emerges in Tehran. The Assembly of Experts names an interim leadership structure while IRGC commanders and security organs effectively run the state, imposing emergency law to crush protests and continue confrontation under new leadership. This scenario is Sc3's dark mirror: the same conditions that could produce collapse could instead produce consolidation, if the IRGC acts faster than the protest movement.

The Mojtaba Factor

  • Mojtaba Khamenei is the single most important figure in determining whether this scenario succeeds or collapses into Sc3. His assets: IRGC intelligence files on every senior commander, Basij mobilization capability, and years of cultivated loyalty networks. His liabilities: zero public legitimacy, no constitutional role, and every opposition faction — including reformists inside the system — sees him as exactly the corrupt dynastic succession the revolution was against.
  • If Mojtaba moves in the next 48 hours to control the succession process, he either consolidates power (Sc6) or triggers the factional civil war that makes collapse (Sc3) inevitable. There is no middle path for him.
  • Russia and China are the wild cards here. Both have strong incentives to back a junta that maintains continuity — chaos in Iran threatens their energy and trade relationships more than a compliant junta does. Expect them to quietly signal support for Mokhber/Mojtaba continuity within 24 hours.

Why the Junta Could Fail

  • IRGC fractional math — the IRGC has six independent corps (Aerospace, Ground Forces, Navy, Quds, Intelligence, Basij). They are not a unified command; they are competing fiefdoms. A junta requires all six to agree on who is in charge. In a shooting war with the US, they have 48–72 hours to agree before operational paralysis sets in.
  • Economic collapse accelerates under junta — any government that takes power inherits a rial at 1.7M/USD, oil exports disrupted, and a population that has just watched their leadership killed in US strikes. The junta cannot improve any of these metrics in the near term.
  • International rejection — a Mojtaba-backed junta will be sanctioned immediately and comprehensively. The EU, US, UK, Canada will not recognize it. Gulf states will not fund it. This accelerates the economic spiral.
  • Provincial commanders calculate their own survival — once it's clear a junta will be internationally isolated and economically collapsing, the provincial IRGC commanders who haven't yet defected will do their own calculation. The junta's window to consolidate before that math changes is very short.
If the Junta Consolidates: Consequences

A stable IRGC junta — if it achieves internal cohesion within 72 hours — would: impose emergency law and a communications blackout; execute or detain the most prominent protest leaders; refuse IAEA access to surviving nuclear sites (using them as leverage); activate remaining proxy networks (Iraqi PMF, Houthis) to signal continued deterrence; and seek recognition from Russia and China while buying time economically. The nuclear dimension is critical: a junta with surviving enriched uranium and a reason to accelerate weaponization (existential threat from US/Israel) is the most dangerous near-term proliferation scenario in the world. This is why the US military component of Operation Epic Fury must be accompanied by a political plan — military success without a political endgame produces a junta, not a democratic transition.

Scenario 4

Pahlavi Returns — Democratic Transition

Likelihood: 16% — Rising as regime fractures under strike pressure
NEW (Mar 1) — The "Prosperity Project": What It Actually Proposes

Reza Pahlavi's "Prosperity Project", unveiled March 1 as strikes continued, is a three-phase governance plan — not a manifesto:

  • Phase 1 — Emergency (Days 1–30): Provisional Government of National Unity declared; release of all political prisoners immediately; UN Security Council resolution recognizing the transitional authority; IRGC officers who stand down receive amnesty and pension protection; diaspora professionals invited back to fill civil service vacancies
  • Phase 2 — Reconstruction (Days 30–100): Unlock $100B+ in frozen assets via US/EU sanctions relief; IMF/World Bank emergency reconstruction facility; Gulf state co-investment in energy and infrastructure; "Contract with Iran" — constitutionally binding guarantees for minority rights, women's rights, religious freedom, and press freedom; IRGC economic empire placed under civilian oversight, not dissolved overnight
  • Phase 3 — Democracy (Post-100 days): Free elections with international monitoring within 18 months; constitutional convention with representatives from all 31 provinces, ethnic groups, and diaspora; military placed under civilian command; secular democratic republic — explicitly not a monarchy

The key differentiator from 1979: Pahlavi explicitly frames this as "One Iran" — not federalization — while offering the maximum autonomy guarantees for Kurdish, Baloch, Arab, and Azeri communities within a unified state. This is designed to close the one fracture point that destroyed every previous opposition coalition. — Sources: Times of India, JPost, Washington Post, Mar 1–2

The US–Israel strike campaign fatally weakens the Islamic Republic's military and economic foundations. The IRGC fractures — senior commanders, heeding Pahlavi's "wide tent" offer and the CIA's recruitment campaign, choose the people over the regime. Reza Pahlavi returns to Iran for the first time since 1979 to lead a 100-day transitional process toward the first free elections in the Islamic Republic's history. This scenario requires Sc3 (collapse) to not descend into chaos — it is specifically the ordered version of regime change, where the IRGC's stand-down is coordinated rather than anarchic.

What Would Trigger This

  • IRGC command fractures in coordination — not chaotic defection, but 3–5 senior generals collectively announcing support for a transitional authority, removing the coordination barrier for others
  • Khamenei death — CONFIRMED (Mar 1) — succession vacuum active; no figure with equivalent authority to order the IRGC to resist
  • Trump administration recognition — Witkoff back-channel becomes official; White House endorses Pahlavi's transitional council, giving it international legitimacy before it has domestic control
  • Gulf state financial support — Saudi Arabia and UAE pledge reconstruction financing; removes the economic collapse fear that keeps IRGC loyalists on the fence
  • Protests provide cover — a mass uprising in Tehran gives commanders political justification to say they were "responding to the people's will," not surrendering to foreign pressure

Critical Obstacles — and Why They Matter Now

  • Mojtaba Khamenei's network — even if the formal IRGC stands down, the Supreme Leader's son controls IRGC intelligence and Basij ideological units. He doesn't need legal authority to spoil a transition if he retains armed loyalists in Tehran.
  • Kurdish–Pahlavi fracture (Feb 27) — friction between Pahlavi's circle and Kurdish dissident groups is public. Kurdish armed groups (PJAK) in the northwest may not honor an amnesty they weren't part of negotiating, creating a low-grade insurgency in the transition's first weeks.
  • Nuclear material as leverage — whatever IRGC faction controls the remaining enriched uranium (1,000+ kg, location uncertain post-strikes) holds the most valuable bargaining chip in history. They will not give it up without specific guarantees Pahlavi may not be positioned to offer unilaterally.
  • No party, no bureaucracy, no network — Pahlavi has lived in exile since age 17. A transitional government without a functioning civil service will rely on the same technocrats who served the Islamic Republic, creating legitimacy problems from Day 1.
Historical Parallel — and Why February–March 2026 Is Different

Every previous Iranian opposition effort since 1979 failed against the same wall: IRGC loyalty to the regime. Five things have simultaneously changed in February–March 2026 that have never been simultaneously true before: (1) the IRGC's economic base is being destroyed, removing the financial incentive for loyalty; (2) the protest movement has named a single leader for the first time in 46 years, giving fence-sitting commanders a face to surrender to; (3) the CIA has opened a verified, operational defection channel — not rhetoric but a live Tor portal; (4) the Trump administration has signaled — via the Witkoff back-channel — that it will recognize a transitional government; (5) Khamenei's death removes the authority figure who could have ordered the IRGC to resist regardless of material incentives. The question is not whether these conditions exist — they do. The question is whether Pahlavi and the opposition can convert conditions into a coordinated institutional move before the Mojtaba faction or the junta faction fills the vacuum first.

What a Free Iran Looks Like: The 10-Year Trajectory

A democratic Iran — even an imperfect one — fundamentally reshapes the Middle East within a decade. Year 1: Hormuz reopens under UN maritime governance; proxy network (Hezbollah, Houthis, PMF) loses state funding and collapses organically within 18 months. Year 2–3: $100B+ in frozen assets plus IMF/World Bank reconstruction financing create the fastest-growing economy in the Middle East. Years 3–5: Iranian diaspora — 5 million people, heavily educated, concentrated in the US and Europe — returns capital and talent; Iran joins the WTO and begins normalized trade with Europe. Years 5–10: A secular Iran with functioning democratic institutions seeks normalized relations with Israel, ends the nuclear program voluntarily under IAEA monitoring, and applies for EU association agreement. The proxy wars that have cost the region $500B+ since 2003 end. This is the scenario that every US administration since Carter has pursued and none has achieved. Operation Epic Fury, for the first time, makes it structurally available.

Scenario 1

Diplomatic Deal — JCPOA 3.0

Likelihood: 0% — Eliminated by strikes

Iran's pre-strike Oman-mediated talks had produced real progress — including conditional acceptance of zero uranium stockpiling. The Feb 28 strikes and Khamenei's death have eliminated the diplomatic track entirely for the foreseeable future. No Iranian government — whether Mokhber's caretaker administration, a junta, or a protest-aligned transitional council — can negotiate enrichment limits under active bombardment without political collapse. The domestic framing would be indistinguishable from unconditional surrender.

What Would Have Made a Deal Possible (and No Longer Exists)

A deal required three simultaneous conditions: (1) a Khamenei with enough political authority to absorb the domestic cost of concessions — he is now dead; (2) a Trump willing to accept face-saving language that let Iran claim partial victory — he has now committed to military force; (3) IRGC hardliners overruled — they are now running the state. A post-conflict arrangement that resembles a deal could theoretically emerge in 6–18 months — but it would be terms dictated to a defeated adversary, which is a fundamentally different political and legal instrument. This scenario is archived, not merely paused.

Scenario 5

Prolonged Standoff — Ceasefire & Attrition

Likelihood: 0% — Eliminated by the pace of events

A frozen standoff required that strikes produce limited damage and neither side achieves a decisive outcome — allowing international pressure to mount for a ceasefire and a "frozen" exchange of asymmetric retaliation. Three events on March 1 eliminated this path simultaneously: Khamenei confirmed dead (no authority to authorize a ceasefire); Iran fired 170 ballistic missiles (neither side can now claim limited engagement); and major traders suspended Hormuz shipments (the economic clock is already running, forcing resolution within weeks not months).

Why the Economics Foreclosed This Scenario

A standoff requires both sides to absorb the cost of indefinite confrontation. Iran's foreign exchange reserves are under $10B accessible (OFAC/CRS estimates) — at the current burn rate with oil exports disrupted, this is less than 60 days of government operations. Trump's domestic political credibility requires visible "winning" within weeks, not months. And the global economy cannot absorb a 90-day Hormuz closure without recession — creating pressure from Europe, China, India, and Japan on all parties to resolve. The economics of a standoff were always fatal to it. March 1 confirmed the timeline: this resolves in weeks, not months.
