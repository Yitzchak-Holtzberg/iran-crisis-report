Nuclear Negotiations

Vienna technical talks slated Mar 2 — US‑Israeli strikes and Iran's confirmation of Khamenei's death threaten the session.

The Diplomatic Track

FEB 6 — Muscat, Oman
Round 1: Indirect talks mediated by Oman. Iran's FM Abbas Araghchi and US envoys Steve Witkoff & Jared Kushner establish initial contact.
FEB 17-18 — Geneva
Round 2: Both sides report "good progress" and agree on "guiding principles." Iran's FM Araghchi: "I believe we made good progress... we will not reach it quickly." Iranian delegation promised to return with detailed proposals within two weeks. JD Vance mixed review: "In some ways it went well. They agreed to meet afterwards. But the president has set some red lines the Iranians are not yet willing to acknowledge." Strait of Hormuz partially closed for IRGC live-fire drills while talks were underway.
FEB 17 — Tehran
Khamenei rejects terms: During IRGC drills in the Strait of Hormuz, warns the US Navy "could be sunk." Separately: "The US President says their army is the world's strongest, but the strongest army in the world can sometimes be slapped so hard it cannot get up."
FEB 20 — Washington
Trump: Strikes could begin "within 10 days" if no deal. Orders largest military surge since 2003. Iran sends letter to UN: "All bases, facilities and assets of the hostile force in the region would constitute legitimate targets." US military planning reportedly includes targeting individuals and possibly regime change (Reuters). Senior admin official: all forces in place by mid-March.
FEB 25 — State of the Union
Trump: "I will never allow the world's number one sponsor of terror to have a nuclear weapon." Claims 32,000 killed in protests. Iran's FM fires back: Trump told "big lies" — but says deal is "within reach."
FEB 27 (FRI) — DELEGATIONS DEPART FOR VIENNA; IAEA CONFIRMS MARCH 2 SESSION
Day-after: Vienna-bound. US and Iranian technical delegations departed Geneva Friday, traveling directly to Vienna for expert-level working groups at the IAEA headquarters beginning Monday, March 2. IAEA DG Grossi confirmed hosting and that enrichment verification and sanctions sequencing are primary agenda items. Trump: "Progress has been made — but the deadline remains." IRGC hardliners issued a statement calling Round 3 outcome "insufficient" — FM Araghchi publicly defended the diplomatic track. The diplomatic clock is now running toward Monday's opening session. — Sources: Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera, Feb 27
FEB 26 (THU) — GENEVA — ROUND 3 CONCLUDES; "GOOD PROGRESS"; VIENNA TECHNICAL TALKS MONDAY
Geneva Round 3 concluded Thursday with Iran FM Araghchi reporting "good progress" — the strongest post-session language of any round — and announcing that technical-level talks at the IAEA headquarters in Vienna will begin Monday, March 2. Oman FM Badr al-Busaidi confirmed: "US and Iran will continue to hold nuclear talks next week." The sides are described as "closer to agreement than in previous rounds." Iran brought "practical" draft proposals on phased sanctions relief in exchange for verifiable nuclear restrictions. The US insists ballistic missiles must eventually be addressed; Iran refuses any scope expansion. The agreement to advance to expert-level talks in Vienna — rather than a third politician-level round — is a significant procedural step toward a framework. Trump's early-March deadline remains in force. — Sources: Bloomberg, France24, Al Jazeera, Feb 26
FEB 27 — Muscat / Vienna
Oman hails 'breakthrough': Oman's foreign minister says Iran has agreed to zero uranium stockpiling and to convert existing enriched material into fuel as part of technical-level progress; mediator called the development a significant step while US officials expressed cautious optimism pending details. — Sources: Al Jazeera, Le Monde, Feb 27-28
FEB 28 — Iran / US / Israel
Major strike: The United States and Israel launched major military strikes against targets inside Iran; Iran responded with missile attacks and warnings of wider retaliation, dramatically escalating tensions and imperiling the diplomatic track to Vienna. Nearby states reported explosions and heightened alert as the situation risked derailing planned technical-level sessions. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, New York Times, Feb 28
MAR 1 — Aftermath
Escalation: U.S. and Israeli strikes continued; conflicting reports emerged about the health and fate of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei — Israeli officials publicly claimed he had been killed, a claim unverified as communications and reporting were disrupted. The strikes intensified regional alarm and further jeopardized the planned Vienna technical sessions. — Sources: Reuters, CNN, Al Jazeera, Mar 1

Confirmation & escalation: Iranian state media and officials confirmed the death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei following U.S.-Israeli strikes; attacks continued into a second day. The confirmation and ongoing strikes have effectively derailed the Vienna technical sessions scheduled for March 2 and sharply raised regional tensions. — Sources: Reuters, BBC, Mar 2

What a Deal Would Actually Require

The gap between US demands and Iranian red lines is significant — but not unbridgeable in theory. What the two sides are actually negotiating:

US Demands (Trump's Red Lines)

  • Zero enrichment — or at most 3.67% (JCPOA 1.0 level), far below weapons-grade
  • Dismantlement of Fordow, Natanz advanced centrifuge cascades, and the "Pickaxe Mountain" facility
  • Intrusive IAEA inspections — anywhere, anytime access, including military sites like Parchin
  • End to ballistic missile development beyond defensive range
  • Cessation of proxy funding — Hezbollah, Houthis, PMF
  • Trump stated in SOTU: "I will never allow the world's number one sponsor of terror to have a nuclear weapon" — implying regime behavior change, not just nuclear limits

Iran's Red Lines (Non-Negotiable)

  • Right to enrichment — Khamenei calls this an "unalienable right"; Araghchi: "Iran will under no circumstances forgo its right to peaceful nuclear technology"
  • No military site inspections — Parchin is a military facility; IAEA access would be unprecedented
  • Sanctions lifted upfront, not in phases — Iran was burned by JCPOA 1.0 when the US withdrew in 2018 after Iran complied
  • No interference in regional policy — proxy support framed as "resistance" ideology, not negotiable
  • Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf: "If you choose the path of diplomacy — one in which Iranian dignity and mutual interests are respected — we will remain at the table."
The Potential Bridging Formula

Diplomatic sources suggest the possible middle ground: Iran caps enrichment at 20% (not zero, but far below 90% weapons-grade), accepts IAEA cameras at all declared sites (but not military), ships its 60-90% enriched uranium stockpile to a third country (Russia or Turkey), and agrees to a 10-year moratorium on advanced centrifuge deployment. In exchange: partial sanctions relief, unfreezing of $6B in South Korean oil payments, and a US commitment not to seek regime change. Whether Khamenei — and Trump — can sell this to their domestic audiences is the central question.

The UK-US Rift — & UK Evacuates Tehran

PM Starmer blocked the US from using RAF Fairford (B-52/B-2 bombers) and Diego Garcia. Legal basis: participation would breach international law. In retaliation, Trump withdrew support for the UK-Mauritius Chagos Islands deal.

However, Britain quietly deployed Typhoons and F-35Bs to Qatar and Cyprus — defensive assets in case of Iranian retaliation. UK officials privately express concern that a US strike without British legal cover sets a dangerous precedent for unilateral military action.

NEW (Feb 27): The UK government withdrew its ambassador and all embassy staff from Tehran, citing "ongoing security concerns." The Foreign Office issued emergency guidance: British nationals in Iran should leave immediately; consular support is now "extremely limited." The UK joins China, Australia, India, South Korea, and Sweden in upgrading Iran to a "do not travel" zone — a coordinated multi-nation departure widely interpreted as intelligence-based anticipation of imminent military action. — Sources: The Independent, Sky News, Politico.eu, Feb 27

The practical consequence of the base denial: without Diego Garcia, B-2 Spirit stealth bombers carrying GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrators — the only weapons that can potentially reach the deepest Iranian bunkers — would require either a 34-hour round-trip from Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri, or a diplomatic reversal from Starmer. Senior US military planners have quietly noted the strike package is "less effective" without the Indian Ocean base.

Israel: Finger on the Trigger

IDF at highest readiness since the June 2025 twelve-day war. 280,000 reservists authorized. IDF Chief: "prepared to employ unprecedented offensive power." Coordinating with CENTCOM; requesting advance warning before any US strikes. Jan 5 Security Cabinet strike authorization remains active.

Israel's position is uniquely urgent: Iran's ballistic missile arsenal — even degraded from the June 2025 strikes — can still reach Tel Aviv in under 12 minutes. The "Pickaxe Mountain" facility, if it produces a nuclear device, changes Israel's strategic calculus permanently. Prime Minister Netanyahu has reportedly told the Security Cabinet: "We cannot allow a second chance."

Israel's independent strike option: the IAF's F-35I "Adir" fleet has demonstrated the range to reach Iran via Saudi airspace (with or without Riyadh's permission). The June 2025 strikes proved Israeli aircraft can penetrate Iranian air defenses. But without American GBU-57 bunker-busters, Israel cannot guarantee destroying the deepest facilities — making US participation a strategic necessity, not just a preference.
