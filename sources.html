Source Reliability Guide

How we evaluate and weight sources — understanding editorial bias and credibility tiers
Read First

Not all sources carry equal weight. In an active conflict with high information fog, it matters whether a claim comes from an official press release, a wire-service dispatch, a specialized think tank, a regionally partisan outlet, or an advocacy group. This guide categorizes every source type used in this report so readers can calibrate how much confidence to place in each claim. Use tier-1 and tier-2 sources to establish facts; use tiers 3–5 for context and analysis; treat tier-6 sources as one perspective among many.

TIER 1 — HIGHEST WEIGHT

Official Primary Sources

Government and international-body statements are the authoritative record for confirmed military actions, casualty acknowledgments, and policy positions. Treat as ground truth for what governments claim — not necessarily objective reality.

  • US CENTCOM — official DoD press releases and briefings
  • IAEA — nuclear safeguards and inspections
  • State Dept / DoD — US policy statements
  • UN / OCHA / WHO — humanitarian and casualty assessments

⚠ Official sources reflect the issuing government's narrative — always note which government is speaking.

TIER 2 — HIGH WEIGHT

Major Wire Services

Wire services have editors, fact-checkers, and legal accountability. Breaking-news dispatches are less verified than analyzed pieces — but wire reports are still among the most reliable for confirming discrete events.

  • Reuters — global wire; strong Middle East bureau
  • AP (Associated Press) — broad international verification
  • AFP — strong on European and MENA coverage
TIER 3 — GOOD WEIGHT

Specialized Military & Defense Analysis

These outlets have deep domain expertise and are generally the most reliable for military order-of-battle, technical claims, and operational assessments. Preferred for hardware, deployments, and strike analysis.

  • ISW (Institute for the Study of War) — rigorous daily updates; some US-aligned framing
  • USNI News — authoritative on US naval movements
  • The War Zone (TWZ) — excellent OSINT and technical analysis
  • CSIS — strong strategic analysis; generally centrist Western perspective
  • Defense News / Stars & Stripes — credible DoD-adjacent reporting
TIER 4 — STANDARD WEIGHT

Quality Journalism (Mainstream US/UK)

Major broadcast and print outlets with editorial standards and international correspondents. Good for confirmed events and context; analysis pieces may reflect editorial positions.

  • NYT, WaPo, BBC, The Guardian, FT, The Economist
  • CNN, NBC, CBS, CNBC, ABC
  • PBS, NPR, Axios, Politico
  • The Independent, Sky News, The Telegraph
TIER 5 — REGIONAL / VERIFY FRAMING

Regional & Foreign Media — Note Editorial Angle

These outlets often have the best on-the-ground access in their regions but carry editorial perspectives tied to their ownership or location. Valuable for regional reactions and events; cross-check framing with wire services.

  • Al Jazeera — Qatar-government funded; credible journalism but watch framing on Gulf state politics and Israel-Palestine
  • Iran International — London-based; strongly anti-Iranian government; valuable for internal Iran reporting, but framing leans toward regime collapse narratives
  • Al Arabiya — Saudi-owned; check for Saudi government perspective
  • Times of Israel — Israel-based; credible but check Israeli government framing
  • SCMP — Hong Kong; credible but note Beijing editorial constraints
  • HRANA — Iran human-rights NGO; valuable casualty data but opposition-aligned; figures should be treated as estimates pending corroboration
TIER 6 — ADVOCACY / USE WITH CAUTION

Advocacy Think Tanks & Partisan Sources

These organizations publish detailed analysis but have explicitly stated policy missions that shape their framing. Useful for perspectives and detailed breakdowns — not neutral arbiters. Weight their conclusions accordingly.

  • JINSA (Jewish Institute for National Security Affairs) — pro-Israel advocacy; Israel strike analysis leans toward justifying Israeli military action
  • MEF (Middle East Forum) — conservative, strongly anti-Iran and pro-Israel; favors regime change framing
  • Alma Research Center — Israeli security think tank; pro-Israel institutional perspective on Iranian threats
  • National Interest — realist/conservative; anti-interventionist but sometimes ideologically driven analysis
  • CGTN — Chinese state media; pro-Beijing, may amplify anti-US or pro-Iran narratives
  • Wikipedia — user-edited; rapidly updated during crises but unverified; use as a starting point only, not a primary source
⚠ Source Weighting Note: This report uses all six tiers to build a comprehensive picture. Official denial of a claim (Tier 1) outweighs an advocacy source's assertion (Tier 6). A wire-service confirmation (Tier 2) outweighs a regional outlet's single-source claim (Tier 5). Where key statistics or confirmed events rely primarily on Tier 5–6 sources, they are flagged in the Fog of War section. Casualty figures, BDA claims, and strike tallies from any single source should be treated as preliminary until corroborated across tiers.
